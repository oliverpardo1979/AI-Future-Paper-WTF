\subsection{A lower research-expenditure calibration}
\label{subsec:rewrite-rsi-research-share}

I now use research expenditure rather than the decline in the AI-service
price to guide the choice of $\chi$. The lower reference is the United
States in 2023: \citet{korinekmckelvey2026} estimate training compute
expenditure of \$18.46 billion, equivalent to $0.0664\%$ of nominal
GDP.\footnote{Training compute is from Table 3 of
\citet{korinekmckelvey2026}. Nominal GDP of \$27,811.517 billion is the
2023 value of the BEA series \texttt{GDPA}, available through
\href{https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/GDPA}{FRED}, accessed September 14, 2026.}
This is the lowest of the 2023--2025 estimates considered, not a lower
confidence bound or an estimate of current research spending. The compute
estimate uses rental-equivalent costs and assigns half of AI compute
spending to training and research. It proxies $M$ but does not measure
autonomous RSI directly.

This exercise retains $\sigma=1$ and the fixed-efficiency pre-RSI BGP
of Section~\ref{subsec:rewrite-rsi-activation}. AI is already used in
production, $B_0=0.01\overline B$, and initial capital satisfies
$K_0/Y_0=3.30$. RSI becomes available unexpectedly at date zero, without
changing the initial stocks, production quantities or prices. Consumption
and research adjust endogenously. All parameters except $\chi$ remain
unchanged; Table~\ref{tab:rewrite-research-share-parameters} lists them.

I compare annual expenditure with annual output:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\int_0^1 M_t\,\dd t}{\int_0^1 Y_t\,\dd t}
 \simeq \frac{18.46}{27{,}811.517}=0.00066375.
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-share-target}
\end{equation}
Among the previously explored candidates on the increasing low-$\chi$
branch, $\chi=1.4378$ gives the closest annual ratio, $0.06374\%$.
It falls short of the empirical proxy by $0.26$ basis points, or about
$4.0\%$ of the target. I retain this approximate match rather than refine
$\chi$ to fit an uncertain proxy more closely. This choice does not relax
the equilibrium checks in Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-algorithm}.

The initial effects are modest. Output per person and the real wage
begin growing at an instantaneous annual rate of $1.225\%$, compared
with $1\%$ before RSI activation. In the displayed trajectory, their
growth peaks near $1.30\%$, and the interest rate peaks near $5.28\%$.
The AI-service price falls by $9.0\%$ over 27 months without being targeted.
Annual research expenditure is $0.06204\%$ of output in the second year:
the calibration still does not reproduce the recent increase in research
expenditure relative to GDP.

Figures~\ref{fig:rewrite-research-share-accumulation}--\ref{fig:rewrite-research-share-distribution}
retain the same panels and time windows. The long-run limits are unchanged:
output per person and real wages grow at $\gamma=1\%$, and the interest
rate approaches $\rho+\gamma=5\%$. The displayed horizon does not exhaust
the transition: at year 500, $B/\overline B$ is about $0.46$.
At unit elasticity, labor's income share remains $60.3\%$ and AI revenue
remains $6.7\%$ of output. The initial size of the AI industry is not fitted.
