\subsection{Derivation of Remark \ref{rem:rewrite-ai-labor}}

The CES share in \eqref{eq:rewrite-sx} implies
\[
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln(1-s_X)}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{A,L}
 =-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)s_X.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:rewrite-output-x-elasticity} follows by differentiating
\eqref{eq:rewrite-output} and \eqref{eq:rewrite-ces}. Combining these two
elasticities with the wage equation in \eqref{eq:rewrite-factor-prices} gives
\eqref{eq:rewrite-wage-x-elasticity}. Combining the share derivative with
\eqref{eq:rewrite-labor-share} gives \eqref{eq:rewrite-share-x-elasticity}.
Finally,
\[
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln w}{\partial\ln L}\right|_{K,A,X}
 =-\left[\alpha(1-s_X)+\frac{s_X}{\sigma}\right]<0.
\]
Implicit differentiation of the labor-demand condition at a fixed wage gives
\eqref{eq:rewrite-labor-demand-x-elasticity}. Its denominator is positive, so
the sign statements follow from the two thresholds \(1\) and \(1/\alpha\).

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-cap-invariance}}

Let \(D=\overline B-B\). The finite-frontier state equation gives
\[
 \frac{\dot D}{D}=-\frac{\chi}{\overline B}(BM)^\eta.
\]
Integration gives \eqref{eq:rewrite-cap-gap}. Local integrability makes the
exponent finite at every finite date, so \(D_t>0\).
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-choice}}

Fix \(K,A,L,B>0\), let \(R(X)=p_X(X;K,A,L)X\), and write
\[
 u=\frac{1}{\sigma},\qquad s=s_X,\qquad
 e=u(1-s)+\alpha s.
\]
The demand elasticity in \eqref{eq:rewrite-inverse-demand-elasticity} and the
CES-share derivative imply
\[
 R'(X)=p_X(1-e),\qquad
 \frac{\dd s}{\dd\ln X}=(1-u)s(1-s).
\]
Let \(m(X)=R'(X)\). Wherever marginal revenue is positive, direct
differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{Xm'(X)}{p_X}
 =e(1-e)+(\alpha-u)(1-u)s(1-s)\equiv D(s).
 \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature}
\end{equation}
If \((\alpha-u)(1-u)\geq0\), then \(D(s)>0\) immediately. If this product is
negative, the parameter restrictions imply \(0<\alpha<u<1\), and
\[
 D(s)=u(1-u)(1-s)^2
 +[u^2+2\alpha-3\alpha u]s(1-s)
 +\alpha(1-\alpha)s^2.
\]
The middle coefficient is positive. For \(u\leq2/3\), this follows from
\(u^2+\alpha(2-3u)>0\). For \(u>2/3\), use \(\alpha<u\) and \(2-3u<0\) to
obtain
\[
 u^2+\alpha(2-3u)>u^2+u(2-3u)=2u(1-u)>0.
\]
Thus marginal revenue decreases strictly whenever it is positive.

As \(X\downarrow0\), revenue is proportional to \(X^{1-u}\) when
\(\sigma>1\), to \(X^\beta\) when \(\sigma=1\), and to
\(X^{1-\alpha}\) when \(\sigma<1\). Every exponent lies between zero and
one, so \(m(X)\to\infty\). As \(X\to\infty\), marginal revenue converges to
zero from above if \(\sigma\geq1\); if \(\sigma<1\), it eventually
becomes negative. Hence \(m(X)=1/B\) has exactly one solution. Operating profit
increases before this solution and decreases after it. The linear cost \(X/B\)
dominates revenue as \(X\to\infty\), so this solution is the unique global
maximum. Equation \eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-proof-curvature} also proves the
strict second-order condition.
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-research-interior}}

Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-monopoly-choice} gives \(X^*>0\), and the
envelope condition \eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope} gives
\(\pi_B=X^*/B^2>0\). A marginal increase in capability therefore raises
current optimized profit and weakly raises future capability under a fixed
research policy. At a differentiable optimum its current-value shadow price is
strictly positive: \(q>0\). The part of the Hamiltonian that depends on \(M\)
is
\[
 -M+q\chi B^\eta M^\eta\psi(B).
\]
Its right derivative diverges as \(M\downarrow0\) because
\(q,B,\psi(B)>0\) and \(0<\eta<1\). Therefore \(M=0\) cannot
maximize the Hamiltonian at a finite date.
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-research-scale}}

I first consider the uncapped economy. Write
\[
 \theta=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha},\qquad
 S=\int_0^T M_t\dd t.
\]
For every admissible policy, \(B\) is nondecreasing. The capability law implies
\[
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}B_t^{1-\eta}
 =(1-\eta)\chi M_t^\eta.
\]
H\"older's inequality then gives, for every \(t\leq T\),
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\leq t\leq T}B_t
 \leq C_B\left(1+S^{\eta/(1-\eta)}\right),
 \label{eq:rewrite-capability-expenditure-bound}
\end{equation}
where \(C_B\) does not depend on the research policy. The same bound covers
arbitrarily concentrated controls because, on a support of length \(d\leq T\),
\(\int M_t^\eta\dd t\leq d^{1-\eta}S^\eta\).

Let \(\overline K=\max_{t\leq T}K_t\) and
\(\overline{\mathcal L}=\max_{t\leq T}A_tL_t\). The weighted power-mean
inequality gives \(Z\leq AL+X\). The final-good firm's demand condition therefore
implies
\[
 p_XX\leq(1-\alpha)\overline K^\alpha
 \left(\overline{\mathcal L}^{1-\alpha}+X^{1-\alpha}\right).
\]
Maximizing this upper bound net of \(X/B\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \pi(B;K,A,L)\leq C_\pi(1+B^\theta).
 \label{eq:rewrite-profit-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
On a finite horizon, integrability of \(r\) bounds the discount factor above
and away from zero. Combining
\eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-expenditure-bound} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-upper-bound} yields
\begin{equation}
 J_T(M)\leq C_0+C_1S^p-\underline D S,
 \qquad
 p=\frac{\eta(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(1-\eta)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-truncated-value-bound}
\end{equation}
If \(\eta<\alpha\), then \(p<1\). The bound tends to minus infinity as
\(S\to\infty\), so the value is finite and the objective is coercive.

For the converse, suppose \(\sigma>1\). As \(B\to\infty\), optimized
operating profit satisfies
\[
 \pi(B;K,A,L)\sim\kappa_XKB^\theta
\]
for a constant \(\kappa_X>0\), uniformly when \(K,A,\) and \(L\) remain in
compact subsets of the positive orthant. Fix \(\Delta\in(0,T)\), choose
\(M=\mathcal B\) on \([0,\Delta]\), and choose \(M=0\) afterward. Then
\[
 B_{\mathcal B}(t)^{1-\eta}=B_0^{1-\eta}
 +(1-\eta)\chi\mathcal B^\eta\min\{t,\Delta\}.
\]
Choosing \(X\) optimally at every date gives
\begin{equation}
 J_T(\mathcal B)=c_\pi\mathcal B^{
 \eta(1-\alpha)/[\alpha(1-\eta)]}
 -c_M\mathcal B
 +o\!\left(\mathcal B^{
 \eta(1-\alpha)/[\alpha(1-\eta)]}\right),
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-burst-payoff}
\end{equation}
with \(c_\pi,c_M>0\). The exponent exceeds one when \(\eta>\alpha\), so this
family makes the truncated value unbounded above. At equality, the exponent is
one and the sign of the leading coefficient determines this family's result.

For a finite frontier, Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-cap-invariance} confines
\(B\) to the compact interval \([B_0,\overline B]\). Optimized operating profit
is continuous in \(B\) and uniformly bounded on the fixed horizon. Hence
\(J_T(M)\leq C_0-\underline D S\), so every maximizing sequence has bounded
total expenditure. Set \(v=M^\eta\) and \(p=1/\eta>1\). The control cost is
\(v^p\), while the state equation is affine in \(v\):
\[
 \dot B=\chi B^\eta\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right)v.
\]
A maximizing sequence is bounded in the reflexive space \(L^p[0,T]\). A weakly
convergent subsequence and the associated uniformly convergent state paths
preserve feasibility. The operating-profit integral is continuous under
uniform state convergence, while \(\int v^p\) is weakly lower semicontinuous.
The objective is therefore weakly upper semicontinuous and attains its maximum.
\hfill\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:rewrite-developer-sufficiency}}

The transformation \eqref{eq:rewrite-research-depth} satisfies
\[
 B'(R_B)=\psi(B)>0,
 \qquad
 B''(R_B)=-\frac{\psi(B)}{\overline B}<0.
\]
Using \(\pi_B=X/B^2\) and \eqref{eq:rewrite-profit-curvature} gives
\eqref{eq:rewrite-capped-profit-curvature}. Condition
\eqref{eq:rewrite-capped-concavity-gate} therefore makes optimized operating
profit concave in \(R_B\) throughout the reachable state domain.

When \(2\eta\leq1\), the monomial \((BM)^\eta\) is jointly concave and
increasing in \((B,M)\). Because \(B(R_B)\) is increasing and concave,
\(\chi[B(R_B)M]^\eta\) is jointly concave in \((R_B,M)\). Research cost is
linear. The present-value Hamiltonian written in research depth is therefore
jointly concave, and the transformed first-order and costate conditions
maximize it globally.

The shadow value of research depth is
\(q_R=qB'(R_B)=q\psi(B)\). Moreover,
\[
 \psi(B)R_B
 =-(\overline B-B)\log\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right)\leq B.
\]
The developer's transversality condition therefore implies the terminal
condition required by the concavity argument in \(R_B\). For any admissible
alternative with the same initial stock, the standard finite-horizon
concavity inequality gives
\[
 J_T^*-\widetilde J_T
 \geq\mu_T(\widetilde R_{B,T}-R_{B,T}^*)
 \geq-\mu_TR_{B,T}^*,
\]
where \(\mu_T\) is the present-value shadow price of research depth. The final
term converges to zero. Taking the lower limit as \(T\to\infty\) establishes
global optimality.
\hfill\(\square\)
