\section{Model}
\label{sec:rewrite-model}

Time is continuous.  The economy contains a representative household, competitive final-good firms, and an integrated AI developer. Labor productivity $A$ grows exogenously. AI capability $B$ grows through autonomous AI research: the only current input used to improve $B$ is research compute. The model therefore describes an economy in which the replacement of human researchers at the technological frontier has already occurred.

\subsection{Population and labor productivity}
\label{subsec:rewrite-population}

Population grows at the exogenous rate $n\geq0$:
\begin{equation}
 \dot N=nN,
 \qquad N(0)=N_0>0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-population}
\end{equation}
Each person supplies one unit of homogeneous labor inelastically. The aggregate labor endowment is therefore $N$. Because autonomous research uses no human labor, all labor is employed in final production and equilibrium will impose $L=N$.

The variable $A$ measures labor-augmenting productivity. One unit of labor supplies $A$ efficiency units, so effective labor is $AL$. Labor productivity follows
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\gamma A,
 \qquad A(0)=A_0>0,
 \qquad \gamma\geq0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-labor-productivity}
\end{equation}
%For any positive variable $V$, I write $g_V\equiv\dot V/V$. Thus $g_A=\gamma$.

\subsection{Household}
\label{subsec:rewrite-household}

The representative household owns physical capital and the AI developer. Let \(\Pi\) denote the net distribution the household receives from the AI developer. The household takes the paths of the net interest rate \(r\), the wage \(w\) and \(\Pi\) as given. For a predetermined capital stock $K_0>0$, it chooses aggregate consumption $C$ to solve
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{C_t\}_{t\geq0}}
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}N_t\log(C_t/N_t)\dd t,
 \qquad \rho>n,
 \label{eq:rewrite-household-problem}
\end{equation}
subject to
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wN+\Pi-C.
 \label{eq:rewrite-household-budget}
\end{equation}
The variable $r$ is the net real interest rate: depreciation has already been deducted from the rental return to capital. The household's Euler condition is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+r-\rho.
 \label{eq:rewrite-euler}
\end{equation}
Its transversality condition is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-household-tvc}
\end{equation}
%These conditions use only objects in the household's problem. Production-side conditions determining $r,w,$ and \(\Pi\) are introduced below.

\subsection{Final-good production}
\label{subsec:rewrite-final-production}

Competitive firms produce the final good from physical capital $K$, effective labor $AL$, and AI production services $X$. Let $0<\alpha<1$ be the physical-capital elasticity, $\delta\geq0$ the depreciation rate, and $\sigma>0$ the elasticity of substitution between effective labor and AI production services. The CES weights satisfy
$\omega_L,\omega_X\in(0,1)$ and $\omega_L+\omega_X=1$. Production is
\begin{align}
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},
 \label{eq:rewrite-output}\\
 Z&=\left[\omega_L(AL)^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}
 +\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}\right]^{
 \sigma/(\sigma-1)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-ces}
\end{align}
%At the continuous unit-elasticity limit,
%$Z=(AL)^{\omega_L}X^{\omega_X}$.

Let \(p_X\) denote the price of one unit of AI production services. Taking the input prices \(r+\delta,w,\) and \(p_X\) as given, a final-good firm solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{K,L,X\geq0}
 \left\{K^\alpha Z(AL,X)^{1-\alpha}-(r+\delta)K-wL-p_XX\right\}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-final-firm-problem}
\end{equation}
Define $s_X$ as the elasticity of $Z$ with respect to $X$:
\begin{equation}
 s_X=\frac{\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}}
 {\omega_L(AL)^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}
 +\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-sx}
\end{equation}
The interior first-order conditions are
\begin{equation}
 r=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,
 \qquad
 w=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},
 \qquad
 p_X=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-factor-prices}
\end{equation}

Constant returns and these conditions imply
\begin{equation}
 Y=(r+\delta)K+wL+p_XX.
 \label{eq:rewrite-final-zero-profit}
\end{equation}

Solving the final-good firm's AI condition for its price yields the inverse
demand schedule $p_X(X)$. %This notation conditions on the current values of $K,A,$ and $L$; I display those arguments only when the time dependence matters.
The inverse absolute elasticity is
\begin{equation}
 e_X\equiv-
 \frac{\partial\ln p_X(X)}{\partial\ln X}
 =\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma}+\alpha s_X.
 \label{eq:rewrite-inverse-demand-elasticity}
\end{equation}
This demand schedule is an input to the developer's problem.

\subsubsection{AI services and production labor}
\label{subsubsec:rewrite-static-substitution}

The elasticity \(\sigma\) governs two different margins. The first is whether more AI services raise or lower labor's marginal product. The second is whether labor receives a larger or smaller fraction of output. These margins have different thresholds. Define production labor's share of output as
\begin{equation}
 \ell^Y\equiv\frac{wL}{Y}=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X).
 \label{eq:rewrite-labor-share}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{remark}[Effect of AI on employment, wages, and labor's income share;
\citealp{autorkausik2026}; \citealp{yango2026}]\label{rem:effect}
\label{rem:rewrite-ai-labor}
Consider an increase in $X$. To evaluate its effects on $w$ and $\ell^Y$, hold $K$ and $L$ fixed; to evaluate its effect on conditional labor demand $L$, hold $K$ and $w$ fixed. If $\sigma<1$, $w$, $\ell^Y$, and $L$ all rise with $X$. If $1<\sigma<1/\alpha$, $w$ and $L$ rise while $\ell^Y$ falls. If $\sigma>1/\alpha$, all three fall. %At $\sigma=1$, $w$ and $L$ rise while $\ell^Y$ is unchanged. At $\sigma=1/\alpha$, $\ell^Y$ falls while $w$ and $L$ are unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
The remark follows from the final-good firm's first-order conditions:
\begin{align}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln w}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,L}
 &=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}-\alpha\right)s_X,
 \label{eq:rewrite-wage-x-elasticity}\\
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln\ell^Y}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,L}
 &=-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)s_X.
 \label{eq:rewrite-share-x-elasticity}
\end{align}
%Equivalently, holding $K,A,$ and $w$ fixed, conditional labor demand obeys
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln L}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,w}
 =\frac{s_X(\sigma^{-1}-\alpha)}
 {\alpha(1-s_X)+s_X/\sigma}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-labor-demand-x-elasticity}
\end{equation}
%The denominator in the last expression is strictly positive. The signs therefore follow from the thresholds $1$ and $1/\alpha$.
\end{proof}

Note, however, that Remark \ref{rem:effect} is not a general equilibrium effect. Still, it hints at the elasticity of substitution as pivotal in defining the effect of expanding AI capabilities in the demand for labor, wages and labor's income share.


\subsection{The AI developer}
\label{subsec:rewrite-developer}

\subsubsection{AI production and research services}
\label{subsubsec:rewrite-ai-services}

The model distinguishes two uses of compute. The variables $U$ and $M$ are flows of computing resources allocated to inference and research, respectively. In a concrete application, their empirical counterparts would be compute expenditure per unit of time. %They are not measures of AI capability.
I choose to measure compute in units such that one unit of compute costs one unit of the final good.

The stock $B$ is a scalar index of compute-augmenting AI capability. It summarizes improvements that allow a given amount of compute to accomplish more. Thus $B$ is an effective-compute multiplier. This terminology follows the distinction between compute and AI capabilities in \citet{sastryetal2024}. Inference compute produces AI production services according to
\begin{equation}
 X=BU.
 \label{eq:rewrite-ai-service}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-service} distinguishes the raw computing input $U$ from the resulting flow $X$ of effective AI production services. Research compute analogously produces the flow $BM$ of effective AI research services. Thus $U$ and $M$ are rival resource inputs, while $BU$ and $BM$ are the corresponding effective service flows. This capability--compute distinction follows the accounting architecture in \citet{davidsonetal2026}.

\subsubsection{Autonomous capability accumulation}
\label{subsubsec:rewrite-capability}

Given initial capability \(B_0>0\) and a capability frontier
\(\overline B\in(B_0,\infty]\), AI capability evolves according to
\begin{equation}
 \dot B=\chi(BM)^\eta\psi(B),
 \qquad
 \psi(B)=
  1-\frac{B}{\overline B},
 \qquad \chi>0,\quad0<\eta<1.
 \label{eq:rewrite-capability-law}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:rewrite-capability-flow} summarizes the
capability-accumulation mechanism.

 \label{fig:rewrite-capability-flow}
\end{figure}
The parameter \(\chi\) shifts research productivity. The parameter \(\eta\) governs returns to current research scale. The same capability stock $B$ augments both inference and research compute. This common-productivity assumption is parsimonious but substantive. Because $BM$ enters the AI capability accumulation technology, a higher $B$ makes each unit of research compute $M$ more productive and creates recursive improvement. The term \(\psi(B)\) reduces the remaining room for improvement as capability approaches \(\overline B\). The uncapped specification is recovered at \(\overline B=\infty\).

The frontier \(\overline B\) allows to establish and compute an equilibrium for each finite frontier and then ask whether these equilibria have a meaningful limit as \(\overline B\to\infty\). Without the frontier, recursive improvement may instead produce a finite-time singularity, as discussed below. Capability may approach the frontier asymptotically but cannot cross it in finite time. %Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-cap-invariance} gives the derivation.

\subsubsection{Supply of production AI services}
\label{subsubsec:rewrite-market-structure}

Conditional on $B,K,A,$ and $L$, the AI developer can 
choose $X$ within each date. Define optimized operating profit before
research expenditure as
\begin{equation}
 \pi(B;K,A,L)=\max_{X\geq0}
 \left\{p_X(X)X-\frac{X}{B}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-service-choice}
\end{equation}
The first-order condition equates marginal revenue to the compute cost of one additional service unit:
\begin{equation}
 p_X(1-e_X)=\frac{1}{B}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise monopoly choice]
\label{prop:rewrite-monopoly-choice}
Suppose $K,A,L,B>0$, $0<\alpha<1$, $\sigma>0$, and
\(\omega_L,\omega_X\in(0,1)\). Problem \eqref{eq:rewrite-service-choice} has a
unique global maximizer $X^*>0$. It is the unique solution to
\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma^{-1})(1-\sigma^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)>0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-soc}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The monopoly price is therefore
\begin{equation}
 p_X=\frac{1/B}{1-e_X}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-markup}
\end{equation}
%At \(\sigma=1\), define
%\(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\). The solution is explicit:
%\begin{equation}
% X^*=\left[\beta^2BK^\alpha
% (AL)^{(1-\alpha)\omega_L}\right]^{1/(1-\beta)}.
% \label{eq:rewrite-monopoly-cd-solution}
%\end{equation}
The envelope theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 \pi_B=\frac{X}{B^2}=\frac{U}{B}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-profit-envelope}
\end{equation}
%The $B^2$ term does not represent quadratic returns to capability. It results from differentiating the inference cost $X/B$: 
At a fixed $X$, a marginal increase in AI capability saves $U/B$ units of the consumption good.

Capability is nonrival in use. Once $B$ has been developed, using it to
augment one unit of inference or research compute does not reduce its ability to augment other units. This does not make the production of AI services costless: $BU$ and $BM$ require the rival and costly compute inputs $U$ and $M$. Nonrivalry also does not rule out excludability. An owner can restrict access to model weights, software, or AI services.

To see the resulting appropriability problem, suppose that competitive
service providers had free access to $B$. Free entry would imply
\begin{equation*}
 p_X=\frac{1}{B},
 \qquad p_XX-\frac{X}{B}=0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-competitive-ai}
\end{equation*}
The service market would generate no operating rent. A developer that paid for $M$ would increase a common AI capability stock $B$ that competing providers could use without compensating it. The developer would bear the research cost but could not appropriate the resulting benefits. %The problem is therefor one of research incentives, not the physical availability of compute.
%I therefore assign exclusive ownership of $B$ to an integrated developer that conducts research and sells AI production services. 
Exclusive ownership allows the developer to capture part of the value created by a higher $B$ and makes the return to research appropriable, as in the standard endogenous-growth treatment of nonrival ideas \citep{romer1990}.

That being said, monopoly is an institutional assumption, not a technological implication of nonrivalry. Licensing, oligopoly, public funding, or open-source provision with research subsidies could also support investment in $B$. Each alternative would require an additional institution and would change prices, profits, and the distribution of rents. Recent work studies these alternatives in more detail \citep{korinekvipra2025,gans2024,demireretal2025}.

\subsubsection{The dynamic problem}
\label{subsubsec:rewrite-dynamic-developer}

The developer sells $X$, purchases the $U=X/B$ units of inference compute
needed to supply those services, uses $M$ for research, and owns the resulting capability stock. Taking the paths of $r,K,A,$ and $L$ as given, it solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{X_t,M_t\}_{t\geq0}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[p_X(X_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)X_t-\frac{X_t}{B_t}-M_t\right]\dd t,
 \label{eq:rewrite-developer-problem}
\end{equation}
subject to \eqref{eq:rewrite-capability-law} and $B(0)=B_0$. Its distributed
profit is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=p_XX-\frac{X}{B}-M=p_XX-U-M.
 \label{eq:rewrite-developer-profit}
\end{equation}
Profit can be negative at a date because the model imposes no separate
cash-flow constraint. A negative \(\Pi\) represents an equity injection from
the household that owns the developer.

%\subsubsection{Dynamic research choice}
%\label{subsubsec:rewrite-research-choice}

Let $q$ be the current-value shadow price of capability. An interior research choice satisfies
\begin{equation}
 q\chi\eta B(BM)^{\eta-1}\psi(B)=1,
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-foc}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 M=\left[q\chi\eta B^\eta\psi(B)
 \right]^{1/(1-\eta)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-demand}
\end{equation}
The current-value costate equation is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=r-\frac{X}{qB^2}-\eta\frac{\dot B}{B}
 -\dot B\frac{\psi'(B)}{\psi(B)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-costate}
\end{equation}
For a finite frontier,
\(\psi'/\psi=-1/(\overline B-B)\); for the
uncapped economy, the derivative is zero. Rearranging gives the return equation
\begin{equation}
 rq=\frac{X}{B^2}+q\eta\frac{\dot B}{B}
 +q\dot B\frac{\psi'(B)}{\psi(B)}+\dot q.
 \label{eq:rewrite-capability-return}
\end{equation}
The first term is the inference-compute saving from better capability. The
second is the gain in research productivity. With a finite frontier, the third
term is negative because a higher $B$ leaves less room for further
improvement. The final term is the capital gain on capability. Together these
returns must equal the market return $rq$.

The developer's transversality condition is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}q_tB_t=0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-developer-tvc}
\end{equation}
It rules out a path that leaves positive discounted value in unused capability.
It does not require $q$, $B$, or their undiscounted product to converge to
zero.

\begin{proposition}[Research is interior]
\label{prop:rewrite-research-interior}
Suppose $0<\eta<1$ and the developer has a differentiable, finite-valued
optimum with $B>0$ and positive demand for AI production services. Then
$q>0$ and $M>0$ at every finite date. Research may converge to zero
asymptotically, but the developer cannot stop capability accumulation by
choosing $M=0$ at a finite date.
\end{proposition}

The first-order and transversality conditions are necessary, but in a nonlinear
infinite-horizon problem they need not establish that the developer has a
finite value or that its candidate policy is globally optimal. The next two
restrictions address these distinct issues.

\begin{proposition}[Large-scale research expenditure]
\label{prop:rewrite-research-scale}
Fix a finite horizon, an initial capability stock, positive continuous paths
for $K,A,L$, and an integrable path for $r$. In the uncapped economy, if
$0<\eta<\alpha<1$, the developer's truncated value is finite and its
objective tends to minus infinity as total research expenditure diverges. If
\(\sigma>1\) and \(\eta>\alpha\), fixed-duration expansions of research
compute make the truncated value unbounded above. At \(\eta=\alpha\), the
leading benefit and cost terms have the same order, so their coefficients
determine the result.

For a fixed finite frontier, the truncated problem instead has a finite value
and attains its maximum for every $0<\eta<1$.
\end{proposition}

The uncapped restriction has a direct interpretation. If $S$ is total
research expenditure over a fixed horizon, the resulting profit benefit grows
no faster than
\begin{equation}
 S^{\eta(1-\alpha)/[\alpha(1-\eta)]},
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-benefit-order}
\end{equation}
whereas the cost of research is linear in $S$. The exponent is smaller than
one exactly when \(\eta<\alpha\). I therefore maintain
\begin{assumption}[Large-scale research curvature]
\label{ass:rewrite-research-curvature}
\begin{equation}
 0<\eta<\alpha<1.
 \label{eq:rewrite-eta-alpha}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
This assumption prevents arbitrarily large research expenditure from being
optimal on a fixed horizon. It does not by itself prove an infinite-horizon
equilibrium. The quantitative analysis uses $\eta=0.20<\alpha=0.33$; the
value of \(\eta\) is illustrative rather than estimated.

To state a sufficient condition for global optimality, define the
service--capability elasticity
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_B\equiv
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln X^*(B)}{\partial\ln B}\right|_{K,A,L}
 =\frac{1-e_X}{e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma^{-1})(1-\sigma^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-service-capability-elasticity}
\end{equation}
Because \(\pi_B=X^*/B^2\),
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{BB}=\frac{X^*}{B^3}(\varepsilon_B-2).
 \label{eq:rewrite-profit-curvature}
\end{equation}
For a finite frontier, define research depth
\begin{equation}
 R_B=-\overline B\log\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right).
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-depth}
\end{equation}
Then
\(B(R_B)=\overline B[1-e^{-R_B/\overline B}]\) and
\begin{equation}
 \dot R_B=\chi[B(R_B)M]^\eta.
 \label{eq:rewrite-research-depth-law}
\end{equation}
A direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial^2\pi(B(R_B))}{\partial R_B^2}
 =\frac{X\psi(B)}{B^3}
 \left[\psi(B)(\varepsilon_B-2)
 -\frac{B}{\overline B}\right].
 \label{eq:rewrite-capped-profit-curvature}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Developer sufficiency with a finite frontier]
\label{prop:rewrite-developer-sufficiency}
Consider a feasible finite-frontier developer policy with a finite objective.
Suppose it satisfies the pointwise monopoly choice, research first-order
condition, capability law, costate equation, and developer transversality
condition derived above. If \(2\eta\leq1\) and
\begin{equation}
 \psi(B)(\varepsilon_B-2)
 \leq\frac{B}{\overline B}
 \label{eq:rewrite-capped-concavity-gate}
\end{equation}
for every date and every capability in the reachable interval
\([B_0,\overline B)\), then the policy is a global solution to the developer's
problem.
\end{proposition}

Condition \eqref{eq:rewrite-capped-concavity-gate} is sufficient, not
necessary. It holds strictly near the frontier because its left-hand side
converges to zero while its right-hand side converges to one. It can fail at
lower capability levels, which is why the numerical equilibrium audit checks
it along the entire reachable path.

\subsection{Equilibrium}
\label{subsec:rewrite-equilibrium}

I can now combine the conditions of the three agents. Capital-market clearing
identifies household capital with the capital rented by final-good firms.
Labor-market clearing gives $L=N$. AI-service market clearing identifies the
developer's sales with the final sector's purchases. Combining the household
budget, final-good zero profit, developer profit, and market clearing yields
the aggregate resource constraint
\begin{equation}
 C+\dot K+\delta K+U+M=Y.
 \label{eq:rewrite-resource}
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Regular interior equilibrium]
\label{def:rewrite-equilibrium}
Fix the model's parameters, a frontier
\(\overline B\in(B_0,\infty]\), exogenous initial values $A_0,N_0>0$, and
predetermined stocks $K_0,B_0>0$. If the frontier is finite, require
$B_0<\overline B$. A regular interior equilibrium is an
infinite-horizon trajectory
\begin{equation}
 \left\{K_t,B_t,C_t,q_t,L_t,U_t,M_t,X_t,Z_t,Y_t,r_t,w_t,p_{X,t},
 s_{X,t},e_{X,t},\Pi_t\right\}_{t\geq0}
 \label{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-trajectory}
\end{equation}
with strictly positive quantities and prices \(w,p_X,q\), and with \(r\) and
\(\Pi\) allowed to have either sign. The paths \(K,B,C,\) and \(q\) are
absolutely continuous. The controls are measurable and nonnegative, the state
and flow constraints below hold, and the household's and developer's improper
objective integrals converge to finite real values. For every date, the
following conditions hold, with the differential equations understood almost
everywhere.

The exogenous paths and predetermined initial conditions are
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-exogenous}
\begin{align}
 A_t&=A_0e^{\gamma t}, & N_t&=N_0e^{nt},\\
 K(0)&=K_0, & B(0)&=B_0.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The intratemporal allocation and prices satisfy
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-static}
\begin{align}
 L&=N,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-labor}\\
 Z&=\left[\omega_L(AL)^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}
 +\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}\right]^{
 \sigma/(\sigma-1)},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-z}\\
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-y}\\
 s_X&=\frac{\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}}
 {\omega_L(AL)^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}
 +\omega_XX^{(\sigma-1)/\sigma}},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-sx}\\
 e_X&=\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma}+\alpha s_X,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-ex}\\
 r&=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-r}\\
 w&=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-w}\\
 p_X&=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-px}\\
 p_X(1-e_X)&=\frac{1}{B},
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-monopoly}\\
 X&=BU,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-service}\\
 q\chi\eta B(BM)^{\eta-1}\psi(B)&=1,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-research}\\
 \Pi&=p_XX-U-M.
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-profit}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The four dynamic equations are
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-dynamics}
\begin{align}
 \dot K&=Y-C-U-M-\delta K,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-kdot}\\
 \dot B&=\chi(BM)^\eta\psi(B),
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-bdot}\\
 \frac{\dot C}{C}&=n+r-\rho,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-cdot}\\
 \frac{\dot q}{q}&=r-\frac{X}{qB^2}-\eta\frac{\dot B}{B}
 -\dot B\frac{\psi'(B)}{\psi(B)}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-qdot}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The two transversality conditions are
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:rewrite-equilibrium-tvcs}
\begin{align}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}&=0,
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-household-tvc}\\
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}q_tB_t&=0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-eq-developer-tvc}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
At \(\sigma=1\), equations \eqref{eq:rewrite-eq-z} and
\eqref{eq:rewrite-eq-sx} are understood at their continuous limits:
$Z=(AL)^{\omega_L}X^{\omega_X}$ and $s_X=\omega_X$. If
\(\overline B<\infty\), the path satisfies $B_t<\overline B$ at every finite
date. Finally, the household and developer solve their stated optimization
problems, final-good firms maximize profit, and all markets clear. The initial
values \(C(0)\) and \(q(0)\) are endogenous jump variables selected to satisfy
the two transversality conditions.
\end{definition}

The definition does not impose balanced growth. A balanced-growth path is one
possible equilibrium trajectory, not the meaning of equilibrium. Nor are the
dated equations alone enough: a numerical path is admitted as an equilibrium
only if it also satisfies both transversality conditions and the global
optimality requirements in Definition \ref{def:rewrite-equilibrium}.

The system contains no separate household budget or final-good zero-profit
equation because these are redundant after market clearing. The consolidated
income decomposition follows from the equations in the definition:
\begin{equation}
 1=\frac{(r+\delta)K}{Y}+\frac{wL}{Y}
 +\frac{\Pi}{Y}+\frac{U}{Y}+\frac{M}{Y}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-income-decomposition}
\end{equation}
It records how final output is divided among gross capital income, labor income,
developer profit, inference compute, and research compute. It is an accounting
consequence of equilibrium, not an additional equilibrium condition.
