\section{The uncapped economy with unit elasticity}
\label{app:rewrite-uncapped-unit}

I now remove the efficiency frontier and set $\sigma=1$. The research law is
\begin{equation}
 \dot B=\chi(BM)^\eta.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-law}
\end{equation}
All other primitives and the meaning of equilibrium remain as in
Section~\ref{sec:rewrite-model}. In Definition~\ref{def:rewrite-equilibrium},
I set $\psi(B)=1$ and $\psi'(B)=0$ and remove the upper restriction on $B$.
Allocations must still be finite at every finite date, agents must attain
their optima, and both transversality conditions must hold. This is a
separate equilibrium construction, not a limit of the finite-frontier
equilibria in Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-equilibrium-regimes}.

\subsection{An explicit balanced-growth equilibrium}

At unit elasticity, define the output elasticities
\begin{equation}
 \beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X,\qquad
 \lambda=(1-\alpha)\omega_L,
 \qquad \alpha+\beta+\lambda=1.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-elasticities}
\end{equation}
Then $s_X=\omega_X$ and $e_X=1-\beta$. The AI-demand and monopoly conditions,
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-ai-price} and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc}, give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{p_XX}{Y}=\beta,\qquad \frac UY=\beta^2.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-static-shares}
\end{equation}
Substituting $X=BU=\beta^2BY$ into the Cobb--Douglas production function yields
\begin{equation}
 Y^{1-\beta}=K^\alpha(AL)^\lambda(\beta^2B)^\beta.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-production}
\end{equation}
On an exact balanced-growth path with positive constant $K/Y$, $C/Y$, and $M/Y$,
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-production}
and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-law} require, respectively,
\begin{align}
 g_Y&=n+\gamma+\frac{\beta}{\lambda}g_B,
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-production-growth}\\
 (1-\eta)g_B&=\eta g_Y.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-research-growth}
\end{align}
Define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\equiv\lambda(1-\eta)-\beta\eta
 =(1-\alpha)(1-\eta-\omega_X).
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-feedback}
\end{equation}
For $\Delta>0$, the unique solution of these two growth restrictions is
\begin{align}
 g_Y^*&=\frac{(1-\eta)(1-\omega_X)}{1-\eta-\omega_X}(n+\gamma),
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-output-growth}\\
 g_B^*&=\frac{\eta(1-\omega_X)}{1-\eta-\omega_X}(n+\gamma).
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-efficiency-growth}
\end{align}
The superscript $*$ denotes the candidate balanced-growth path. Its interest
rate, wage growth, and output growth per person are
\begin{align}
 g_{Y/N}^*=g_w^*&=g_Y^*-n
 =\gamma+\frac{\eta\omega_X}{1-\eta-\omega_X}(n+\gamma),
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-wage-growth}\\
 r^*&=\rho+g_Y^*-n.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-return}
\end{align}
Here $g_K=g_C=g_Y^*$ follows from constant capital and consumption ratios;
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-wage} and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-euler} then give
\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-wage-growth}
and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-return}.

Define the candidate ratios $k^*=K/Y$, $u^*=U/Y$,
$i^*=(\dot K+\delta K)/Y$, $m^*=M/Y$, and $c^*=C/Y$ by
\begin{align}
 k^*&=\frac{\alpha}{r^*+\delta},\qquad u^*=\beta^2,
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-capital-inference}\\
 i^*&=(g_Y^*+\delta)k^*,
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-investment}\\
 m^*&=\frac{\beta^2\eta g_B^*}{\rho-n+\eta g_Y^*},
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-research-share}\\
 c^*&=1-u^*-i^*-m^*.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-consumption}
\end{align}
In this appendix, $k^*$ is a capital--output ratio, not capital per unit of
effective labor. The research share comes from the developer's first-order
and costate conditions; the consumption share comes from market clearing.
These ratios are endogenous, not fixed saving or research rules.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[An uncapped balanced-growth equilibrium]
\label{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-bgp}
Maintain $0<\alpha<1$, $0<\omega_X<1$, $\omega_L=1-\omega_X$,
$\chi>0$, $\delta\geq0$, $n\geq0$, $\gamma>0$, and $\rho>n$.
Suppose $\sigma=1$ and
\begin{equation}
 0<\eta<\alpha,\qquad \eta\leq\tfrac12,\qquad
 \beta\leq\tfrac12,\qquad \eta+\omega_X<1.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-sufficient}
\end{equation}
For every $A_0,N_0>0$, there exist $K_0^*,B_0^*>0$ and an
infinite-horizon equilibrium with the constant ratios and growth rates in
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-output-growth}--\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-consumption}.
Along this equilibrium, $B_t=B_0^*e^{g_B^*t}\to\infty$,
$g_K=g_C=g_U=g_M=g_Y^*$, $g_X=g_Y^*+g_B^*$, and $g_q=g_Y^*-g_B^*$.
Consumption is strictly positive, both agents have finite-valued global
optima, and both transversality conditions hold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-proofs},
\hyperref[proof:rewrite-uncapped-unit-bgp]{Proof of
Proposition~\ref*{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-bgp}}.
\end{proof}

This result constructs the initial stocks together with the equilibrium.
It does not establish existence for independently specified $K_0$ and $B_0$.
Nor is it a steady state in levels: quantities grow exponentially, while
ratios and appropriately normalized variables are stationary.

The conditions have different roles. Since $n+\gamma>0$, equations
\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-production-growth}
and~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-research-growth} require $\Delta>0$ for
this positive finite-rate BGP. If $\Delta=0$, they are inconsistent; if
$\Delta<0$, they imply $g_B<0$, contrary to the research law. Thus
$\eta+\omega_X\geq1$ excludes this BGP, not every possible equilibrium
trajectory. The restrictions $\beta\leq1/2$ and $\eta\leq1/2$ make operating
profit and research technology concave and are sufficient for the global
optimality proof. They are not asserted to be necessary for existence.
I retain $\eta<\alpha$ to match the research-curvature restriction discussed
in Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-research-scale}; the construction here
does not use that comparison separately. Finally, $\rho>n$ makes both
transversality expressions decline and the objective integrals converge.

\subsection{Equilibrium trajectories near the balanced-growth path}

The BGP supplies a reference for proving existence from nearby initial
stocks that need not be on that path. Define stationary log deviations
\begin{equation}
 \xi_K=\log\frac K{K^*},\quad
 \xi_B=\log\frac B{B^*},\quad
 \xi_C=\log\frac C{C^*},\quad
 \xi_q=\log\frac q{q^*}.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-deviations}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the four equilibrium dynamics gives a smooth autonomous
system $\dot\xi=f(\xi)$ with $f(0)=0$, where
$\xi=(\xi_K,\xi_B,\xi_C,\xi_q)'$. Let $J^*=Df(0)$ denote its Jacobian.
The proof in Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-proofs} derives its entries directly from
equations~\eqref{eq:rewrite-eq-kdot}--\eqref{eq:rewrite-eq-qdot}, with
$\psi=1$ and $\psi'=0$.

\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Local existence of uncapped equilibrium trajectories]
\label{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-local}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-bgp},
fix $A_0,N_0>0$ and their reference BGP. Suppose $J^*$ has no eigenvalue
with zero real part and exactly two with negative real parts. Let
$V_S\in\mathbb R^{4\times2}$ be a real basis of its stable eigenspace and
$V_{KB}$ its first two rows. If $\det V_{KB}\ne0$, then every
$(K_0,B_0)$ in a sufficiently small open neighborhood of $(K_0^*,B_0^*)$
has an infinite-horizon equilibrium trajectory converging to the BGP in
the normalized variables~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-deviations}.
The required $(C_0,q_0)$ are continuously differentiable functions of
$(K_0,B_0)$. The trajectory satisfies both transversality conditions and
global agent optimality. Uniqueness is local, among paths that remain in
the specified neighborhood and converge to the reference BGP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-proofs},
\hyperref[proof:rewrite-uncapped-unit-local]{Proof of
Proposition~\ref*{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-local}}.
\end{proof}

No constant growth rates or allocation shares are imposed along these
transitions. The result is local in initial stocks, not in time; it does
not establish existence or uniqueness from arbitrary initial conditions.
For the parameters in Table~\ref{tab:rewrite-parameters}, the eigenvalues are
approximately $-0.098971$, $-0.003194$, $0.040391$, and $0.149290$.
The absolute determinant of the state projection of unit-length stable
eigenvectors is $0.6037$. The two spectral conditions therefore hold at
this calibration. The normalized Jacobian is independent of $\chi$;
changing research productivity changes the reference levels, not these roots.

\subsection{Growth and distribution without a frontier}

The uncapped BGP has
\begin{equation}
 \frac{wL}{Y}=\lambda>0,\qquad
 \frac{(r+\delta)K}{Y}=\alpha,\qquad
 \frac{\Pi}{Y}=\beta-\beta^2-m^*.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-distribution}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-wage-growth} shows that real wages
and output per person grow together at a rate above $\gamma$, even though
labor retains a positive, constant income share. Without a frontier,
continued growth in AI efficiency sustains this wage-growth premium.
With any finite frontier, the unit-elastic equilibria in
Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-capped-unit-existence} instead have
$g_{Y/N},g_w\to\gamma$. The equivalence between wage growth above
$\gamma$ and a vanishing labor share across the finite-frontier regimes
does not extend to the uncapped economy.

Within the restrictions of Proposition~\ref{prop:rewrite-uncapped-unit-bgp},
increasing the AI weight raises the common growth rate and the interest rate:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial g_{Y/N}^*}{\partial\omega_X}
 =\frac{\partial g_w^*}{\partial\omega_X}
 =\frac{\partial r^*}{\partial\omega_X}
 =\frac{\eta(1-\eta)(n+\gamma)}{(1-\eta-\omega_X)^2}>0.
 \label{eq:rewrite-uncapped-unit-comparative-statics}
\end{equation}
Labor's income share falls across these parameterizations, but remains
constant over time on each BGP. As $\omega_X\to0^+$, the common per-person
growth rate converges to $\gamma$ and $r^*\to\rho+\gamma$. These statements
compare balanced-growth equilibria; they do not describe a transition
from a no-AI economy or establish an uncapped equilibrium for $\sigma>1$.
