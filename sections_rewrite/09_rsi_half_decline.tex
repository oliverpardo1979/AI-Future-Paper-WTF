\subsection{Central illustrative scenario}
\label{subsec:rewrite-rsi-half-decline}

In the central illustrative scenario, I choose $\chi$ to approximate a
first-year research-expenditure share of $0.183\%$ at $\sigma=1$:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\int_0^1 M_t\,dt}{\int_0^1 Y_t\,dt}=0.00183.
 \label{eq:rewrite-rsi-central-research-target}
\end{equation}
This is an illustrative target, not an observed estimate of autonomous
RSI expenditure. It measures total research expenditure divided by total
output during the first year, not the instantaneous ratio $M_0/Y_0$.
The retained $\chi=7.616305$ gives $0.182916\%$, which rounds to the
target. Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-algorithm} documents this approximate match
and the original price-based calibration from which this value was retained.

The implied AI-service price decline at unit elasticity is approximately
$40\%$ over 2.25 years; it is not an additional calibration target.
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-monopoly-foc} gives a constant markup and a
price inversely proportional to $B$, so AI productivity increases by a
factor of approximately $5/3$ over this interval.

Each economy starts on the fixed-productivity BGP described in
Section~\ref{subsec:rewrite-rsi-activation}, with existing AI and
$K_0/Y_0=3.30$. RSI is activated unexpectedly at date zero. I calibrate
$\chi$ at unit elasticity, hold it fixed across the other three elasticities,
and solve consumption and research using the BVP and equilibrium checks
described in Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-algorithm}.
Table~\ref{tab:rewrite-rsi-half-parameters} reports the parameters and initial stocks.

The illustrative target is of a similar order of magnitude to estimated
US training and research compute expenditure: $0.1544\%$ of GDP in 2024 and $0.3562\%$
in 2025.\footnote{Training compute expenditure in Table 3 of
\citet{korinekmckelvey2026} is \$45.23 billion in 2024 and \$109.58 billion
in 2025. The corresponding nominal GDP values in the BEA series
\texttt{GDPA}, accessed through
\href{https://fred.stlouisfed.org/data/GDPA}{FRED} on September 14, 2026,
are \$29,298.013 billion and \$30,762.099 billion.}
These estimates use rental-equivalent compute costs and allocate half of
AI compute spending to training and research; they do not measure worldwide
autonomous RSI directly. The comparison supports the order of magnitude at
unit elasticity, not an empirical fit for all four scenarios. In particular,
first-year research expenditure is $3.0091\%$ of output at $\sigma=1.50$,
well above these estimates. Research expenditure relative to output also
falls between the first and second years in all four scenarios, unlike the
increase in the US estimates.
