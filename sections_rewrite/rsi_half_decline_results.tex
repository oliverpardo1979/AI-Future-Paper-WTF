The fitted value is $\chi=7.616305$, about 5.3 times the principal value
of $1.4378$. Initial output-per-person growth is $1.62\%$, $2.71\%$,
$3.02\%$, and $4.54\%$ across the four elasticities. At $\sigma=1.50$,
it rises from $1.45\%$ in the principal calibration to $4.54\%$.
Output, wages and prices remain continuous at activation, but growth
rates adjust immediately. The faster transition therefore retains an
abrupt response to the unexpected availability of RSI.

The initial resource reallocation is larger. At $\sigma=1.50$,
consumption rises by $5.05\%$ at activation instead of $0.84\%$, and
research absorbs $3.10\%$ of output instead of $0.57\%$. Initial developer
net profit falls from $2.73\%$ to $0.20\%$ of output but remains positive.
Gross investment is $13.79\%$ of output, compared with $19.57\%$ in the
principal calibration. Neither calibration requires initial developer
losses or negative gross investment in this case. Investment in the faster
transition is nevertheless below depreciation, so the capital stock
initially falls at an annualized rate of $0.82\%$. These are equilibrium
outcomes, not added financing or investment constraints.

At $\sigma=1.50$, labor's income share reaches half its initial value after
approximately 47 years rather than 221. It completes $90\%$ of its decline
toward zero after about 137 years rather than 424. The limiting growth
rates, interest rate and income shares are unchanged. Research productivity
changes the speed and shape of adjustment, not these long-run outcomes.
The initial size of the AI industry is also unchanged and remains an
unfitted feature of the calibration.
