\section{Numerical algorithm and equilibrium audit}
\label{app:rewrite-algorithm}

\subsection{Economic intuition and initial conditions}

The BVP selects an equilibrium trajectory, not an initial-value solution
from arbitrary consumption and shadow prices. Capital and AI efficiency
are predetermined; consumption and the developer's shadow value adjust
to put the economy on the stable continuation of its long-run regime.
An initial choice that satisfies the static conditions alone need not do so.

The RSI-activation exercises first solve the same auxiliary economy:
AI is already used in production, but its efficiency is fixed because RSI
is unavailable. For each $\sigma$, a scalar root search in $\log K_0$
solves Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-rsi-initial-ratio} using the original
production and monopoly equations. Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-rsi-pre-consumption}
then gives pre-event consumption. Homogeneity extends these date-zero
values to a pre-event BGP: $K,Y,C,U,X$ grow at $n+\gamma$, the wage grows
at $\gamma$, and $B,p_X,r$ and income shares are constant. Since
$r-(n+\gamma)=\rho-n>0$, the developer's pre-event profit has finite
present value. The household's transversality expression decays at
$n-\rho<0$. Research is unavailable, rather than suppressed despite a
profitable research opportunity.

At the unanticipated activation, only the states are inherited. The
post-event problem uses the original positive-$\chi$ equilibrium equations;
it does not impose pre-event consumption or a post-event zero-research
corner. A separate audit checks continuity of the production quantities
and prices at date zero and the pre-event Euler, resource, and monopoly
conditions. It also records the endogenous consumption and research jumps.

\subsection{BVP and calibration}

I retain the collocation solver \texttt{scipy.integrate.solve\_bvp} and
the computational coordinates
\[
 \left(\log K,\ \log\frac{B}{\overline B-B},\ \log C,\ \log q\right).
\]
Subtracting the analytical terminal aggregate growth rate times $t$ removes
their linear trends without changing any model variable or its timing.
Effective labor remains exogenous. At every trial date and state, the code
solves the static monopoly problem and recovers research from its first-order
condition. The left boundary fixes $K_0,B_0$; two right-boundary projections
remove the unstable components of the normalized equilibrium dynamics near
the long-run limit. The regime-specific normalization and stable continuation
are those of Appendix~\ref{app:rewrite-finite-existence}.

For each elasticity, I start near its analytical limiting point and move
the initial stocks to their prescribed values in 32 continuation stages.
This is a numerical construction inside the positive-AI, positive-RSI
problem; neither $\omega_X=0$ nor $\chi=0$ is used as a degenerate BVP seed.
At unit elasticity the static block uses its exact Cobb--Douglas limit;
near one, \texttt{log1p} and \texttt{expm1} avoid cancellation.

An outer bracketed search in $\log\chi$ solves the unit-elastic BVP and
evaluates the price ratio at exactly 2.25 years. It matches
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-rsi-price-target} or
Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-rsi-half-price-target}, then fixes $\chi$ for the
other three elasticities. The tolerance in $\log\chi$ is $2\times10^{-6}$;
the final price-ratio log error must be below $2\times10^{-5}$ after both
horizon extensions. These numerical tolerances are well below the precision
of the rounded empirical target.

Section~\ref{subsec:rewrite-rsi-research-share} instead uses an approximate
research-expenditure calibration at $\sigma=1$. For each trial $\chi$,
Gauss--Legendre quadrature with 64 and 128 nodes evaluates the two integrals
in Equation~\eqref{eq:rewrite-research-share-target}; their ratio must agree
within $10^{-8}$ in logs. I reuse the increasing low-$\chi$ branch from the
earlier numerical search and select its candidate closest to the 2023
proxy. The selected $\chi=1.4378$ produces an annual ratio of $0.06374\%$,
below the $0.06638\%$ target by $0.26$ basis points. This is an approximate
empirical match, not a root solved to numerical precision. Publication
requires a discrepancy below one basis point as well as all the original
equilibrium checks. The annual moment must also remain stable within
$2\times10^{-5}$ in logs after both horizon extensions. Only the verified
unit-elastic path is displayed; the earlier high-target search and its
figures remain available in the repository.

\subsection{Equilibrium checks and replication}

I extend each computational horizon twice by 500 years and tighten the
collocation tolerance from $2\times10^{-6}$ to $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-9}$.
The final boundary tolerance is $10^{-11}$. The initial meshes have
221, 401, and 601 points and adapt as needed. The displayed window lies
within each solved horizon; no spline is extrapolated.

The original equations are reconstructed independently with five-point
differences at 1,001 dates and steps of 0.003 and 0.001 years. An additional
801-date check over the first ten years uses steps of 0.0003 and 0.0001.
Admission requires dynamic residuals below $10^{-6}$, first-order residuals
below $10^{-9}$, final-horizon changes below $2\times10^{-5}$ in detrended
logs, and terminal-coordinate gaps below $10^{-4}$. These are unchanged
from the earlier algorithm; none relaxes an economic condition.

The audit also checks feasibility and the developer's global optimality.
When global profit concavity in research depth fails, I apply the
Hamiltonian support condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:rewrite-developer-verification},
with counterfactual-state minimization and an analytical bound beyond the
grid. Two grids use 81/101 and 321/241 date/state points, supplemented by
dense first-decade checks. The analytical terminal support bound and the
asymptotic rate $n-\rho<0$ of both transversality expressions support the
infinite-horizon continuation. These are numerically verified equilibrium
approximations, not interval-arithmetic existence certificates for arbitrary
initial stocks.
