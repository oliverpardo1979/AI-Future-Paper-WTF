\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor\\
Under Recursive AI Self-Improvement}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
I develop an endogenous-growth model in which a monopolistic developer
supplies AI production services and uses AI research services to improve AI
efficiency, creating recursive self-improvement. I characterize the model's
long-run equilibrium regimes when AI efficiency has a finite upper bound.
Long-run outcomes depend jointly on the substitutability between AI and labor
and on the level of this bound. When labor remains a production bottleneck,
output per person and real wages grow at an exogenously given rate of
labor-augmenting technological progress, and labor retains a positive income
share. With sufficient substitution and a sufficiently high upper bound, the
economy instead becomes AI-dominated: real-wage growth exceeds this exogenous
rate, but output per person grows even faster. Labor's income share therefore
converges to zero. Within this regime, long-run growth and the interest rate
rise with the upper bound. Numerical equilibrium paths show that moderate
growth can persist for a long time before output accelerates and labor's income
share falls sharply. Rapid growth of AI production services, an interest rate
above the labor-bottleneck benchmark, and a widening gap between
output-per-person and real-wage growth can appear earlier.
\end{abstract}
