\section{Baseline model}
\label{sec:axm-model}

Time is continuous, and I suppress time subscripts except where they are needed
for clarity. There is one final good, a representative household, competitive
final-good firms, and an integrated AI developer. Two productivity indexes enter
the model. Labor productivity \(A\) grows exogenously. AI capability \(B\) is
produced endogenously by the developer. The developer monopolistically supplies
AI production services and invests in better AI. The initial economy already
uses AI production services; the model does not describe their first commercial
adoption.

\subsection{Population, labor endowment, and labor productivity}

Population grows exogenously at the constant rate \(n>0\):
\begin{equation}
 \dot N=nN.
 \label{eq:axm-population}
\end{equation}
Each person supplies one unit of homogeneous labor inelastically, so the
household supplies \(N\) units at the common wage \(w\). Final-good firms demand
production labor \(L\), and the AI developer demands human research labor \(H\).
The equality \(N=L+H\) is a labor-market-clearing condition and is imposed only
when equilibrium is defined.

The variable \(A\) measures labor-augmenting productivity in final production.
One unit of production labor supplies \(A\) efficiency units, so effective
production labor is \(AL\). Labor productivity follows the exogenous law
\begin{equation}
 \dot A=\gamma_A A,
 \qquad \gamma_A\geq0,
 \label{eq:axm-labor-productivity}
\end{equation}
with \(A(0)>0\). This productivity index augments production labor \(L\), not
human research labor \(H\). The case \(\gamma_A=0\) nests the specification
without conventional labor-productivity growth. For any positive variable
\(V\), write \(g_V\equiv\dot V/V\); hence \(g_A=\gamma_A\).

\subsection{Household}

The representative household discounts utility at rate \(\rho>n\). It takes the paths of the net interest rate \(r\),
the wage \(w\), distributed developer profit \(\Pi\), and population \(N\) as
given. Let \(K\) denote its holdings of physical capital. Given \(K(0)>0\), it
chooses \(C\) and \(K\) to maximize
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-\rho t}N_t\log(C_t/N_t)\,\dd t.
\]
It owns physical capital and the AI developer and receives labor income, the net
return on capital, and distributed profit. Its flow budget is
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wN+\Pi-C.
 \label{eq:axm-household-budget}
\end{equation}
The variable \(r\) is the net real interest rate: depreciation is already
deducted from the rental return to capital. At this stage, \(r\) is a price the
household takes as given; no production-side condition has yet been imposed. The
household's interior Euler equation in aggregate variables is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+r-\rho.
 \label{eq:axm-euler}
\end{equation}
The infinite-horizon terminal restriction maintained for the household is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0.
 \label{eq:axm-household-tvc}
\end{equation}
The developer's problem below defines \(\Pi\). The equilibrium definition later
requires the household's capital holdings to equal final-good firms' capital
demand and links household labor supply to the two sectors' labor demands.

\subsection{Final-good firms}

Competitive final-good firms take \(r,w,p_X\) as given and demand capital,
production labor, and AI production services, where \(p_X\) is their price.
To preserve the notation I use throughout the
analysis and in the numerical code, I do not give \(K\) and \(X\) agent-specific
superscripts. In this subsection they denote capital and AI production services demanded by
the final sector; in the household problem \(K\) denotes capital holdings, and in
the developer's problem \(X\) denotes sales. Equality of the corresponding
quantities is imposed only in equilibrium. Let \(0<\alpha<1\) be the physical-capital
elasticity, let \(\delta\geq0\) be the depreciation rate, and let
\(\sigma_{XL}>0\) be the elasticity of substitution between production labor and
AI production services. The CES weights satisfy \(\omega_L,\omega_X\in(0,1)\) and
\(\omega_L+\omega_X=1\). The production technology is
\begin{align}
 Y&=K^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha},\\
 Z&=\left[\omega_L (AL)^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}
 +\omega_X X^{\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}}\right]^{
 \frac{\sigma_{XL}}{\sigma_{XL}-1}},
 \qquad \omega_L+\omega_X=1.
 \label{eq:axm-final-production}
\end{align}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, \(\sigma_{XL}\to1\), the CES becomes the
Cobb--Douglas aggregate \(Z=(AL)^{\omega_L}X^{\omega_X}\). Define the contribution
of AI production services to the CES quantity index as
\begin{equation}
 s_X=\frac{\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}
 {\omega_L (AL)^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 +\omega_X X^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}}.
 \label{eq:axm-sx}
\end{equation}
Thus \(s_X\) is the elasticity of \(Z\) with respect to \(X\). The competitive
sector solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{K,L,X\geq0}\left\{K^\alpha Z(AL,X)^{1-\alpha}
 -(r+\delta)K-wL-p_XX\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-final-firm-problem}
\end{equation}
The interior first-order conditions are
\begin{equation}
 r=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta,\qquad
 w=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\frac{Y}{L},\qquad
 p_X=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{Y}{X}.
 \label{eq:axm-factor-prices}
\end{equation}
Labor productivity \(A\) affects the wage through \(Y\) and \(s_X\). It does
not appear as an additional factor in the displayed wage equation because
\(AL\) is linear in \(L\), so the output elasticity with respect to \(L\) equals
the elasticity with respect to effective labor \(AL\).

To ask how an expansion of AI production services affects production labor,
holding \(K\) fixed is not enough: either the wage or labor must also be held
fixed. I therefore state both sides of the same comparative static. The
conditional labor-demand response holds \(K\), \(A\), and \(w\) fixed; the dual
wage response holds \(K\), \(A\), and \(L\) fixed. The distinction between the
wage and labor-share responses follows the general result in
\citet[Theorem~1]{autorkausik2026}. For this nested Cobb--Douglas--CES
technology, \citet[Proposition~2.2, Appendix~A.4, Lemma~4.1, and
Proposition~4.2]{yango2026} derive the corresponding labor-demand sign and the
two elasticity thresholds.

Define production labor's share of final output as
\begin{equation}
 \ell^Y\equiv\frac{wL}{Y}=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X).
 \label{eq:axm-production-labor-share}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{24\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[AI services and production labor]
\label{prop:axm-ai-labor-comparative-static}
For an interior final-good firm, an increase in \(X\), holding \(K\), \(A\),
and \(L\) fixed, satisfies
\begin{align}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln Y}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,L}
 &=(1-\alpha)s_X>0,
 \label{eq:axm-output-x-elasticity}\\
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln w}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,L}
 &=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{XL}}-\alpha\right)s_X,
 \label{eq:axm-wage-x-elasticity}\\
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln \ell^Y}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,L}
 &=-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{XL}}\right)s_X.
 \label{eq:axm-labor-share-x-elasticity}
\end{align}
Equivalently, holding \(K\), \(A\), and \(w\) fixed, conditional production-labor
demand satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln L}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,w}
 =
 \frac{s_X\left(\sigma_{XL}^{-1}-\alpha\right)}
 {\alpha(1-s_X)+s_X/\sigma_{XL}}.
 \label{eq:axm-labor-demand-x-elasticity}
\end{equation}
Thus an expansion of AI production services raises conditional labor demand---or,
at fixed labor, raises the wage---if and only if
\(\sigma_{XL}<1/\alpha\). It lowers conditional labor demand and the wage if and
only if \(\sigma_{XL}>1/\alpha\). By contrast, production labor's share falls if
and only if \(\sigma_{XL}>1\). Since \(0<\alpha<1\), the interval
\begin{equation}
 1<\sigma_{XL}<\frac{1}{\alpha}
 \label{eq:axm-wage-share-decoupling-region}
\end{equation}
is nonempty: within it, more AI production services raise labor demand at a
given wage, and raise the wage at a given employment level, even as labor
receives a smaller share of output.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The CES share in \eqref{eq:axm-sx} implies
\[
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln(1-s_X)}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{A,L}
 =-\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma_{XL}}\right)s_X.
\]
The output elasticity in \eqref{eq:axm-output-x-elasticity} follows directly
from \eqref{eq:axm-final-production}. Combining these two elasticities with the
wage equation in \eqref{eq:axm-factor-prices} gives
\eqref{eq:axm-wage-x-elasticity}; combining the share equation above with
\eqref{eq:axm-production-labor-share} gives
\eqref{eq:axm-labor-share-x-elasticity}. Finally,
\[
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln w}{\partial\ln L}\right|_{K,A,X}
 =-\left[\alpha(1-s_X)+\frac{s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}\right]<0.
\]
Implicit differentiation of the labor-demand condition at fixed \(w\) then
gives \eqref{eq:axm-labor-demand-x-elasticity}. Its denominator is strictly
positive, so its sign is the sign of \(\sigma_{XL}^{-1}-\alpha\). The remaining
sign statements follow from the displayed elasticities.
\end{proof}

The final-good firm's AI-demand condition implies that expenditure on AI production services
as a share of final output is
\(p_XX/Y=(1-\alpha)s_X\). Equivalently, within total payments to the
noncapital composite, \(p_XX/[(1-\alpha)Y]=s_X\). After market clearing for AI
production services, the first ratio is the developer's gross service revenue as a share of
final output.
Constant returns and the three first-order conditions imply zero profit:
\begin{equation}
 Y=(r+\delta)K+wL+p_XX.
 \label{eq:axm-final-zero-profit}
\end{equation}
Solving the AI-demand condition for its price yields the inverse demand schedule
\(p_X(X;K,A,L)\). I use the following notational convention: \(p_X\) without
arguments denotes the realized price, whereas \(p_X(X;K,A,L)\) denotes the inverse
demand function. This schedule comes from final-good firms' optimal demand; it is
not a first-order condition of the developer. Define its inverse absolute
elasticity, holding \(K\), \(A\), and \(L\) fixed, as
\begin{equation}
 \varepsilon_X^{-1}
 \equiv-\left.\frac{\partial\ln p_X(X;K,A,L)}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,L}
 =\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}+\alpha s_X.
 \label{eq:axm-inverse-elasticity}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{11\baselineskip}
\begin{remark}[Tasks, jobs, and labor services]
The inputs \(L\) and \(H\) are quantities of homogeneous labor services, not
numbers of tasks, occupations, or jobs. Labor-market clearing imposes full
employment and allows workers to move between production and research. The
model therefore predicts wages, factor shares, and sectoral labor allocation,
not job creation, unemployment, or the number of tasks displaced by AI.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Integrated AI developer}

\subsubsection{AI production services}

The model distinguishes two uses of compute. \(U\) is inference compute: raw
compute used to operate existing AI systems and supply services to final-good
firms. \(M\) is AI R\&D compute---called research compute below---used to
improve AI capability. Both are flows rather than stocks of chips, and both have
the same constant unit resource cost. I choose units so that one unit of raw
compute costs one unit of final output.

\(B\) indexes current AI capability: what one unit of compute can accomplish.
Inference compute produces the flow of AI production services supplied by
the developer,
\begin{equation}
 X=BU.
 \label{eq:axm-ai-service}
\end{equation}
Research compute analogously produces the AI research service flow
\(BM\), which enters the research technology below. The paid inputs are \(U\)
and \(M\), whereas \(X\) and \(BM\) are service flows. This product of nonrival
capability and rival compute is the one-capability version of the
capability--compute accounting in \citet{davidsonetal2026}.

This unit-cost choice is a normalization; it does not make compute free.
Producing one unit of either service flow uses \(1/B\) units of final output.

technology. It separates raw inference compute, the AI production services purchased by
final-good firms, and the service composite used in production. The two
technology diagrams in this subsection are schematics, not numerical results.
Gray boxes denote current inputs, blue boxes denote stocks, and orange boxes
denote transformations or outputs.

\subsubsection{Frontier research technology}

Let \(\sigma_{HM}>0\) be the elasticity of substitution between human and
AI research services. The CES weights satisfy
\(\omega_H,\omega_M\in(0,1)\) and \(\omega_H+\omega_M=1\). The parameter
\(\chi>0\) shifts research productivity, while \(0<\eta<1\) governs returns to
the current scale of research. For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), human researchers and AI research services
improve capability through the homothetic CES technology
\begin{equation}
 \dot B=\chi
 \left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(BM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{\eta/\varrho_{HM}},
 \qquad
 \varrho_{HM}=\frac{\sigma_{HM}-1}{\sigma_{HM}},
 \qquad \omega_H+\omega_M=1.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law}
\end{equation}
For its dual representation, let \(E\) denote the constant-returns
effective-research index
\begin{equation}
 E\equiv\left[\omega_H H^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(BM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{1/\varrho_{HM}}.
 \label{eq:axm-effective-research}
\end{equation}
The object \(E\) is an index of the two research inputs, not an additional good,
stock, or production stage. The capability law is equivalently
\(\dot B=\chi E^\eta\); hence a target \(\dot B\) requires
\(E=(\dot B/\chi)^{1/\eta}\). At the unit-elasticity parameter limit,
\(\sigma_{HM}\to1\), the technology becomes
\begin{equation}
 \dot B=\chi H^{\eta\omega_H}(BM)^{\eta\omega_M},
 \qquad E=H^{\omega_H}(BM)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-capability-law-cd}
\end{equation}

The parameters have separate economic roles. The elasticity \(\sigma_{HM}\)
governs substitution between human research services \(H\) and AI
research services \(BM\). The exponent \(\eta\) governs returns to their joint
scale: for a fixed \(B\), multiplying both \(H\) and \(M\) by \(\lambda\)
multiplies \(\dot B\) by \(\lambda^\eta\). The scalar \(\chi\) shifts research
productivity and affects the level and timing of capability improvement.
Finally, \(B\) enters only through \(BM\): capability raises the AI research
services produced by one unit of research compute. The baseline therefore has
no separate \(B^\phi\) term, and \(\eta\) governs returns to current research
scale rather than a separate ideas-getting-harder force
\citep{jones1995,davidsonetal2026}.

\subsubsection{Dynamic problem}

The developer sells \(X\), pays \(X/B\) for inference compute, hires \(H\), uses
\(M\) internally, and owns \(B\). It faces the inverse demand schedule derived
from final-good firms' optimal demand. Labor-market clearing is imposed in
equilibrium, but the developer does not internalize its effect on the competitive
wage. Taking the paths of \(r,w,K,A,\) and \(L\) as given, it solves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{X,H,M\}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[p_X(X_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)X_t-\frac{X_t}{B_t}-w_tH_t-M_t\right]\dd t.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-problem}
\end{equation}
subject to the capability law and the initial capability stock. This is the
developer's primitive optimization problem. The discount rate is the market
return available to the household that owns the developer. At this stage, the
developer takes that price path as given. Equilibrium later requires consistency
between this discount factor, household saving, and the return generated by
final-good firms. The developer's instantaneous distributed profit is
\begin{equation}
 \Pi=p_X(X;K,A,L)X-\frac{X}{B}-wH-M
 =p_XX-U-wH-M.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-profit}
\end{equation}
This net payout can be negative. The model imposes no separate cash-flow or
borrowing constraint, so a negative \(\Pi\) represents an equity injection from
the household that owns the developer.

Sales \(X\) do not enter the capability law or another intertemporal constraint,
and there is no firm-level capacity constraint linking \(U\) and \(M\). The
developer therefore chooses \(X\) pointwise, while \(H\) and \(M\) affect future
capability. These are parts of the same optimization problem, not additional
equilibrium conditions.

\subsubsection{Within-date supply of AI production services}

Conditional on current \(B,K,A,\) and \(L\), the choice of \(X\) is pointwise.
The developer chooses a sales quantity along the inverse demand schedule; this
uses the buyer's demand relation inside the seller's problem, but it is not the
developer's own demand condition. Optimized operating profit before research
expenditure is
\begin{equation}
 \pi(B;K,A,L)=\max_{X\geq0}
 \left\{p_X(X;K,A,L)X-\frac{X}{B}\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-service-supply}
\end{equation}
The residual-demand constraint requires the quantity offered to equal the
quantity demanded at its associated transaction price. General equilibrium
below records this transaction-level condition together with capital-, labor-,
and goods-market clearing. On an interior monopoly branch, the first-order
condition is
\begin{equation}
 p_X(1-\varepsilon_X^{-1})=\frac{1}{B}.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-foc}
\end{equation}
Writing \(e_X=\varepsilon_X^{-1}\), the strict second-order condition for the
within-date supply choice is
\begin{equation}
 e_X(1-e_X)
 +(\alpha-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})(1-\sigma_{XL}^{-1})s_X(1-s_X)>0.
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}
\end{equation}

Whenever the pointwise maximum in \eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} is attained,
substituting optimized operating profit into
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem} gives the equivalent reduced problem
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\{H,M\}}
 \int_0^\infty
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 \left[\pi(B_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)-w_tH_t-M_t\right]\dd t,
 \label{eq:axm-developer-problem-reduced}
\end{equation}
subject to the same capability law and initial stock. Thus the maximization over
\(X\) in \eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} is the pointwise inner maximization of
the dynamic problem, not a second decision problem. Along the optimum,
\(\Pi_t=\pi(B_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)-w_tH_t-M_t\).

not add an agent or an equilibrium condition; it only displays two equivalent
representations of the same optimization problem.

\subsubsection{Conditional research cost and dynamic optimality}

The mix of \(H\) and \(M\) can also be characterized within date. Conditional
on current \(B\) and \(w\), the unit-expenditure problem for the
effective-research index is
\begin{equation}
 P_E(B,w)=\min_{H,M\geq0}\left\{wH+M:
 E(H,BM)\geq1\right\}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-cost-problem}
\end{equation}
This is the dual representation of the research-input choice inside
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem-reduced}, not a separate equilibrium condition.
For \(\sigma_{HM}\neq1\), its solution is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left[\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}w^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 +\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{1}{B}\right)^{1-\sigma_{HM}}
 \right]^{1/(1-\sigma_{HM})}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price}
\end{equation}
At the unit-elasticity parameter limit, its continuous limit is
\begin{equation}
 P_E=\left(\frac{w}{\omega_H}\right)^{\omega_H}
 \left(\frac{1}{B\omega_M}\right)^{\omega_M}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-price-cd}
\end{equation}
Conditional on current \(B\) and \(w\), the minimum expenditure required to
produce a target flow \(\dot B\) is
\begin{equation}
 P_E\left(\frac{\dot B}{\chi}\right)^{1/\eta}.
 \label{eq:axm-research-expenditure-function}
\end{equation}
Thus \(P_E\) is not the unit cost of \(\dot B\) when \(\eta<1\).
Conditional input demands are
\begin{equation}
 H=\omega_H^{\sigma_{HM}}\left(\frac{P_E}{w}\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}E,
 \quad
 BM=\omega_M^{\sigma_{HM}}(BP_E)^{\sigma_{HM}}E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-demands}
\end{equation}
The share of research expenditure allocated to AI research services is
\begin{equation}
 s_M=\frac{M}{wH+M}.
 \label{eq:axm-sm}
\end{equation}
Because producing \(BM\) costs \(M\), the conditional demands imply that, along
an interior cost-minimizing research allocation, \(s_M\) is also the elasticity
of \(E\) with respect to \(BM\). It follows that the elasticity of \(\dot B\)
with respect to \(BM\) is \(\eta s_M\). Holding \(H\) and \(M\) fixed, this is
also the local elasticity of \(\dot B\) with respect to the predetermined
capability stock \(B\). These equalities use interior cost minimization; \(s_M\)
is not a physical fraction of research tasks performed by AI. Together with
\(s_X=\partial\ln Z/\partial\ln X\), it is an endogenous local feedback weight,
not a task share.

Let \(q\) be the current-value shadow price of AI capability \(B\). A common compute cost
\(\xi>0\) can be absorbed by setting
\(\widetilde U=\xi U\), \(\widetilde M=\xi M\),
\(\widetilde B=B/\xi\), \(\widetilde\chi=\chi/\xi\), and
\(\widetilde q=\xi q\). This leaves service flows, resource costs, and the
equilibrium conditions unchanged. I use the normalized variables below. The
direct first-order conditions for the two research inputs are
\begin{equation}
 q\frac{\partial\dot B}{\partial H}=w,\qquad
 q\frac{\partial\dot B}{\partial M}=1.
 \label{eq:axm-research-focs}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, combining the conditional input demands with the optimal choice of
the scale \(E\) gives
\begin{equation}
 q\chi\eta E^{\eta-1}=P_E.
 \label{eq:axm-research-scale-foc}
\end{equation}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
The pointwise operating choice and the conditional research-input allocation
provide the derivatives needed for the dynamic condition. On a differentiable
interior maximizing branch, the envelope theorem applied to
\eqref{eq:axm-service-supply} gives \(\pi_B=X/B^2\), where \(X\) is the selected
sales quantity. Along the interior cost-minimizing research allocation,
\(\partial\dot B/\partial B=\eta s_M\dot B/B\), because a fixed \(M\) supplies
more AI research services when \(B\) rises. The current-value costate equation is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=r-\frac{X}{qB^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot B}{B}.
 \label{eq:axm-costate}
\end{equation}
This is the developer's condition in terms of the market return it takes as
given. The last term is the main dynamic difference from research that combines
\(H\) directly with \(M\). The infinite-horizon terminal restriction maintained
for the developer is
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}q_tB_t=0.
 \label{eq:axm-developer-tvc}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Why research requires sufficient diminishing returns}

The restriction \(\eta<1\) gives decreasing returns to current research inputs
when capability is fixed. It need not control the developer's objective because
research also raises capability, which raises future operating profit. The next
result states the relevant large-scale restriction; the Appendix provides the
exact bounds and proof.

\begin{proposition}[Large-scale research expenditure]
\label{prop:axm-research-burst}
Suppose \(\sigma_{XL},\sigma_{HM}>0\) and \(0<\eta<1\). Fix a finite horizon,
an initial capability stock, positive continuous paths for \(K,A,L,w\), and
an integrable path for \(r\). Consider all nonnegative measurable research policies
with finite expenditure that generate an absolutely continuous capability path.
Truncate the reduced developer problem at this horizon and call the supremum of
its objective the truncated value. If \(\eta<\alpha\), this value is finite and
the objective tends to minus infinity as total research expenditure diverges.
If instead both CES nests permit gross substitution and \(\eta>\alpha\),
fixed-duration bursts of research compute make the truncated value unbounded above. At
\(\eta=\alpha\), under the same gross-substitution conditions, the leading
benefit and cost terms have the same order, so their coefficients determine the
result for this family of policies.
\end{proposition}

\begin{assumption}[Baseline large-scale research curvature]
\label{ass:axm-large-scale-curvature}
All baseline equilibrium results and numerical exercises maintain
\begin{equation}
 0<\eta<\alpha<1.
 \label{eq:axm-large-scale-curvature}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

The restriction is stronger than diminishing returns to current research
inputs. Proposition \ref{prop:axm-research-burst} shows that, on every fixed
horizon satisfying its conditions, it bounds the value and rules out
arbitrarily large research expenditure as optimal. It does not prove that a
maximizer exists, establish global concavity, control the infinite-horizon tail,
or prove equilibrium existence. The benchmark uses a strict margin,
\(\eta=0.20<\alpha=0.33\), chosen illustratively rather than estimated.

\subsection{Equilibrium}

Capital-market clearing requires the household's capital holdings to equal the
capital rented by final-good firms. Market clearing for AI production services requires the
quantity purchased by final-good firms to equal the quantity sold by the
developer at the transaction price \(p_X\). These conditions identify the
quantities denoted by \(K\) and \(X\) in the separate agent problems. From this
point onward, \(K\) and \(X\) denote their common equilibrium values. Labor-market
clearing imposes
\begin{equation}
 N=L+H.
 \label{eq:axm-labor-market}
\end{equation}

The aggregate resource constraint is derived rather than imposed as an
additional restriction. Substitute developer profit
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-profit}, labor-market clearing, and market clearing for
AI production services into the household budget \eqref{eq:axm-household-budget}:
\begin{equation}
 \dot K=rK+wL+p_XX-U-M-C.
 \label{eq:axm-capital-aggregation-step}
\end{equation}
Using the final sector's zero-profit identity
\eqref{eq:axm-final-zero-profit} yields
\begin{equation}
 C+\dot K+\delta K+U+M=Y.
 \label{eq:axm-resource}
\end{equation}

The same equilibrium conditions also yield a consolidated accounting identity.
Combining the final-sector zero-profit identity, the
definition of developer profit, and \(N=L+H\) gives
\begin{align}
 Y&=(r+\delta)K+wL+wH+\Pi+U+M,\notag\\
 1&=\frac{(r+\delta)K}{Y}+\frac{wL}{Y}+\frac{wH}{Y}
 +\frac{\Pi}{Y}+\frac{U}{Y}+\frac{M}{Y},\notag\\
 &=\frac{rK}{Y}+\frac{\delta K}{Y}+\frac{wN}{Y}
 +\frac{\Pi}{Y}+\frac{U+M}{Y}.
 \label{eq:axm-consolidated-distribution}
\end{align}
The second line is a six-part decomposition of final output: the gross capital
payment, the two sectoral wage bills, distributed developer profit, inference
compute, and research compute. The third line instead separates household
income from resource uses. The household receives net capital income \(rK\),
aggregate labor income \(wN\), and distributed developer profit \(\Pi\), whereas
depreciation and compute are resource uses. These are equivalent accounting
representations implied by the equilibrium conditions, not additional
restrictions.

Only now can price-taking conditions from different agents be combined. The
household Euler equation is \(g_C=n+r-\rho\), while the final-good firm's capital
demand satisfies \(r=\alpha Y/K-\delta\) after capital-market clearing. Their
combination gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot C}{C}=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho.
 \label{eq:axm-euler-reduced}
\end{equation}
Likewise, substituting the firm's capital-price condition into the developer's
costate equation gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot q}{q}=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qB^2}
 -\eta s_M\frac{\dot B}{B}.
 \label{eq:axm-costate-reduced}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:axm-euler-reduced} and
\eqref{eq:axm-costate-reduced} are equilibrium conditions. They are not
first-order conditions of the household or the developer in isolation.

The model therefore contains two feedback loops in the terminology of
\citet{davidsonetal2026}. The technological loop is
\(B\to BM\to E\to\dot B\to B\). The economic loop is
\(B\to X\to Y\to(K,M)\to BM\to E\to\dot B\to B\).
Three local elasticities summarize the links that form these feedback loops:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln X}{\partial\ln B}\right|_U=1,\qquad
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln Y}{\partial\ln X}\right|_{K,A,L}
 =(1-\alpha)s_X,\qquad
 \left.\frac{\partial\ln\dot B}{\partial\ln(BM)}\right|_H=\eta s_M.
 \label{eq:axm-local-feedback-links}
\end{equation}
The strength of the full economic loop is not a primitive constant because
saving, compute allocation, labor allocation, and prices are determined in
equilibrium.

\begin{definition}[Admissible path]
An admissible path has measurable nonnegative controls, absolutely continuous
nonnegative stocks \(K\) and \(B\), the exogenous population path \(N\), the
exogenous labor-productivity path \(A\), the stated initial values, and satisfies the
relevant flow budget and state equations. Its improper objective integrals
converge to finite real values. Deviations used to test household or developer
optimality must satisfy the corresponding requirements.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior equilibrium]
Given \(K(0),A(0),B(0),N(0)>0\), an interior equilibrium is an admissible path
with \(C,K,A,B,N,L,H,U,M,X,E,Z,Y>0\) and \(w,p_X>0\). The net interest
rate \(r\) and the developer's net payout \(\Pi\) may have either sign. The
household solves its intertemporal problem subject to
\eqref{eq:axm-household-budget} and \(K\geq0\), competitive final-good firms
maximize profits, the integrated developer solves
\eqref{eq:axm-developer-problem}, and labor, capital, AI production services,
and goods markets clear. I call the equilibrium regular when the associated
maximum-principle multipliers are normal (their objective multipliers are
nonzero), the relevant paths are differentiable wherever the displayed
derivatives are used, and the pointwise maxima are attained. Under these
conditions, the dated first-order and costate equations apply almost everywhere.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Interior candidate path]
An interior candidate path has the positive allocations and prices listed above,
has \(q>0\), allows \(r\) and \(\Pi\) to have either sign, and
satisfies the household Euler and flow-budget equations, the population and
labor-productivity laws,
competitive factor demands, the monopoly first- and second-order conditions,
both research first-order conditions, the developer's state, costate, and
transversality condition, the household transversality condition, all technology
and service identities, and labor, capital, AI production services, and goods market clearing.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Equilibrium conditions and candidate-path characterization}
\label{sec:axm-dated-equilibrium-system}

Every regular interior equilibrium satisfies the dated first-order conditions,
state equations, and market-clearing and accounting identities derived above.
The two transversality restrictions are treated separately below. The household
Euler equation, the developer costate, and the final-good firm's factor-price
conditions remain conditions from different problems until market clearing
produces \eqref{eq:axm-euler-reduced} and \eqref{eq:axm-costate-reduced}.

Some displayed conditions are equivalent representations rather than additional
restrictions. The dual research system implies the direct research first-order
conditions, the final-firm first-order conditions imply zero profit, and the
household budget becomes the resource constraint after market clearing. The
reduced system retains one independent representation of these conditions.

\subsubsection{Reduced dynamic system}

After imposing capital-market clearing and market clearing for AI production services, the independent
algebraic subsystem given \(K,A,B,N,q\) consists of labor
clearing; final production, shares, and prices; the service identity \(X=BU\);
inverse demand and the monopoly first-order condition; and the research price,
conditional demands, and scale first-order condition. This subsystem
characterizes a root on the selected interior branch for the candidate
quantities \(L,H,U,M,X,E,\) and \(Y\). It also determines the prices
\(r,w,p_X\), the research unit cost \(P_E\), and the shares \(s_X,s_M\).
The subsystem may admit multiple roots; I follow an interior branch that
satisfies the monopoly second-order condition and make no claim of global
uniqueness.

Combining the resource constraint, the reduced Euler and costate equations, the
capability law, and the two exogenous laws gives
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:axm-canonical-system}
\begin{align}
 \frac{\dot N}{N}&=n,\\
 \frac{\dot A}{A}&=\gamma_A,\\
 \frac{\dot K}{K}&=\frac{Y-C-U-M}{K}-\delta,\\
 \frac{\dot B}{B}&=\frac{\chi E^\eta}{B},\\
 \frac{\dot C}{C}&=n+\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\rho,\\
 \frac{\dot q}{q}
 &=\alpha\frac{Y}{K}-\delta-\frac{X}{qB^2}\notag\\
 &\quad-\eta s_M\frac{\dot B}{B}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\subsubsection{Initial and terminal conditions}

After capital-market clearing, \(K\) and \(B\) are predetermined, while the
paths of labor productivity and population are exogenous; \(C\) and \(q\) are
jump variables. Initial \(K,A,B,N\), market clearing, and the terminal
restrictions close the system. The
household and developer transversality conditions were stated separately in
\eqref{eq:axm-household-tvc} and \eqref{eq:axm-developer-tvc}. After imposing
capital-market clearing and substituting the final-good firm's capital-price
condition into the developer's discount factor, they can be collected in
equilibrium as
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-\rho t}\frac{N_tK_t}{C_t}=0,\qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t\left(\alpha\frac{Y_s}{K_s}-\delta\right)\dd s\right\}
 q_tB_t=0.
\label{eq:axm-tvc}
\end{equation}
The first restriction applies to household capital and the second to developer
capability. The display only rewrites the original agent conditions using
equilibrium quantities. A finite-horizon calculation cannot itself verify either
infinite-horizon limit.

The dated first-order, state, market, and accounting conditions are necessary for
a regular interior equilibrium. I maintain the transversality conditions as
terminal restrictions without claiming their universal necessity. The monopoly
second-order condition establishes only local optimality in \(X\), and I do not
establish global concavity of the developer's intertemporal problem for all
parameter values. Outside the sufficient cases stated next, a solution of the
displayed equations is therefore only a candidate path.

\begin{proposition}[Global sufficiency in the unit-elasticity cases]
\label{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency}
Let \(\sigma_{XL}=1\) and
\(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\leq1/2\). Suppose either
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=1,\qquad \eta(1+\omega_M)\leq1,
\]
or
\[
 \sigma_{HM}=2,\qquad 2\eta\leq1.
\]
Every regular interior equilibrium that satisfies the two stated transversality
conditions obeys the candidate system. Conversely, every admissible
infinite-horizon candidate path is an interior equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

The proof is in the Appendix. The benchmark satisfies these restrictions with
\(\beta=0.134\), \(\eta(1+\omega_M)=0.2700\), and \(2\eta=0.40\).
The full system is therefore globally sufficient for both unit-elasticity
research technologies under the stated conditions. The proposition does not turn a finite-horizon
approximation into a proof that an admissible infinite-horizon continuation
exists or satisfies the two transversality restrictions.
