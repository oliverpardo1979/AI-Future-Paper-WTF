\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-monopoly-existence}}

Fix \(K,A,L,B>0\), and let
\(R(X)=p_X(X;K,A,L)X\) denote service revenue. Write
\[
 u=\frac{1}{\sigma_{XL}},\qquad s=s_X,\qquad
 e=u(1-s)+\alpha s.
\]
The demand elasticity in \eqref{eq:axm-inverse-elasticity} and the CES-share
derivative imply
\[
 R'(X)=p_X(1-e),\qquad
 \frac{\dd s}{\dd\ln X}=(1-u)s(1-s).
\]
Let \(m(X)=R'(X)\). Wherever marginal revenue is positive, \(0<e<1\), and
direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{Xm'(X)}{p_X}
 =e(1-e)+(\alpha-u)(1-u)s(1-s)\equiv D(s).
 \label{eq:axm-monopoly-proof-curvature}
\end{equation}
I next show that \(D(s)>0\). If
\((\alpha-u)(1-u)\geq0\), the result follows immediately. If this product is
negative, the maintained restrictions imply \(0<\alpha<u<1\). In that case,
\begin{align*}
 D(s)={}&u(1-u)(1-s)^2
 +[u^2+2\alpha-3\alpha u]s(1-s)
 +\alpha(1-\alpha)s^2.
\end{align*}
The middle coefficient is positive. For \(u\leq2/3\), this follows from
\(u^2+\alpha(2-3u)>0\). For \(u>2/3\), the facts that \(\alpha<u\) and
\(2-3u<0\) imply
\[
 u^2+\alpha(2-3u)>u^2+u(2-3u)=2u(1-u)>0.
\]
Thus all three terms are positive for \(s\in(0,1)\), and marginal revenue is
strictly decreasing whenever it is positive.

The CES asymptotics complete the argument. As \(X\downarrow0\), revenue is
proportional to \(X^{1-u}\) when \(\sigma_{XL}>1\), to \(X^\beta\) when
\(\sigma_{XL}=1\), and to \(X^{1-\alpha}\) when \(\sigma_{XL}<1\). Each
exponent lies strictly between zero and one, so \(m(X)\to\infty\). When
\(X\to\infty\), marginal revenue converges to zero from above if
\(\sigma_{XL}\geq1\); if \(\sigma_{XL}<1\), it eventually becomes negative.
Therefore the equation \(m(X)=1/B\) has exactly one solution. Operating profit
increases before this solution and decreases after it. Moreover, its linear
resource cost dominates revenue as \(X\to\infty\), so the solution is the unique
global maximum. Equation \eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-proof-curvature} also establishes
the strict second-order condition. The implicit-function theorem implies that
the optimizer is continuously differentiable in \((K,A,L,B)\).

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-local-canonical-existence}}

Proposition \ref{prop:axm-monopoly-existence} and the implicit-function theorem
give a unique continuously differentiable service-supply function
\[
 X=\mathcal X(K,A,L,B)>0.
\]
In the benchmark, \(L=N\), \(U=X/B\), and the research first-order condition
gives the explicit function
\[
 M=(q\chi\eta B^\eta)^{1/(1-\eta)}>0.
\]
Final production, shares, prices, and profit are therefore continuously
differentiable functions of \((K,A,B,N,q)\) on the positive orthant. Substituting
these functions into \eqref{eq:axm-canonical-system-benchmark}, with \(A\) and
\(N\) given by their displayed exogenous paths, produces an ordinary
differential equation for the endogenous vector \((K,B,C,q)\). Its right-hand
side is continuously differentiable, and hence locally Lipschitz, on the
positive orthant. The Picard--Lindel\"of theorem gives a unique local solution
for every positive \((K_0,B_0,C_0,q_0)\) and every positive exogenous
\((A_0,N_0)\). The standard continuation theorem
extends this solution uniquely until it reaches the boundary of the positive
domain or becomes unbounded. Neither theorem selects \((C_0,q_0)\) or proves
the transversality conditions.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-finite-cap-invariance}}

Let \(D=\overline B-B\). Equation
\eqref{eq:axm-capability-law-benchmark} gives
\[
 \frac{\dot D}{D}=-\frac{\chi}{\overline B}(BM)^\eta.
\]
Integration gives \eqref{eq:axm-cap-gap-solution}. Local integrability of
research services makes the exponent finite at every finite date, so
\(D_t>0\) and the state cannot cross the frontier.

For the finite-horizon existence result, first optimize pointwise over \(X\).
Operating profit is continuous in \(B\). Compactness of
\([B_0,\overline B]\) and regularity of the exogenous paths therefore give a
finite uniform profit bound on \([0,T]\). The discount factor is also bounded
above and away from zero. Hence, for constants \(C_0<\infty\) and
\(d_0>0\),
\[
 J_T(M)\leq C_0-d_0\int_0^T M_t\dd t.
\]
The truncated value is finite, and every maximizing sequence has bounded total
research expenditure.

Set \(v=M^\eta\) and \(p=1/\eta>1\). The control cost is
\(M=v^p\), while the state equation is affine in \(v\):
\[
 \dot B=\chi B^\eta\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right)v.
\]
A maximizing sequence is bounded in the reflexive space \(L^p[0,T]\), so it
has a weakly convergent subsequence. The associated state paths are uniformly
bounded and equicontinuous; H\"older's inequality supplies a common modulus of
continuity. A further subsequence converges uniformly. Weak convergence of
\(v\), uniform convergence of \(B\), and the integral form of the state
equation show that the limit pair is feasible. The operating-profit integral
is continuous under uniform state convergence, while \(\int v^p\) is weakly
lower semicontinuous. The objective is therefore weakly upper semicontinuous
and attains its maximum. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-path-specific-sufficiency}}

Fix a date and the paths that the developer takes as given. Differentiating
the logarithm of \eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-foc} with respect to \(\ln B\) gives
\eqref{eq:axm-service-capability-elasticity}. The denominator is positive by
the strict pointwise second-order condition
\eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}. The envelope theorem gives
\(\pi_B=X^*/B^2\), so differentiation with respect to \(B\) gives
\eqref{eq:axm-operating-profit-curvature}. Condition
\eqref{eq:axm-path-specific-profit-concavity} therefore makes
\(\pi_t(B)\) concave over the full state domain reachable from \(B_0\).
Capability cannot fall because every research technology in the model has
\(\dot B\geq0\).

In the automated-research benchmark,
\(F(B,M)=\chi B^\eta M^\eta\) is jointly concave when \(2\eta\leq1\). With
human research and \(\sigma_{HM}=1\),
\[
 F(B,H,M)=\chi H^{\eta\omega_H}
 B^{\eta\omega_M}M^{\eta\omega_M}
\]
is jointly concave when \(\eta(1+\omega_M)\leq1\). With
\(\sigma_{HM}=2\),
\[
 F(B,H,M)=\chi\left[\omega_H\sqrt H+
 \omega_M\sqrt{BM}\right]^{2\eta}
\]
is jointly concave when \(2\eta\leq1\): the geometric mean
\(\sqrt{BM}\) is concave, and the outer power is increasing and concave.

Let
\[
 \mathcal D_t=\exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\},
 \qquad \mu_t=\mathcal D_tq_t.
\]
Optimized operating profit is concave in \(B\), research cost is linear in
the controls, and the relevant capability law is jointly concave. Since
\(q_t>0\), the developer's present-value Hamiltonian is therefore jointly
concave in the state and controls. The candidate's first-order and costate
conditions maximize that Hamiltonian globally. For any admissible alternative
with the same initial capability, the standard concavity inequality gives
\[
 J_T^*-\widetilde J_T
 \geq \mu_T(\widetilde B_T-B_T^*)
 \geq-\mu_TB_T^*.
\]
The developer's transversality condition makes the final term converge to
zero. Taking the lower limit as \(T\to\infty\) establishes global developer
optimality because both improper objective integrals are finite by
admissibility. Household optimality follows from concave log utility, the
linear budget, its Euler equation, and its transversality condition.
Competitive final-good firms face a concave constant-returns technology, and
Proposition \ref{prop:axm-monopoly-existence} establishes the pointwise global
choice of \(X\). The remaining dated conditions and market clearing therefore
establish the relevant equilibrium. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-capped-developer-sufficiency}}

The transformation in \eqref{eq:axm-research-depth} has
\[
 B'(R_B)=\psi_{\overline B}(B)>0,
 \qquad
 B''(R_B)=-\frac{\psi_{\overline B}(B)}{\overline B}<0.
\]
Using \(\pi_B=X/B^2\) and
\eqref{eq:axm-operating-profit-curvature} gives
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-profit-curvature}. Condition
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-concavity-gate} therefore makes optimized operating profit
concave in research depth over the complete reachable state domain.

When \(2\eta\leq1\), the monomial \((BM)^\eta\) is jointly concave and
increasing in \((B,M)\). Because \(B(R_B)\) is increasing and concave,
\(\chi[B(R_B)M]^\eta\) is jointly concave in \((R_B,M)\). Research cost remains
linear. The present-value Hamiltonian written in research depth is therefore
jointly concave, and the transformed first-order and costate equations maximize
it globally.

The shadow value of research depth is
\(q_R=qB'(R_B)=q\psi_{\overline B}(B)\). Moreover,
\[
 \psi_{\overline B}(B)R_B
 =-(\overline B-B)\log\left(1-\frac{B}{\overline B}\right)
 \leq B.
\]
Thus the maintained developer transversality condition implies the terminal
condition required by the concavity argument in \(R_B\). Applying the same
finite-horizon inequality and limiting argument as in the proof of Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-path-specific-sufficiency} establishes global developer
optimality. The remaining conditions in Definition
\ref{def:axm-benchmark-equilibrium} then establish equilibrium. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency}}

Under the usual normality and differentiability conditions, every regular
interior equilibrium satisfies the dated first-order conditions, state equations,
and market-clearing identities. The proposition conditions the converse on the
two displayed transversality restrictions; it does not claim that those limits
are necessary for every conceivable infinite-horizon optimum. I prove the stated
global sufficiency.

With \(\sigma_{XL}=1\), define
\[
 \beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X,
 \qquad G_t=K_t^\alpha(A_tL_t)^{(1-\alpha)\omega_L}.
\]
Conditional on the paths that the developer takes as given, final output is
\(Y=G_tX^\beta\). The final-good firm's AI-demand condition gives
\(p_XX=\beta Y\), so developer revenue is \(\beta G_tX^\beta\). Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-monopoly-existence} gives
\[
 X^*=(\beta^2G_tB)^{1/(1-\beta)}
\]
and optimized operating profit
\[
 \pi_t(B)=\beta(1-\beta)G_t
 (\beta^2G_tB)^{\beta/(1-\beta)}.
\]
This function is concave in capability \(B\) when \(\beta\leq1/2\).

In the automated-research benchmark, the capability law is
\[
 F(B,M)=\chi B^\eta M^\eta.
\]
It is jointly concave in \((B,M)\) when \(2\eta\leq1\). In the human-research
extension with \(\sigma_{HM}=1\), the law is
\[
 F(B,H,M)=\chi H^{\eta\omega_H}
 B^{\eta\omega_M}M^{\eta\omega_M}.
\]
It is jointly concave in \((B,H,M)\) when the sum of its exponents,
\(\eta(1+\omega_M)\), does not exceed one. With \(\sigma_{HM}=2\),
\[
 F(B,H,M)=\chi\left[\omega_H\sqrt H+
 \omega_M\sqrt{BM}\right]^{2\eta}.
\]
Both \(\sqrt H\) and the geometric mean \(\sqrt{BM}\) are concave. Their
weighted sum is concave, and composition with the increasing concave map
\(v\mapsto v^{2\eta}\) preserves concavity when \(2\eta\leq1\).

Let
\[
 \mathcal D_t=\exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\},
 \qquad \mu_t=\mathcal D_tq_t.
\]
For the extension, define \(\mathcal C_t(H,M)=w_tH+M\); for the benchmark,
define \(\mathcal C_t(M)=M\). The present-value Hamiltonian of the corresponding
reduced developer problem is
\[
 \mathcal H_t^p
 =\mathcal D_t[\pi_t(B)-\mathcal C_t]+\mu_tF.
\]
The research-compute first-order condition implies \(q>0\), so this Hamiltonian
is concave in the state and relevant controls in each case covered by the
proposition. The interior first-order conditions consequently maximize the
Hamiltonian globally. Concavity and the costate equation imply, for any
admissible alternative \((\widetilde B,\widetilde H,\widetilde M)\), with
\(\widetilde H\) omitted in the benchmark and with the same initial capability,
that
\[
 J_T^*-\widetilde J_T
 \geq \mu_T(\widetilde B_T-B_T^*),
\]
where \(J_T\) denotes the developer's objective truncated at date \(T\).
Because \(\widetilde B_T\geq0\), \(\mu_T\geq0\), and the developer's
transversality condition gives \(\mu_TB_T^*\to0\),
\[
 \mu_T(\widetilde B_T-B_T^*)\geq-\mu_TB_T^*\to0.
\]
Because both improper objective integrals converge to finite real values,
taking the lower limit as \(T\to\infty\) establishes global optimality.

The household problem has a concave objective and a linear flow budget.
Let its present-value shadow price be
\(\Lambda_t=e^{-\rho t}N_t/C_t\). The price-form Euler equation
\eqref{eq:axm-euler} is the household costate equation. Concavity and the
consumption FOC imply, for any admissible alternative \(\widetilde K\) with the
same initial capital holdings, where \(\mathcal U_T\) denotes
household utility truncated at date \(T\),
\[
 \mathcal U_T^*-\widetilde{\mathcal U}_T
 \geq \Lambda_T(\widetilde K_T-K_T^*)
 \geq-\Lambda_TK_T^*\to0,
\]
where the last limit is the household transversality condition. In equilibrium,
capital-market clearing identifies household capital holdings with the capital
rented by final-good firms. Convergence of
the improper utilities then establishes household optimality. Competitive
final-good firms face a concave constant-returns technology, so their factor
demands are globally optimal. Market clearing then establishes an interior
equilibrium. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-unit-saddle-path}}

I first derive the Jacobian from the dated equilibrium system. Let
\begin{equation}
 a_K=\frac{\alpha}{1-\beta},\qquad
 a_B=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta},\qquad
 R^*=r^*+\delta,
 \qquad
 \psi^*=\frac{U^*}{q^*B^*}
 =\rho-n+\eta g_Y^*.
 \label{eq:axm-saddle-jacobian-definitions}
\end{equation}
For the ordering
\(\xi=(\xi_K,\xi_B,\xi_C,\xi_q)'\), let \(e_K,e_B,e_C,e_q\)
denote the corresponding unit row vectors and define
\begin{align}
 y_\xi&=(a_K,a_B,0,0),\notag\\
 m_\xi&=\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}e_B
       +\frac{1}{1-\eta}e_q,\notag\\
 b_\xi&=g_B^*\left[(\eta-1)e_B+\eta m_\xi\right].
 \label{eq:axm-saddle-linear-blocks}
\end{align}
These expressions follow respectively from
\begin{equation}
 \widehat Y=a_K\xi_K+a_B\xi_B,\qquad
 \widehat M=\frac{\xi_q+\eta\xi_B}{1-\eta},\qquad
 \widehat g_B=b_\xi\xi,
 \label{eq:axm-saddle-log-linear-identities}
\end{equation}
where a hat denotes a log deviation, except that
\(\widehat g_B\) denotes the level deviation of the capability growth rate.
Using the output shares in
\eqref{eq:axm-benchmark-existence-shares}, the four rows of the Jacobian are
\begin{align}
 J_K^*&=\frac{1-u^*}{k^*}y_\xi
 -(g_Y^*+\delta)e_K-\frac{c^*}{k^*}e_C
 -\frac{m^*}{k^*}m_\xi,\notag\\
 J_B^*&=b_\xi,\notag\\
 J_C^*&=R^*(y_\xi-e_K),\notag\\
 J_q^*&=R^*(y_\xi-e_K)
 -\psi^*(y_\xi-e_q-e_B)-\eta b_\xi.
 \label{eq:axm-saddle-jacobian}
\end{align}
The four terms have direct origins: capital accumulation, capability
accumulation, the Euler equation, and the developer's costate equation. No
terminal condition has been used to construct \(J^*\).

Under the baseline calibration, the balanced-growth values are
\begin{align*}
 &g_Y^*=0.0138667,\quad g_B^*=0.0034667,\quad r^*=0.0508667,
 \quad u^*=0.017956,\\
 &k^*=3.271646,\quad m^*=0.0003130,\quad c^*=0.772782,
 \quad \psi^*=0.0397733.
\end{align*}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:axm-saddle-jacobian} gives
\begin{equation}
 J^*=\begin{pmatrix}
  0.0505161& 0.0464224&-0.2362059&-0.0001196\\
  0        &-0.0026000& 0        & 0.0008667\\
 -0.0624302& 0.0156075& 0        & 0\\
 -0.0775863& 0.0497466& 0        & 0.0396000
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:axm-calibrated-saddle-jacobian}
\end{equation}
Its characteristic roots are those reported in
\eqref{eq:axm-calibrated-jacobian-roots}. Hence the steady point of the
normalized system is hyperbolic and its stable manifold has dimension two. If
the two stable eigenvectors are normalized to Euclidean length one, their
\((\xi_K,\xi_B)\) block is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0.763724&0.207707\\
 -0.003858&0.789358
 \end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is \(0.6037\). The projection of the local stable manifold
onto the two predetermined variables is therefore locally invertible.

By Proposition \ref{prop:axm-local-canonical-existence}, the exact unit-elastic
conditions give a continuously differentiable system
\(\dot\xi=f(\xi)\) near \(\xi=0\), with \(f(0)=0\) and \(Df(0)=J^*\).
Every nonzero orbit reconstructs the full dated candidate system without
imposing constant growth rates or constant allocation shares.

Because \(J^*\) is hyperbolic, the stable-manifold theorem gives a
two-dimensional continuously differentiable manifold \(\mathcal W^s\) of
initial conditions whose paths remain near \(\xi=0\) and converge to it
exponentially. The derivative of the projection from \(\mathcal W^s\) onto
\((\xi_K,\xi_B)\) is \(V_{KB}\). Since \(\det(V_{KB})\ne0\), the
inverse-function theorem makes \(\mathcal W^s\) locally the graph of a unique
continuously differentiable function from \((K_0,B_0)\) to \((C_0,q_0)\).

For every pair of predetermined stocks in the resulting neighborhood, this
graph selects a unique nearby solution of the exact dated candidate system.
When the initial stocks differ from their reference values, \(\xi(0)\ne0\), so
this solution is an equilibrium transition rather than the balanced-growth
trajectory. Shrinking the neighborhood if necessary preserves strict
positivity and all dated interior conditions.

Exponential convergence makes the deviations of \(r\) from \(r^*\)
integrable. Each transversality expression therefore equals its
balanced-growth counterpart, which declines at rate \(\rho-n>0\), times a
positive factor converging to a finite limit. Both transversality conditions
hold. Proposition \ref{prop:axm-equilibrium-sufficiency} then establishes
household and developer optimality and turns each selected candidate path into
an equilibrium trajectory. This proves local existence and uniqueness among
equilibrium trajectories that converge to the reference path. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-near-unit-finite-window}}

The representation in \eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-ces} has a removable singularity
at \(\varphi=0\). To verify smoothness, expand
\[
 e^{\varphi d}=1+\varphi d+\frac{\varphi^2d^2}{2}
 +O(\varphi^3d^3)
\]
and then expand the logarithm of
\(\omega_L+\omega_Xe^{\varphi d}\). Because
\(\omega_L+\omega_X=1\), division by \(\varphi\) yields
\eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-ces-expansion}. Differentiation with respect to
\(\ln X\) yields \eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-share-expansion}. Thus the production
block is continuously differentiable in quantities and \(\sigma_{XL}\) on
every strictly interior compact set around \(\sigma_{XL}=1\).

Proposition \ref{prop:axm-monopoly-existence} gives a strict second-order
condition for the service choice. Its optimizer is therefore continuously
differentiable in the dated variables and \(\sigma_{XL}\) near one. After
substitution of the static conditions, write the four dynamic equations as
\[
 \dot y=f(t,y,\sigma_{XL}),
 \qquad y=(\ln K,\ln B,\ln C,\ln q).
\]
Let \(P_Sy(0)=(\ln K_0,\ln B_0)\) impose the two initial-state conditions and
let \(\Psi_T(y(T),\sigma_{XL})=0\) collect the two stated terminal
restrictions. The boundary-value operator is
\[
 \mathcal F_T(y,\sigma_{XL})=
 \left(\dot y-f(t,y,\sigma_{XL}),
 P_Sy(0)-(\ln K_0,\ln B_0),
 \Psi_T(y(T),\sigma_{XL})\right).
\]
It maps a neighborhood in \(C^1([0,T],\mathbb R^4)\) into
\(C^0([0,T],\mathbb R^4)\times\mathbb R^4\) and is continuously
differentiable. By assumption, its derivative with respect to \(y\) at the
unit-elastic solution is invertible. The implicit-function theorem on Banach
spaces therefore gives a unique continuously differentiable branch
\(y^{\sigma_{XL}}\) for \(\sigma_{XL}\) sufficiently close to one.
Continuous differentiability of this branch gives
\eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-finite-window-bound}, after shrinking the parameter
neighborhood if necessary. Strict interiority is preserved by continuity.
The construction says nothing about dates beyond \(T\), which is why no
infinite-horizon conclusion follows. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-near-unit-separation-scale}}

Because \(\varphi\) is constant and \(d=\ln X-\ln A-\ln L\), direct
differentiation gives \eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-slow-law}. Along the unit-elastic
balanced path,
\[
 g_X^*-(n+\gamma_A)=g_Y^*+g_B^*-(n+\gamma_A).
\]
Using \(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\),
\(\lambda=(1-\alpha)\omega_L\), and
\(\omega_L+\omega_X=1\) in
\eqref{eq:axm-benchmark-existence-growth} reduces this expression to
\[
 \nu=\frac{\eta(n+\gamma_A)}{\omega_L-\eta}.
\]
The denominator is positive because \(\Delta>0\).

Now suppose that, until the departure threshold is reached,
\(0<a_-\leq g_X-(n+\gamma_A)\leq a_+<\infty\). Integrating the exact law gives
\[
 h(t)=\varphi\left[d(0)+\int_0^t(g_X(s)-(n+\gamma_A))\dd s\right].
\]
For a fixed threshold \(\bar h>0\) and sufficiently small nonzero
\(\varphi\), the first date at which \(h\) reaches
\(\operatorname{sign}(\varphi)\bar h\) satisfies
\[
 \frac{\bar h/|\varphi|-d(0)}{a_+}
 \leq T_{\varphi}(\bar h)\leq
 \frac{\bar h/|\varphi|-d(0)}{a_-}.
\]
Boundedness of \(d(0)\) and
\(|\varphi|=|\sigma_{XL}-1|/\sigma_{XL}\) prove the stated
\(1/|\sigma_{XL}-1|\) order. Taking the local benchmark value \(\nu\) for the
relative-input growth rate and an order-one threshold gives
\eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-separation-time}. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-complements}}

Write \(\mathcal L=AL\), \(x=X/\mathcal L\), and first use the final-good
firm's AI-demand condition.
Let
\[
 z(x)=\left[\omega_L+\omega_Xx^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 \right]^{\sigma_{XL}/(\sigma_{XL}-1)}.
\]
Then \(Y=K^\alpha\mathcal L^{1-\alpha}z(x)^{1-\alpha}\) and that demand condition gives
\[
 p_X=(K/\mathcal L)^\alpha\widetilde p(x),\qquad
 \widetilde p(x)=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{z(x)^{1-\alpha}}{x}.
\]
Substitution into the monopolist's markup condition, followed by normalization
by \((K/\mathcal L)^\alpha\), gives
\[
 \widetilde p(x)
 \left[1-\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}-\alpha s_X\right]
 =\frac{1}{B(K/\mathcal L)^\alpha}.
\]
The right-hand side tends to zero by hypothesis. The assumption that
\(p_X/(K/\mathcal L)^\alpha=\widetilde p(x)\) remains bounded away from zero therefore
implies that the term in square brackets converges to zero. Solving gives
\[
 \bar s_X=\frac{1-\sigma_{XL}}{1-\alpha\sigma_{XL}}.
\]
For \(\sigma_{XL}<1\), this number is strictly between zero and one. The CES share
mapping \eqref{eq:axm-sx} is one-to-one in \(X/\mathcal L\), so \(X/\mathcal L\) converges to a
finite positive constant. Inverting that mapping gives exactly \(\bar x\) in
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-ratios}; substituting \(\bar x\) into the CES gives
\(Z/\mathcal L\to\bar z\). Hence \(Y\) is asymptotically a positive constant multiple
of \(K^\alpha\mathcal L^{1-\alpha}\).

Now suppose, as in the proposition, that a finite-rate balanced path exists.
The household Euler equation makes the net interest rate finite when consumption
growth is finite. The final-good firm's capital-demand condition then requires
\(Y/K=(r+\delta)/\alpha\) to have a finite limit. This limit cannot be zero.
Because \(Z/\mathcal L\) has a positive constant limit,
\(Y/K\to0\) would require \(K/\mathcal L\to\infty\); then gross investment divided by
output, \((g_K+\delta)K/Y\), would diverge. To see why, balanced growth makes
both \(g_K\) and \(g_{\mathcal L}\) converge; because
\(K/\mathcal L\to\infty\) and \(g_{\mathcal L}\to n+\gamma_A\),
the limiting capital-growth rate cannot be below \(n+\gamma_A>0\). This violates the
resource constraint. Hence
\(Y/K\) converges to a positive constant.
Because \(Z/\mathcal L\) also converges to a positive constant, final production implies
that \(K/\mathcal L\) converges to a constant. Since \(L/N\) has a positive
constant limit, \(\mathcal L,K,Y,X,\) and \(C\) grow at
\(n+\gamma_A\). The labor-payment identity gives
\(g_w=(n+\gamma_A)-n=\gamma_A\). The household Euler equation then gives
\(r\to\rho+\gamma_A\), and the final-good firm's capital-demand condition gives
\(K/Y\to\alpha/(\rho+\gamma_A+\delta)\).
Finally, the final firm's labor payment satisfies
\(wL/Y=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\), which converges to the strictly positive value in
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-factor-limits}. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-complements-research}}

Proposition \ref{prop:axm-complements} implies that
\(g_w\to\gamma_A\), \(g_X\to n+\gamma_A\), and
\(r\to\rho+\gamma_A\). Therefore \(Bw\to\infty\).
Because \(X=BU\),
\begin{equation}
 g_U\to n+\gamma_A-\bar g_B.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-gu}
\end{equation}
The capability law and the regular convergence of \(g_B\) imply
\begin{equation}
 g_E\to\frac{\bar g_B}{\eta}.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-ge}
\end{equation}
Define the nonnegative marginal-benefit term
\begin{equation}
 d_t\equiv\frac{X_t}{q_tB_t^2}=\frac{U_t}{q_tB_t}.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-d}
\end{equation}
The costate equation gives
\(d_t=r_t-\eta s_{M,t}g_{B,t}-g_{q,t}\). The maintained convergence of the
growth rates and the limit of \(s_M\) derived below therefore imply that \(d_t\)
has a finite nonnegative limit. I now show, rather than assume, that this limit
is strictly positive.

First consider \(\sigma_{HM}=1\). Interior cost minimization gives
\(s_M=\omega_M\) and \(M/(wH)=\omega_M/\omega_H\). Hence
\(g_M=g_H+\gamma_A\). Log differentiation of the
Cobb--Douglas research index, combined with
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-ge}, yields
\begin{equation}
 g_M=(\eta^{-1}-\omega_M)\bar g_B+\omega_H\gamma_A,
 \qquad g_H=g_M-\gamma_A.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-cd-gm}
\end{equation}
The research-compute first-order condition is
\begin{equation}
 M=q\eta\omega_M g_B B.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-cd-mfoc}
\end{equation}
Regular convergence of \(g_B\) then gives
\(g_q=g_M-\bar g_B=(\eta^{-1}-\omega_M-1)\bar g_B\).

Suppose for contradiction that \(d_t\to0\). The costate equation would imply
that the growth rate of \(qB\), which equals \(g_M\) by
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-cd-mfoc}, exceeds the discount rate
\(r\to\rho+\gamma_A\) by \((1-\eta\omega_M)\bar g_B>0\).
The developer's terminal object would therefore grow rather than
converge to zero, contradicting its transversality condition. Thus
\(d_t\to\bar d>0\). Its log growth rate has a limit, and convergence to a
positive constant makes that limiting rate zero. The identity in
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-d} therefore requires
\(g_U=g_q+\bar g_B=g_M\). Combining this equality with
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-gu} and
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-cd-gm} gives
\[
 \bar g_B=\frac{n+\omega_M\gamma_A}
 {1+\eta^{-1}-\omega_M}.
\]
The remaining rates in \eqref{eq:axm-complement-cd-research-growth} follow
directly. In particular, \(g_{BM}=\bar g_B+g_M=n+\gamma_A\).

Now let \(\sigma_{HM}>1\). Since \(Bw\to\infty\), Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-research-automation} implies \(s_M\to1\). The exact share identity
\[
 g_E=(1-s_M)g_H+s_M(g_B+g_M)
\]
and the finite limiting growth rate of \(H\) imply, together with
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-ge}, that
\begin{equation}
 g_M=(\eta^{-1}-1)\bar g_B.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-ces-gm}
\end{equation}
The research-compute first-order condition can be written
\begin{equation}
 M=q\eta s_M g_B B.
 \label{eq:axm-proof-complement-ces-mfoc}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:axm-automation-odds} implies that the log growth rate of
\(s_M\) tends to zero. Hence
\(g_q=(\eta^{-1}-2)\bar g_B\).

If \(d_t\to0\), the costate equation would make the growth rate of \(qB\)
exceed \(r\) by \((1-\eta)\bar g_B>0\). Equation
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-ces-mfoc} would again make the developer's
terminal object grow, contradicting transversality. Therefore
\(d_t\to\bar d>0\). Its limiting log growth rate is zero, so
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-d} again requires \(g_U=g_M\). Equations
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-gu} and
\eqref{eq:axm-proof-complement-ces-gm} then give
\(\bar g_B=\eta(n+\gamma_A)\) and
\(g_M=(1-\eta)(n+\gamma_A)\). Finally, log differentiation of
\eqref{eq:axm-input-ratio}, using \(g_w\to\gamma_A\), gives
\[
 g_H=n+\gamma_A-\sigma_{HM}\bigl(\eta(n+\gamma_A)+\gamma_A\bigr).
\]
This proves all rates in \eqref{eq:axm-complement-ces-research-growth}.

In both cases, \(g_M=g_U<n+\gamma_A=g_Y\), so \(M/Y\to0\) and \(U/Y\to0\). Also
\(g_H<n=g_N\), so \(H/N\to0\), and labor clearing gives \(L/N\to1\).
Substitution in the resource constraint, together with
\(K/Y\to\alpha/(\rho+\gamma_A+\delta)\), gives the consumption share in
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-vanishing-shares}. Given the positive limiting ratios,
the maintained household TVC requires \(n-\rho<0\). Since the derived rate of
research compute satisfies \(g_M<n+\gamma_A\), the developer's terminal object has
limiting log growth \(g_M-(\rho+\gamma_A)<0\). The derived rates therefore reproduce both
maintained terminal restrictions. This is a consistency check; the developer's
TVC was used above to select the positive-\(d\) branch. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-complement-local-existence}}

Write \(\mathcal L=AN\), \(G=n+\gamma_A\), and
\(x=X/\mathcal L\). For a given \((k,b,\tau)\), the static monopoly condition is
\begin{equation}
 (1-\alpha)s_X(x)[1-e_X(x)]\frac{y(k,x)}{x}
 =\frac{\tau}{b},
 \qquad
 y(k,x)=k^\alpha
 \left[\omega_L+\omega_Xx^{(\sigma_{XL}-1)/\sigma_{XL}}
 \right]^{(1-\alpha)\sigma_{XL}/(\sigma_{XL}-1)}.
 \label{eq:axm-app-complement-static-map}
\end{equation}
At \(\tau=0\), its unique positive solution is \(x=\bar x\), where
\(e_X=1\) and
\(s_X=(1-\sigma_{XL})/(1-\alpha\sigma_{XL})\). Moreover,
\(\partial e_X/\partial x>0\) when \(\sigma_{XL}<1\). The derivative of the
left-hand side of \eqref{eq:axm-app-complement-static-map} at
\((\bar x,0)\) is therefore strictly negative. The implicit-function theorem
gives a unique continuously differentiable map
\(x=x(k,b,\tau)\) in a neighborhood of the limiting point.

Define
\[
 \widetilde u=\frac{x}{b},\qquad
 \widetilde m=
 \left(\widetilde q\chi\eta b^\eta\right)^{1/(1-\eta)}.
\]
The levels of inference and research compute satisfy
\(U/\mathcal L=\tau\widetilde u\) and
\(M/\mathcal L=\tau\widetilde m\). Substitution in the dated equilibrium
conditions gives the autonomous system
\begin{align}
 \dot k&=y-c-\tau(\widetilde u+\widetilde m)
 -(\delta+G)k,\notag\\
 \frac{\dot b}{b}&=\chi b^{\eta-1}\widetilde m^\eta-\eta G,\notag\\
 \frac{\dot c}{c}&=\alpha\frac{y}{k}-\delta-\rho-G,\notag\\
 \frac{\dot{\widetilde q}}{\widetilde q}
 &=\alpha\frac{y}{k}-\delta
 -\frac{x}{\widetilde q b^2}
 -\eta\chi b^{\eta-1}\widetilde m^\eta-(1-2\eta)G,\notag\\
 \dot\tau&=-\eta G\tau.
 \label{eq:axm-app-complement-autonomous-system}
\end{align}
Direct substitution verifies that
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-local-fixed-point}, together with \(\bar\tau=0\),
is a stationary point of this system. Positivity of \(\bar c\) follows from
\(\rho>n\), and \(\mathcal D_q>0\) follows immediately from the same
restriction.

Use log deviations for the four positive variables. At the stationary point,
the Jacobian block for capital and consumption is
\begin{equation}
 J_K=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \rho-n&-[\mathcal R-\delta-G]\\
  -(1-\alpha)(\rho+\gamma_A+\delta)&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:axm-app-complement-capital-block}
\end{equation}
Its determinant is strictly negative. To write the
capability--shadow-value block, let
\[
 a_\eta=\frac{2\eta-1}{1-\eta},\qquad
 h_\eta=\frac{\eta}{1-\eta}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 J_B=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  \eta G a_\eta&\eta G h_\eta\\
  2\mathcal D_q-\eta^2G a_\eta&
  \mathcal D_q-\eta^2G h_\eta
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det J_B=-\frac{\eta G\mathcal D_q}{1-\eta}<0.
 \label{eq:axm-app-complement-capability-block}
\end{equation}
Each two-dimensional block therefore has one negative and one positive root.
The remaining root, associated with \(\tau\), is \(-\eta G<0\). The full
Jacobian is block triangular up to terms running from the capital block and
\(\tau\) into the other equations. It consequently has three stable roots and
two unstable roots. Under the stated pairwise-distinctness condition, the projection of
the stable eigenspace onto the three predetermined variables
\((k,b,\tau)\) is nonsingular: the negative root of \(J_K\) has a nonzero
\(k\) component, the negative root of \(J_B\) has a nonzero \(b\) component,
and the \(-\eta G\) eigenvector has a nonzero \(\tau\) component.

The stable-manifold theorem now gives a three-dimensional local stable
manifold. The nonsingular projection and the inverse-function theorem express
that manifold uniquely as positive initial jumps
\((c_0,\widetilde q_0)\) for each nearby predetermined triple
\((k_0,b_0,\tau_0)\). The resulting path is defined for every finite date,
remains interior, and converges as stated in
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-local-convergence}. This constructs a transition; it
does not impose balanced growth away from the limiting point.

It remains to verify equilibrium rather than only the canonical equations.
For \(\sigma_{XL}<1\), the service--capability elasticity in
\eqref{eq:axm-service-capability-elasticity} converges to zero as capability
increases. Shrinking the neighborhood makes \(k\) uniformly bounded away from
zero and, because \(B=b/\tau\), makes initial capability sufficiently large.
Continuity then makes the elasticity no larger than two for every date and
every alternative capability \(B\geq B_0\). Together with \(2\eta\leq1\),
Proposition \ref{prop:axm-path-specific-sufficiency} establishes global
developer optimality.

Finally, the household terminal object has asymptotic log growth \(n-\rho<0\).
The developer's terminal object has asymptotic log growth
\[
 (1-\eta)G-(\rho+\gamma_A)
 =-[\rho-n+\eta G]<0.
\]
Operating profit grows at rate \(G\), research expenditure grows at rate
\((1-\eta)G\), and the discount rate converges to \(\rho+\gamma_A\), so both
improper objectives are finite. The dated conditions, the two transversality
conditions, agent optimality, and market clearing therefore satisfy the
benchmark equilibrium definition. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-cd-bgp}}

With \(\sigma_{XL}=1\), the final technology is
\[
 Y=K^\alpha(AL)^\lambda X^\beta.
\]
The final-good firm's AI-demand condition gives \(p_XX=\beta Y\). The inverse
demand elasticity is \(1-\beta\), so the monopoly condition is
\(\beta p_X=1/B\). Combining the two equations with \(X=BU\) gives
\(U/Y=\beta^2\), and substitution gives \eqref{eq:axm-reduced-cd}. Constant
\(K/Y\) and \(L/N\) then imply
\[
 g_Y=n+\gamma_A+\nu g_B.
\]

The wage satisfies \(w=\lambda Y/L\), so
\(g_w=\gamma_A+\nu g_B\). Consequently, \(Bw\to\infty\) when
\(\sigma_{HM}>1\), and Proposition \ref{prop:axm-research-automation} gives
\(s_M\to1\). A positive constant research-expenditure share makes \(M/Y\)
constant, so \(g_M=g_Y\). If \(\sigma_{HM}=1\), constant \(wH/Y\) gives
\(g_H=n\), and
\[
 g_E=\omega_Hn+\omega_M(g_B+g_M)
 =n+\omega_M\gamma_A+\omega_M(1+\nu)g_B.
\]
If \(\sigma_{HM}>1\), \(E\) is asymptotically proportional to \(BM\), and
\[
 g_E=n+\gamma_A+(1+\nu)g_B.
\]
Both cases can be written
\[
 g_E=n+\bar s\gamma_A+\bar s(1+\nu)g_B.
\]
Because \(\dot B=\chi E^\eta=g_BB\) and \(g_B\) converges regularly,
equating growth rates gives
\[
 g_B=\eta[n+\bar s\gamma_A+\bar s(1+\nu)g_B].
\]
Solving establishes \eqref{eq:axm-cd-growth-rates}; positivity requires
\(D(\bar s)>0\).

The constant inference share gives \(g_U=g_Y\), \(g_X=g_B+g_Y\), and
\(g_{p_X}=-g_B\). The research-compute condition makes \(qB/Y\) constant and
therefore gives \(g_q=g_Y-g_B\). The capability law gives
\(g_E=g_B/\eta\), while \(g_L=n\). When \(\sigma_{HM}>1\), the input-ratio
condition gives
\[
 g_{BM}-g_H=\sigma_{HM}(g_B+g_w)
 =\sigma_{HM}[\gamma_A+(1+\nu)g_B].
\]
Using \(g_{BM}=g_B+g_M=n+\gamma_A+(1+\nu)g_B\) yields
\eqref{eq:axm-human-research-growth}.

The household Euler equation gives \(r=\rho+\gamma\), where
\(\gamma=\gamma_A+\nu g_B\). The capital-demand condition gives
\(K/Y=\alpha/(\rho+\delta+\gamma)\) and hence the investment share in
\eqref{eq:axm-cd-shares}. Let \(m=M/Y\) and \(u=\beta^2\). The research
first-order condition implies
\[
 \frac{qB}{Y}=\frac{m}{\eta\bar s g_B}.
\]
The envelope derivative of optimized operating profit is
\[
 \frac{\pi_B}{q}=\frac{uY/B}{q}
 =\frac{u\eta\bar s g_B}{m}.
\]
Substitution into the costate equation gives
\[
 n+\gamma-g_B
 =\rho+\gamma-\eta\bar s g_B
 -\frac{u\eta\bar s g_B}{m},
\]
which yields the research share in \eqref{eq:axm-cd-shares}. The resource
constraint gives \(c=1-u-i-m\). Both transversality expressions decline at
rate \(\rho-n\), because \(g_{qB}=g_Y=n+\gamma\) and
\(r=\rho+\gamma\). This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

 d_H=\frac1\eta-\omega_M(1+\nu)=\frac{D(\omega_M)}{\eta}

 H_0=(1-\ell^*)N_0,\qquad
 P=A_0\ell^*N_0(k^*)^{\alpha/\lambda}

 B_0=\left[

 q_0=\frac{M_0}{\eta\omega_M g_B^*B_0}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-research-burst}}

Write
\[
 \varrho_{XL}=\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}<1,
 \qquad
 \varrho_{HM}=\frac{\sigma_{HM}-1}{\sigma_{HM}}<1,
 \qquad
 \theta=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}.
\]
I first establish bounds that hold for every admissible research policy. The
weighted power mean of order \(\varrho_{HM}<1\) is no larger than its arithmetic
mean, including the Cobb--Douglas limit and the standard continuous boundary
extension. Hence
\[
 E=\left[\omega_HH^{\varrho_{HM}}
 +\omega_M(BM)^{\varrho_{HM}}\right]^{1/\varrho_{HM}}
 \leq \omega_HH+\omega_MBM
 \leq H+BM.
\]
Let
\[
 \underline w=\min_{0\leq t\leq T}w_t>0,
 \qquad c_t=w_tH_t+M_t.
\]
Define total research expenditure by
\begin{equation}
 S(H,M)\equiv\int_0^T c_t\dd t
 =\int_0^T(w_tH_t+M_t)\dd t,
 \label{eq:axm-research-total-expenditure}
\end{equation}
and write it as \(S\) when there is no ambiguity.
For a research policy, define the reduced payoff over this horizon by
\[
 J_T(H,M)=\int_0^T
 \exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
 [\pi(B_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)-w_tH_t-M_t]\dd t.
\]
The capability law implies that \(B\) is nondecreasing, so \(B_t\geq B_0\).
Therefore, almost everywhere,
\begin{align*}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}B_t^{1-\eta}
 &=(1-\eta)\chi B_t^{-\eta}E_t^\eta\\
 &\leq(1-\eta)\chi
       \left(M_t+\frac{H_t}{B_t}\right)^\eta\\
 &\leq(1-\eta)\chi
       \left(1+\frac{1}{B_0\underline w}\right)^\eta c_t^\eta.
\end{align*}
H\"older's inequality then gives, for every \(t\leq T\),
\[
 B_t^{1-\eta}
 \leq B_0^{1-\eta}
 +\bar c\int_0^T c_s^\eta\dd s
 \leq B_0^{1-\eta}+\bar cT^{1-\eta}S^\eta,
\]
where \(\bar c>0\) does not depend on the controls. Raising both sides to
the power \(1/(1-\eta)\) proves
\begin{equation}
 \max_{0\leq t\leq T}B_t
 \leq C_B\left(1+S^{\eta/(1-\eta)}\right).
 \label{eq:axm-finite-horizon-capability-bound}
\end{equation}
This estimate includes arbitrarily concentrated measurable controls. In
particular, if expenditure \(S\) is supported on a set of length \(d\leq T\),
then \(\int c_t^\eta\dd t\leq d^{1-\eta}S^\eta\); shrinking the duration does
not overturn the bound.

I next bound optimized operating profit. Let
\(\overline K=\max_{t\leq T}K_t\) and
\(\overline{\mathcal L}=\max_{t\leq T}A_tL_t\). The weighted power-mean inequality and
\(\omega_L+\omega_X=1\) imply
\(Z\leq\omega_LAL+\omega_XX\leq AL+X\), including
the Cobb--Douglas limit. The final-good firm's demand
condition therefore gives
\begin{align*}
 p_X(X;K,L)X
 &=(1-\alpha)s_XK^\alpha Z^{1-\alpha}\\
 &\leq(1-\alpha)\overline K^\alpha
       \left(\overline{\mathcal L}^{1-\alpha}+X^{1-\alpha}\right).
\end{align*}
Set \(a=(1-\alpha)\overline K^\alpha\) and
\(b=a\overline{\mathcal L}^{1-\alpha}\). Maximizing the upper bound over \(X\geq0\)
yields
\begin{align*}
 \pi(B;K,A,L)
 &\leq b+\sup_{X\geq0}\left\{aX^{1-\alpha}-\frac{X}{B}\right\}\\
 &=b+\alpha a[a(1-\alpha)]^\theta B^\theta
 \leq C_\pi(1+B^\theta).
\end{align*}
The revenue product \(p_X(X;K,A,L)X\), rather than necessarily the unit price
itself, has its continuous value zero at \(X=0\). The pointwise maximum defining
\(\pi\) is attained because its objective is continuous and the linear inference
cost eventually dominates revenue. The same domination is locally uniform in
\((B,K,A,L)\), so \(\pi\) is continuous
and therefore measurable along an admissible state path; a measurable
pointwise maximizing selection also exists. The
same bound applies to the primitive problem for any choice of \(X\), since its
operating payoff cannot exceed \(\pi\).

Integrability of \(r\) implies that
\[
 D_t=\exp\left\{-\int_0^t r_s\dd s\right\}
\]
has finite bounds \(0<\underline D\leq D_t\leq\overline D<\infty\) on
\([0,T]\). Combining the capability and profit bounds gives
\begin{align}
 J_T(H,M)
 &\leq \overline D\int_0^T\pi(B_t;K_t,A_t,L_t)\dd t
       -\underline DS\\
 &\leq C_0+C_1S^p-\underline DS,
 \qquad
 p=\frac{\eta\theta}{1-\eta}
  =\frac{\eta(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(1-\eta)}.
 \label{eq:axm-finite-horizon-payoff-bound}
\end{align}
This proves equations \eqref{eq:axm-finite-horizon-capability-bound} and
\eqref{eq:axm-finite-horizon-payoff-bound}. If \(\eta<\alpha\), then
\(p=\eta\theta/(1-\eta)<1\), so the right-hand side tends to \(-\infty\)
as \(S\to\infty\) and has a finite supremum over \(S\geq0\). The zero-research
policy has a finite payoff. Hence the truncated value is finite and the
objective is coercive in total research expenditure. Because \(w\) is bounded
above and away from zero on \([0,T]\), \(S\) is equivalent to the
\(L^1\) norm of the nonnegative pair \((H,M)\).

It remains to establish the converse for \(\eta>\alpha\) and characterize the
boundary when both CES nests permit gross substitution. Hence suppose now that
\(\sigma_{XL}>1\) and \(\sigma_{HM}>1\), so both CES powers lie in \((0,1)\).
A sharper operating-profit asymptotic is needed. For fixed positive
\(K,A,\) and \(L\), as \(X/(AL)\to\infty\),
\[
 Z\sim\omega_X^{1/\varrho_{XL}}X,
 \qquad s_X\to1,
\]
and hence
\[
 p_XX\sim d_XK^\alpha X^{1-\alpha},
 \qquad
 d_X=(1-\alpha)\omega_X^{(1-\alpha)/\varrho_{XL}}.
\]
Under the rescaling \(X=KB^{1/\alpha}x\),
\[
 \frac{p_X(X;K,A,L)X-X/B}{KB^\theta}
 \longrightarrow d_Xx^{1-\alpha}-x.
\]
The limiting objective has the unique maximizer
\[
 x^\star=[d_X(1-\alpha)]^{1/\alpha}
\]
and value
\[
 \kappa_X=\alpha d_X[d_X(1-\alpha)]^\theta>0.
\]
The linear term in \(x\) keeps the maximizing sequence bounded, while the
strictly positive limiting maximum keeps it away from zero. Consequently,
\[
 \pi(B;K,A,L)\sim\kappa_XKB^\theta.
\]
The convergence is uniform when \(K,A,\) and \(L\) remain in compact subsets of
the positive orthant.

Fix \(\Delta\in(0,T)\). Set \(H=0\) on all of \([0,T]\), set
\(M=\mathcal B\) on \([0,\Delta]\), and set \(M=0\) on
\((\Delta,T]\). Because \(\sigma_{HM}>1\),
\[
 E=\omega_M^{1/\varrho_{HM}}BM
\]
along this policy. Capability therefore satisfies
\[
 B_{\mathcal B}(t)^{1-\eta}
 =B_0^{1-\eta}+(1-\eta)d_M\mathcal B^\eta
   \min\{t,\Delta\},
 \qquad
 d_M=\chi\omega_M^{\eta/\varrho_{HM}}>0.
\]
Choosing \(X\) to maximize operating profit at each date gives
\[
 J_T(\mathcal B)
 =\int_0^T D_t\pi(B_{\mathcal B}(t);K_t,A_t,L_t)\dd t
 -\mathcal B\int_0^\Delta D_t\dd t.
\]
For \(\mathcal B\geq1\), the explicit capability solution implies
\[
 \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}
 \frac{B_{\mathcal B}(t)}
 {\mathcal B^{\eta/(1-\eta)}}\leq C
\]
for a constant independent of \(\mathcal B\). The uniform profit bound above
then bounds the discounted operating-profit integrand, after division by
\(\mathcal B^{\eta\theta/(1-\eta)}\), by an integrable constant on
\([0,T]\). Dominated convergence, the operating-profit asymptotic, and the
capability solution therefore yield
\begin{equation}
 J_T(\mathcal B)
 =c_\pi\mathcal B^{\eta\theta/(1-\eta)}
  -c_M\mathcal B
  +o\!\left(\mathcal B^{\eta\theta/(1-\eta)}\right),
 \label{eq:axm-research-burst-payoff}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 c_M=\int_0^\Delta D_t\dd t>0
\]
and
\[
 c_\pi=\kappa_X[(1-\eta)d_M]^{\theta/(1-\eta)}
 \int_0^T D_tK_t
 \min\{t,\Delta\}^{\theta/(1-\eta)}\dd t>0.
\]
Finally,
\[
 \frac{\eta\theta}{1-\eta}
 =\frac{\eta(1-\alpha)}{\alpha(1-\eta)}
 \begin{cases}
  >1,&\eta>\alpha,\\
  =1,&\eta=\alpha,\\
  <1,&\eta<\alpha.
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus the fixed-duration family makes the truncated value unbounded when
\(\eta>\alpha\). If the exogenous paths admit extensions to \([0,\infty)\)
for which the primitive problem is defined, a zero-control continuation is admissible,
and sales revenue is extended continuously at \(X=0\), setting \(X=H=M=0\)
after \(T\) leaves each payoff unchanged. Under those additional conditions,
the same family also makes the corresponding infinite-horizon value unbounded.
At equality, the sign of \(c_\pi-c_M\) determines this family's leading
behavior; a nonpositive coefficient for this family does not rule out a
different unbounded sequence.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-unit-research-threshold}}

At \(\sigma_{XL}=1\), let
\(\beta=(1-\alpha)\omega_X\) and
\(\lambda=(1-\alpha)\omega_L\). The exact pointwise monopoly solution in
\eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-cd-solution}, together with
\(U=\beta^2Y\), gives
\begin{equation}
 \pi(B;K,A,L)
 =\beta(1-\beta)
 (\beta^2B)^{\beta/(1-\beta)}
 \left[K^\alpha(AL)^\lambda\right]^{1/(1-\beta)}.
 \label{eq:axm-unit-profit-power}
\end{equation}
Thus optimized operating profit is a positive dated coefficient times
\(B^a\), where
\[
 a=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta}.
\]

For a nonnegative research-compute policy on \([0,T]\), write
\(S=\int_0^TM_t\dd t\). The transformed capability law is exact:
\[
 B_t^{1-\eta}
 =B_0^{1-\eta}+(1-\eta)\chi\int_0^tM_s^\eta\dd s.
\]
H\"older's inequality therefore implies
\[
 \max_{t\leq T}B_t
 \leq C_B\left(1+S^{\eta/(1-\eta)}\right).
\]
Because the dated coefficient in \eqref{eq:axm-unit-profit-power} and the
discount factor have finite positive bounds on the fixed horizon, the truncated
payoff satisfies
\begin{equation}
 J_T(M)\leq C_0+C_1S^{p_1}-\underline D S,
 \qquad
 p_1=\frac{\beta\eta}{(1-\beta)(1-\eta)}.
 \label{eq:axm-unit-research-payoff-bound}
\end{equation}
If \(\eta<1-\beta\), then \(p_1<1\), so the right-hand side tends to minus
infinity. The zero-research policy has a finite payoff, which proves finiteness
and coercivity.

For the converse, fix \(0<\Delta<T\), set
\(M=\mathcal B\) on \([0,\Delta]\), and set \(M=0\) afterward. Then
\[
 B_{\mathcal B}(t)^{1-\eta}
 =B_0^{1-\eta}+(1-\eta)\chi\mathcal B^\eta
 \min\{t,\Delta\}.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:axm-unit-profit-power} and dominated convergence
give
\[
 J_T(\mathcal B)
 =c_\pi\mathcal B^{p_1}-c_M\mathcal B
 +o(\mathcal B^{p_1}),
 \qquad c_\pi,c_M>0.
\]
The exponent satisfies
\[
 p_1
 \begin{cases}
  >1,&\eta>1-\beta,\\
  =1,&\eta=1-\beta,\\
  <1,&\eta<1-\beta.
 \end{cases}
\]
The truncated value is therefore unbounded when \(\eta>1-\beta\). At
equality, the sign of \(c_\pi-c_M\) determines the leading behavior of this
family. Since an infinite-horizon equilibrium requires the developer's
objective to have a finite real value, \(\eta\leq1-\beta\) is necessary.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-capped-terminal-regimes}}

Write \(\sigma=\sigma_{XL}\),
\(\phi=(\sigma-1)/\sigma\), and
\(a=\sigma^{-1}-\alpha\). Since
\(1-e_X=\phi+as_X\), equation
\eqref{eq:axm-high-sigma-exact-z} gives
\[
 \mathcal H_\sigma(s)^{1/\theta}
 =(1-\alpha)\omega_X^{\sigma/(\sigma-1)}
 (\phi+as)s^{-1/(\sigma-1)}.
\]
The logarithmic derivative of the last two factors has the sign of
\[
 a(\sigma-2)s-\phi.
\]
This expression is negative on \((0,1]\). If
\(a(\sigma-2)\leq0\), the claim is immediate. If \(\sigma>2\) and
\(a>0\), then
\[
 a(\sigma-2)<\frac{\sigma-2}{\sigma}
 <\frac{\sigma-1}{\sigma}=\phi.
\]
If \(1<\sigma<2\) and \(a<0\), then
\[
 a(\sigma-2)
 =\left(\alpha-\frac1\sigma\right)(2-\sigma)
 <\left(1-\frac1\sigma\right)(2-\sigma)<\phi.
\]
Thus \(\mathcal H_\sigma\) is strictly decreasing. Its endpoint limits
follow directly from its definition. Its range is therefore
\([\mathcal D_\sigma,\infty)\).

Consider first a terminal regime with \(\bar s_X\in(0,1)\). The CES demand
system then keeps \(X/(AL)\) finite and positive. Since
\[
 \frac{U}{Y}=(1-\alpha)s_X(1-e_X),\qquad X=BU,
\]
and \(B\to\overline B\), output, AI production services, effective labor,
capital, and consumption have the common terminal growth rate
\(n+\gamma_A\). The household Euler equation implies
\(\bar r=\rho+\gamma_A\), so
\[
 \frac{Y}{K}\to\frac{\rho+\gamma_A+\delta}{\alpha}
 =\mathcal R.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:axm-high-sigma-exact-z} now gives
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-supported-share}. A unique interior solution exists
if and only if
\(\mathcal R>\overline B^\theta\mathcal D_\sigma\), equivalently
\(\overline B<\overline B_c\). The labor-share formula follows from the
competitive wage equation.

A terminal regime cannot have \(s_X\to0\), because then
\(Y/K=\overline B^\theta\mathcal H_\sigma(s_X)\to\infty\), contrary to the
maintained convergence of the return. If \(s_X\to1\), the static system gives
\[
 \frac{Y}{K}\to z_A(\overline B),\qquad
 \bar r=\alpha z_A(\overline B)-\delta.
\]
A positive limiting consumption share and the Euler equation then imply
\(\bar g=n+\bar r-\rho\). This regime is consistent with
\(X/(AL)\to\infty\) precisely when
\(\bar g>n+\gamma_A\), or
\(\overline B>\overline B_c\). This proves the stated regime division away
from equality.

Finally, as \(s_X\to1\), \(e_X\to\alpha\). The static pricing and markup
conditions give
\[
 \frac{U}{Y}\to(1-\alpha)^2,\qquad
 \frac{p_XX}{Y}\to1-\alpha.
\]
This proves the remaining claims. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-capped-frontier-research}}

Let \(D=\overline B-B\). The capability law implies
\[
 \frac{\dot B}{D}=\frac{\chi B^\eta M^\eta}{\overline B}.
\]
The maintained positive finite limit of \(M\) makes the right-hand side
converge to a positive finite constant, denoted by \(a\). The research
first-order condition can be written
\[
 1=qD\frac{\chi\eta B^\eta M^{\eta-1}}{\overline B}.
\]
Therefore \(qD\) converges to a positive finite constant. Since \(q/Y\) has
a positive finite limit and \(g_Y\to\bar g\), it follows that
\(g_q\to\bar g\) and \(\dot D/D\to-\bar g\). But
\(\dot D/D=-\dot B/D\), so \(a=\bar g\). Substitution in the capability law
gives \eqref{eq:axm-capped-terminal-research}.

Because \(g_B=\dot B/B\to0\), the capped costate equation becomes
\[
 \bar g=\bar r-\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{X}{qB^2}+\bar g,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:axm-capped-terminal-shadow-value}. Since
\(\bar g>0\) and \(M\) has a finite limit, \(M/Y\to0\). For the household
transversality object, terminal log growth is
\(-\rho+n+\bar g-\bar g=n-\rho\). For the developer object it is
\(-\bar r+\bar g+0=n-\rho\), using the Euler equation in either terminal
regime. Both are negative because \(\rho>n\). \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-capped-local-equilibrium}}

Let \(G=n+\gamma_A\), \(D=\overline B-B\), and
\(a=\dot B/D\). Consider first
\(\overline B<\overline B_c\). Define the normalized static allocations
\(y=Y/(AN)\), \(x=X/(AN)\), and \(u=U/(AN)\). The variables in
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-normalization} imply
\[
 B=\overline B-d\tau,
 \qquad
 M=\left[
  \widetilde qd\chi\eta\frac{B^\eta}{\overline B}
 \right]^{1/(1-\eta)},
 \qquad
 a=\chi\frac{B^\eta M^\eta}{\overline B}.
\]
After solving the dated static block for \((y,x,u)\) conditional on
\((k,B)\), the normalized dynamic system is
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:axm-capped-labor-normalized-system}
\begin{align}
 \frac{\dot k}{k}
 &=\frac{y-c-u-M\tau}{k}-\delta-G,\\
 \frac{\dot c}{c}&=r-\rho-\gamma_A,\\
 \frac{\dot d}{d}&=G-a,\\
 \frac{\dot{\widetilde q}}{\widetilde q}
 &=r-\frac{x}{\widetilde qB^2}
 -\eta\frac{ad\tau}{B}+a-G,\\
 \dot\tau&=-G\tau,
 \qquad r=\alpha\frac{y}{k}-\delta.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The system uses the same dated equilibrium equations as Definition
\ref{def:axm-benchmark-equilibrium}; only the coordinates have changed.

Let \(\overline e_X=(1-\overline s_X)/\sigma_{XL}
+\alpha\overline s_X\). The terminal point is obtained sequentially from
\begin{align*}
 \overline x
 &=\left[
  \frac{\overline s_X\omega_L}
  {(1-\overline s_X)\omega_X}
 \right]^{1/\varphi},&
 \overline u&=\frac{\overline x}{\overline B},\\
 \overline y
 &=\frac{\overline u}
 {(1-\alpha)\overline s_X(1-\overline e_X)},&
 \overline k&=\frac{\overline y}{\mathcal R},\\
 \overline c
 &=\overline y-\overline u-(\delta+G)\overline k,&
 \overline{\widetilde q}
 &=\frac{\overline x}{\overline r\,\overline B^2},\\
 \overline M
 &=\left(\frac{G\overline B^{1-\eta}}{\chi}\right)^{1/\eta},&
 \overline d
 &=\frac{\overline M^{1-\eta}}
 {\overline{\widetilde q}\chi\eta\overline B^{\eta-1}}.
\end{align*}
Equation \eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-supported-share} determines
\(\overline s_X\) before any of these expressions is used.

Linearize in
\((\ln k,\ln c,\ln d,\ln\widetilde q,\tau)\). At \(\tau=0\), the Ramsey
variables do not depend to first order on \((d,\widetilde q)\), while the
research-gap variables can depend on the Ramsey block. The Jacobian is
therefore block triangular. Let
\(r_k=\partial r/\partial\ln k\) at the terminal point. Along the interior
static branch, an increase in \(k\) raises output less than proportionally
because effective labor remains essential. Hence \(r_k<0\). Direct
differentiation of the static monopoly condition gives the same inequality.
The Ramsey block is the first matrix in
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-jacobian-blocks}; its determinant is
\((\overline c/\overline k)r_k<0\).

The research first-order condition implies
\(\partial\ln a/\partial\ln d
=\partial\ln a/\partial\ln\widetilde q=\mu\). Moreover,
\(a=G\) and
\(x/(\widetilde qB^2)=\overline r\) at the terminal point. These identities
give the second matrix in
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-jacobian-blocks}, whose determinant is
\(-G\mu\overline r<0\). The final root is the root \(-G\) of the
\(\tau\) equation. Thus there are three stable and two unstable roots.

Now consider \(\overline B>\overline B_c\). Under
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-ai-normalization},
\[
 K^{-1}=\tau h^{1/\varphi},
 \qquad
 B=\overline B-d\tau h^{1/\varphi},
 \qquad
 M=\left[vd\chi\eta\frac{B^\eta}{\overline B}
 \right]^{1/(1-\eta)}.
\]
Let \(y=Y/K\), \(x=X/K\), \(u=U/K\), and
\(g_K=\dot K/K\). The exact normalized system is
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:axm-capped-ai-normalized-system}
\begin{align}
 \dot h&=h\varphi(G-g_K),\\
 \frac{\dot d}{d}&=g_K-a,\\
 \frac{\dot c}{c}&=n+r-\rho-g_K,\\
 \frac{\dot v}{v}
 &=r-\frac{x}{vB^2}
 -\eta\frac{ad\tau h^{1/\varphi}}{B}+a-g_K,\\
 \dot\tau&=-G\tau,\\
 g_K&=y-c-u-M\tau h^{1/\varphi}-\delta,
 \qquad r=\alpha y-\delta.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The coordinate \(h\), rather than \(AN/K\), keeps the CES block differentiable
at the AI-dominated terminal point.

At \((h,\tau)=(0,0)\), the static allocation is AI dominated,
\(a=\overline g\), and
\(x/(vB^2)=\overline r\). Linearization gives the root
\(\varphi(G-\overline g)<0\) from the \(h\) equation, the root
\(\overline c>0\) from the consumption--capital equation, and the root
\(-G<0\) from the \(\tau\) equation. The two remaining roots are those of
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-ai-jacobian-block}. Its determinant is
\(-\overline g\mu\overline r<0\), so one is negative and one is positive.
The AI-dominated terminal point also has three stable and two unstable roots.

With distinct roots, the stable direction from the Ramsey block below the
threshold has a nonzero \(k\) component, and the stable research-gap direction
has a nonzero \(d\) component. Above the threshold, the stable \(h\) direction
has a nonzero \(h\) component, and the stable research-gap direction again has
a nonzero \(d\) component. The \(\tau\) direction has a nonzero \(\tau\)
component in both regimes. The projection of the three-dimensional stable
manifold onto the three predetermined normalized variables is therefore
nonsingular.

The stable-manifold theorem now makes the stable manifold locally a graph of
the two jump variables over the three predetermined variables. It selects a
unique local path converging to the terminal point. Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-capped-frontier-research} establishes both transversality
conditions. Finally, condition \eqref{eq:axm-capped-concavity-gate} holds
strictly when \(B\) is sufficiently close to \(\overline B\). A small enough
neighborhood therefore gives developer optimality by Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-capped-developer-sufficiency}; household and final-good-firm
optimality follow from the conditions in Definition
\ref{def:axm-benchmark-equilibrium}. The selected path is consequently a local
finite-cap equilibrium. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-infinite-frontier-limit}}

On a compact subset bounded away from zero,
\[
 \psi_{\overline B}(B)=1+O(\overline B^{-1}),
 \qquad
 \frac{\dot B}{\overline B-B}
 =\frac{\chi(BM)^\eta}{\overline B}=O(\overline B^{-1}),
\]
uniformly together with their first derivatives. The algebraic research
condition is smooth and interior on this set. After substituting the
intratemporal equations, the capped canonical vector field therefore converges
in \(C^1\) to the uncapped vector field. Standard continuous dependence of
ordinary differential equations, applied to the convergent initial state and
jump variables in the proposition, gives uniform convergence on \([0,T]\).

For every frontier above \(\overline B_c\), Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-capped-terminal-regimes} gives
\[
 \bar r=\alpha\mathcal D_\sigma\overline B^\theta-\delta,
 \qquad
 \bar g=n-\rho-\delta
 +\alpha\mathcal D_\sigma\overline B^\theta.
\]
Because \(\theta>0\), both expressions diverge with \(\overline B\). Thus
finite-window convergence cannot be extended uniformly to the terminal regimes,
which proves the noncommutation statement. \(\square\)

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:axm-no-bgp}}

Equation \eqref{eq:axm-high-sigma-exact-z} follows directly from
\[
 \frac{U}{Y}=(1-\alpha)s_X(1-e_X),\qquad X=BU,
 \qquad
 \frac{Z}{X}=\left(\frac{\omega_X}{s_X}\right)^{
 \sigma_{XL}/(\sigma_{XL}-1)}.
\]
For \(\sigma_{XL}>1\), its share-dependent factor tends to infinity as
\(s_X\downarrow0\) and has a positive finite limit as \(s_X\uparrow1\). By
continuity, it has a strictly positive minimum on \((0,1]\). Hence
\(B\to\infty\) implies \(Y/K\to\infty\), without imposing a limit on
\(s_X\). Both \(\alpha Y/K\) and the net interest rate
\(r=\alpha Y/K-\delta\) therefore diverge. Equation \eqref{eq:axm-euler} then
makes consumption growth unbounded, contradicting finite constant growth.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Derivation of the AI-dominated singular limit}

Let \(z=Y/K\), \(u=U/Y\), \(m=M/Y\), \(i=(\dot K+\delta K)/Y\), and
\(c=C/Y\). Let \(\widehat h>0\) denote the assumed limit of \(g_B/z\).
Whenever no ambiguity arises below, a lower-case share also denotes its limit.
Combining the final-good firm's AI-demand condition, the monopolist's markup
condition, market clearing for AI production services with \(X=BU\), and final production gives
\[
 u\to(1-\alpha)^2,\qquad z\sim\mathcal D B^\theta,\qquad
 \theta=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}.
\]
The household Euler equation, the final-good firm's condition
\(r=\alpha z-\delta\), and the assumed asymptotic regularity of the positive
consumption share jointly imply
\[
 \frac{g_Y}{z}\to\alpha.
\]
Since \(g_Y=g_K+g_z\), \(g_K/z\to i\), and the regular scaling relation gives
\(g_z/z\to\theta\widehat h\),
\begin{equation}
 i+\theta\widehat h=\alpha.
 \label{eq:axm-app-resource-growth}
\end{equation}

For the human-research extension, define
\[
 \varrho_{XL}=\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}},\qquad
 \mathcal Q=BY,\qquad
 \psi=\frac{\sigma_{HM}}{\sigma_{XL}}.
\]
The final CES share odds and the wage equation imply the exact relation
\[
 Bw=(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{\omega_L}{\omega_X}
 u^{-\varrho_{XL}}
 A^{\varrho_{XL}}
 \left(\frac{\mathcal Q}{L}\right)^{1/\sigma_{XL}}.
\]
The conditional research demands then give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{H}{L}=\mathcal B_t
 \left(\frac{\mathcal Q}{L}\right)^{1-\psi},
 \qquad
 \mathcal B_t=mA^{-\varrho_{XL}\sigma_{HM}}
 \left(\frac{\omega_H}{\omega_M}\right)^{\sigma_{HM}}
 \left[(1-\alpha)s_X\frac{\omega_L}{\omega_X}
 u^{-\varrho_{XL}}\right]^{-\sigma_{HM}}.
 \label{eq:axm-app-sectoral-labor}
\end{equation}
To establish the required regularity without assuming it for an additional
endogenous ratio, combine the final-good firm's AI-demand condition, the
monopolist's markup condition, and market clearing for AI production services with \(X=BU\):
\[
 u=(1-\alpha)s_X(1-e_X),\qquad
 e_X=\frac{1-s_X}{\sigma_{XL}}+\alpha s_X.
\]
Regularity of \(s_X\) therefore implies
\(u\to(1-\alpha)^2\) and \(g_u=o(z)\). It follows from
\eqref{eq:axm-app-sectoral-labor} that \(\mathcal B_t\to\mathcal B_*>0\) and
\(g_{\mathcal B_t}=o(z)\). Moreover,
\[
 \frac{g_{\mathcal Q/N}}{g_B}
 \to\frac{\widehat h+\alpha}{\widehat h}>0.
\]
Because \(B\to\infty\), the integral of \(g_B\) diverges. Integrating the last
relation therefore gives \(\mathcal Q/N\to\infty\).
Equation \eqref{eq:axm-app-sectoral-labor}, together with \(H+L=N\), therefore
implies
\[
 (H/N,L/N)\to
 \begin{cases}
  (1,0),&\psi<1,\\
  (\bar h_N,1-\bar h_N),&\psi=1,\\
  (0,1),&\psi>1.
 \end{cases}
\]
When \(\psi=1\), taking limits in
\eqref{eq:axm-app-sectoral-labor} gives
\[
 \bar h_N=\frac{\mathcal R_\sigma}{1+\mathcal R_\sigma},\qquad
 \mathcal R_\sigma=\frac{\eta}{\Delta}A_*^{1-\sigma}
 \left[
  \frac{(1-\alpha)\omega_H\omega_X}{\omega_M\omega_L}
 \right]^{\sigma},\qquad
 \sigma=\sigma_{XL}=\sigma_{HM},
\]
where \(A_*=A_0e^{\gamma_AT^*}\in(0,\infty)\). This proves equation
\eqref{eq:axm-singular-equal-elasticity-share}.
To obtain the sectoral labor growth rates without assuming an endogenous limit,
let \(y=H/L\), \(a_H=H/N=y/(1+y)\), and
\(\kappa=1-\psi\). Log-differentiating
\eqref{eq:axm-app-sectoral-labor} and using \(N=L+H\) gives the exact pair
\[
 g_y=g_{\mathcal B_t}+\kappa(g_{\mathcal Q}-g_L),\qquad
 n=g_L+a_Hg_y.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \frac{g_L}{z}
 =
 \frac{n/z-a_H\{g_{\mathcal B_t}/z+\kappa g_{\mathcal Q}/z\}}
 {1-\kappa a_H}.
\]
Using the labor-allocation limits above shows that \(g_L/z\to0\) when
\(\psi\geq1\), whereas, when \(\psi<1\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g_L}{z}\to
 -\frac{1-\psi}{\psi}(\widehat h+\alpha).
 \label{eq:axm-app-production-labor-growth}
\end{equation}
The same two identities imply \(g_H/z\to0\) for \(\psi\leq1\) and
\(g_H/z\to(1-\psi)(\widehat h+\alpha)\) for \(\psi>1\).
Thus both sectoral labor growth rates are \(O(z)\), and the wage relation above
implies that \(g_w/z\) is finite. In the automated-research benchmark this
sectoral block is absent: \(L=N\), so \(g_L=n=o(z)\), and \(s_M\equiv1\).

In the human-research extension, the effective-research index has the exact
envelope identity
\[
 g_E=(1-s_M)g_H+s_M(g_B+g_M).
\]
Regularity of \(M/Y\) gives \(g_M=g_Y+o(z)\). Because \(s_M\to1\) and
\(g_H=O(z)\), it follows that
\((1-s_M)g_H=o(z)\). Thus the human-input term changes \(g_E\) only by
\(o(z)\), and
\[
 g_E=(\widehat h+\alpha)z+o(z).
\]
In the automated-research benchmark, regularity of \(M/Y\) and direct log
differentiation of \(g_B=\chi B^{\eta-1}M^\eta\) give the same next equation.
Thus, in either version,
\[
 g_{g_B}=(\eta-1)g_B+\eta g_Y+o(z).
\]
Because \(g_B/z\to\widehat h\) and this ratio converges asymptotically regularly,
\(g_{g_B}/z\) and \(g_z/z\) have the same limit. Likewise,
\(z/(\mathcal D B^\theta)\to1\) gives
\(g_z/z\to\theta\widehat h\). Dividing by \(z\)
therefore yields
\[
 \theta\widehat h=(\eta-1)\widehat h+\eta\alpha,
\]
so
\[
 \widehat h=\frac{\eta\alpha}{1+\theta-\eta}\equiv h.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:axm-app-resource-growth} gives \(i=\alpha-\theta h\).

The exact research-compute first-order condition can be written as
\[
 \frac{qB}{K}=\frac{M/Y}{\eta s_M(g_B/z)}.
\]
Let \(Q\equiv\lim qB/K\). Taking limits gives \(Q=m/(\eta h)\). The capability
derivative of optimized operating profit gives \(\pi_B/q=uz/(qB/K)\).
Asymptotic regularity of \(qB/K\) implies that
\(g_q-(g_K-g_B)=o(z)\). Substituting these results into the costate equation and using
\eqref{eq:axm-app-resource-growth} yields
\[
 Q=\frac{u}{\eta\alpha},
 \qquad
 m=\frac{\eta u}{1+\theta-\eta}.
\]
The resource constraint determines \(c\). For \(0<\eta<1\), both \(i\) and \(c\)
are positive; in fact
\[
 c=\alpha(1-\alpha)
 \left(1+\frac{\eta}{1+\theta-\eta}\right)>0.
\]
Finally, \(g_z\sim\theta g_B\sim\theta hz\), so
\(\dot z\sim\theta hz^2\). For all sufficiently large \(z\), therefore,
\(\dot z\geq(\theta h/2)z^2\). Integrating the corresponding inequality for
\(1/z\) shows that \(z\) becomes unbounded in finite additional time. Since
\(i=\alpha-\theta h>0\), \(g_K\sim iz>0\). Thus \(K\) does not fall near that
date and \(Y=zK\) also becomes unbounded, thereby meeting the definition of a
finite-time singularity used here.

For the wage, log-differentiating the exact relation for \(Bw\) gives
\[
 \frac{g_w}{z}
 \to\frac{\alpha-(\sigma_{XL}-1)h-g_L/z}{\sigma_{XL}}.
\]
In the automated-research benchmark, \(g_L/z\to0\), and the denominator is
\(\sigma_{XL}\). In the human-research extension, the same conclusion applies
when \(\psi\geq1\). When \(\psi<1\), substituting
\eqref{eq:axm-app-production-labor-growth} reduces the expression to
\([\alpha-(\sigma_{HM}-1)h]/\sigma_{HM}\). Hence, with
\(\underline\sigma\) defined as in Conditional Result
\ref{prop:axm-singular-limit},
\[
 \frac{g_w}{z}\to
 \frac{\alpha-(\underline\sigma-1)h}{\underline\sigma}.
\]
This expression is positive when
\(\underline\sigma<1/(\alpha\eta)\). Finally,
\(wL/Y=(1-\alpha)(1-s_X)\to0\). In the human-research extension, the
research-expenditure share satisfies \(wH/M=(1-s_M)/s_M\to0\), while
\(M/Y\to m>0\), so \(wH/Y\to0\). Hence \(wN/Y=wL/Y+wH/Y\to0\), regardless
of which sector employs the limiting positive share of the population. In the
benchmark, \(N=L\), so \(wN/Y=wL/Y\to0\) directly. \(\square\)

\section{Numerical solution algorithm and equilibrium-system audit}
\label{app:axm-numerical-algorithm}

I formulate the numerical exercises as boundary-value calculations, not as forward
simulations from arbitrary values of the jump variables. I fix initial capital and
capability at \(K_0\) and \(B_0\), while \(C(0)\) and \(q(0)\) are
determined jointly with the transition by terminal restrictions. The fixed-horizon
calculations approximate paths satisfying the dated equilibrium system in
Section~\ref{sec:axm-dated-equilibrium-system}. The free-boundary calculation
instead approximates the dated necessary conditions on the conditional
pre-singular branch. Both use the same timing, prices, and normalizations as the
analytical model.

\subsection{Off-balanced-growth validation of the unit-elastic benchmark}
\label{app:axm-off-bgp-validation}

The numerical construction follows the nesting of the model. I first solve the
no-AI Ramsey--Cass--Koopmans economy as a two-dimensional problem. I then solve
the strictly positive-AI economy as a separate four-dimensional problem. I do
not continue mechanically through \(\omega_X=0\): at zero, \(B,q,U,\) and
\(M\) cease to affect final production, the developer's conditions disappear,
and the dimension and rank of the equilibrium system change.

For the no-AI economy, define capital and consumption per unit of effective
labor by \(k=K/(AN)\) and \(c=C/(AN)\). Their laws of motion are
\begin{equation}
 \dot k=k^\alpha-c-(\delta+n+\gamma_A)k,\qquad
 \frac{\dot c}{c}
 =\alpha k^{\alpha-1}-\delta-\rho-\gamma_A.
 \label{eq:axm-rck-validation-system}
\end{equation}
The initial value \(k_0\) is predetermined and \(c_0\) is a jump variable. I
solve the system in logs with residual-controlled collocation. A terminal
condition places the endpoint on the stable linear direction of the
detrended steady state. I move \(k_0\) from the steady-state value to its
target by continuation. A separately coded backward integration of the Ramsey
system recovers the same \(c_0\). This calculation verifies the numerical
architecture in the standard model before the developer is introduced.

For the positive-AI calculation, I fix
\((\omega_X,\sigma_{XL})=(0.20,1)\) and use the automated-research benchmark.
The exact balanced-growth solution in Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-benchmark-equilibrium-existence} supplies a stationary reference,
not the initial condition imposed on the transition. In the log deviations
\(\xi=(\xi_K,\xi_B,\xi_C,\xi_q)'\) defined in
\eqref{eq:axm-benchmark-log-deviations}, the unit-elastic static conditions
eliminate \(Y,U,\) and \(M\) exactly. Substitution into capital accumulation,
the capability law, the household Euler equation, and the developer's costate
equation gives the autonomous system \(\dot\xi=f(\xi)\) used to derive
\eqref{eq:axm-saddle-jacobian}. Thus the numerical solver uses equations
already derived from the dated equilibrium system; it does not add a terminal
growth equation or impose balanced growth along the transition.

Let \(V_S\) span the two stable directions of \(J^*\), and let
\(P_\perp\) contain two independent rows orthogonal to \(V_S\). For each
finite horizon \(T\), the four boundary conditions are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \xi_K(0)&=\log(K_0/K_0^*),\qquad
 \xi_B(0)=\log(B_0/B_0^*),\\
 P_\perp\xi(T)&=0.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:axm-positive-ai-stable-boundary}
\end{equation}
The first line fixes the two predetermined stocks. The second line removes the
two unstable linear components and thereby selects \(\xi_C(0)\) and
\(\xi_q(0)\), or equivalently \(C_0\) and \(q_0\). It does not set
\(\xi(T)=0\), impose the balanced-growth allocation at \(T\), or replace the
transversality conditions.

The off-BGP calculation proceeds in six steps.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=4.5em,labelsep=0.6em]
 \item Construct the exact positive-AI balanced-growth levels, verify every
 dated equilibrium equation at those levels, and compute \(J^*\).
 \item Verify that \(J^*\) has two stable and two unstable roots and that the
 projection of the stable eigenspace onto \((\xi_K,\xi_B)\) is nonsingular.
 \item Set \(B_0=1\). Choose \(K_0\) so that the date-zero capital--output ratio
 equals its balanced-growth value after solving the date-zero production
 equation. This is an initialization rule, not an equilibrium condition.
 \item At \(T=100\), move the two initial log-stock deviations from zero to
 their targets in 12 steps. Each accepted collocation solution initializes the
 next step.
 \item Holding \((K_0,B_0)\) fixed, extend the horizon through
 \(T=150,200,250\). Compare the two initial jump variables and the paths on
 common initial windows.
 \item Reconstruct the static and dynamic equations independently, tighten the
 solver tolerance, densify the initial mesh, increase the number of stock
 continuation steps, and reintegrate short segments of the path backward with
 a different integrator.
\end{enumerate}

Under the baseline parameters, \(B_0^*=0.4436709\). The target therefore has
\(\xi_B(0)=0.8126721\); the capital initialization gives
\(\xi_K(0)=0.2031680\). The 12-step continuation reaches this pair. Between
\(T=200\) and \(T=250\), the largest change in the two initial jump variables
is \(1.84\times10^{-6}\). Tightening the tolerance from \(10^{-8}\) to
\(10^{-9}\), increasing the initial mesh from 121 to 181 nodes, and increasing
the stock-continuation steps from 12 to 16 changes the initial jumps by at most
\(5.1\times10^{-13}\). Over the first 50 years, the largest change in any of
the four log paths is \(7.7\times10^{-10}\).

The refined solution has a maximum resource residual of
\(3.0\times10^{-9}\), a maximum boundary residual of
\(2.8\times10^{-17}\), and maximum residuals below
\(3.0\times10^{-9}\) for the other reconstructed equilibrium equations. The
backward, segment-by-segment reconstruction differs from the collocation path
by at most \(2.0\times10^{-10}\). Consumption and research expenditure remain
positive, and both finite-date transversality expressions are lower at
\(T=250\) than at date zero.

These checks establish numerical stability of the finite-horizon branch from
the stated off-BGP stocks. They do not prove that \(B_0=1\) belongs to the
local neighborhood in Proposition \ref{prop:axm-unit-saddle-path}, establish
global existence or uniqueness, or verify transversality as \(t\to\infty\).
The terminal projection is a finite-horizon approximation to the nonlinear
stable manifold. A failure at a longer horizon would therefore be a numerical
diagnostic, not evidence that an equilibrium trajectory does not exist.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Finite-window continuation in \(\sigma_{XL}\) around one}{Finite-window continuation in sigma XL around one}}
\label{app:axm-near-unit-algorithm}

I next hold \(\omega_X=0.20\), the initial stocks, and the four dynamic
variables \((K,B,C,q)\) fixed, and continue the accepted unit-elastic
calculation in
\[
 \varphi=\frac{\sigma_{XL}-1}{\sigma_{XL}}.
\]
This order matters. The continuation starts from the positive-AI solution at
\(\sigma_{XL}=1\), not from the no-AI economy. On each fixed horizon, the two
initial conditions for \(K\) and \(B\) and the terminal projection in
\eqref{eq:axm-positive-ai-stable-boundary} remain unchanged. The collocation
solver continues to select \(C(0)\) and \(q(0)\).

For \(\sigma_{XL}\neq1\), the static block is no longer eliminated by a
constant value of \(U/Y\). At every collocation node, I evaluate the CES in
logs using \eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-ces} and solve the monopoly condition
\eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-foc} for \(d=\ln[X/(AL)]\). This determines
\(s_X,X,Y\), and \(U=X/B\). Research compute continues to be determined by
\eqref{eq:axm-research-demand-benchmark}. Substituting these quantities in the
same resource, capability, Euler, and costate equations gives the dated laws
for \((K,B,C,q)\). Thus the extension changes the CES and monopoly equations;
it does not add a dynamic equation or change the timing of an existing one.

The numerical expression for the CES uses \(\log1p\) and \(\operatorname{expm1}\)
when \(\varphi d\) is close to zero and log-sum-exp otherwise. Both branches
evaluate the exact CES. I do not replace the CES by the expansion in
\eqref{eq:axm-near-unit-ces-expansion}. At \(\varphi=0\), the code uses the
analytical Cobb--Douglas limit. The resulting dated system and its analytical
Jacobian reproduce the unit-elastic normalized system to machine precision.

During algorithm development, I used this common terminal projection to verify
the finite-window continuation result on both sides of one. Those outputs solve
the dated equations but do not pass the equilibrium-admission rule because the
terminal restriction belongs to the unit-elastic model. I therefore report no
table or trajectory from that exercise. The calculation remains useful only as
a continuation guess. For the reported \(\sigma_{XL}=0.99\) equilibrium, I
replace it with the complementary-input terminal problem described in
Subsection \ref{app:axm-near-unit-equilibrium-paths}. For
\(\sigma_{XL}>1\), the two regular long-run continuations used by the algorithm
fail the equilibrium-admission rule, so the corresponding segments are
excluded.

\subsection{Finite-cap continuation strategy for gross substitutes}
\label{app:axm-finite-cap-algorithm}

The finite frontier in Section
\ref{sec:axm-finite-cap-gross-substitutes} provides the appropriate BVP for
\(\sigma_{XL}>1\). For each fixed \(\overline B>B_0\), I first compare the
frontier with \(\overline B_c\). Below the threshold, I use the five-dimensional
system \eqref{eq:axm-capped-labor-normalized-system}. Above it, I use
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-ai-normalized-system}. The fifth equation tracks the
exogenous scale \((AN)^{-1}\); the economy still has only the two jump variables
\(C\) and \(q\). The knife-edge is not used as a numerical terminal condition.

Let \(z^*\) denote the terminal point in the relevant normalization and let
\(V_S\) be the \(5\times3\) matrix of stable right eigenvectors. I construct a
\(2\times5\) matrix \(Q_U\) whose rows span the orthogonal complement of
\(V_S\), so \(Q_UV_S=0\). If \(P_S\) selects the three predetermined
normalized variables, the finite-horizon collocation problem uses
\begin{equation}
 P_Sz(0)=z_{S,0},
 \qquad
 Q_U[z(T)-z^*]=0.
 \label{eq:axm-capped-bvp-boundary-conditions}
\end{equation}
The first three restrictions impose the initial states. The last two remove
the unstable terminal components and select the initial values of the two jump
variables. In the code, I multiply \((AN)^{-1}\) by the fixed terminal constant
\(\overline d/\overline B\). This diagonal rescaling improves conditioning but
does not change the roots, the stable manifold, or any model variable.

The implementation evaluates the exact CES and monopoly condition at every
collocation node. It does not use a Taylor approximation around
\(\sigma_{XL}=1\). Automated tests compare both analytic Jacobians with finite
differences, verify three stable and two unstable roots, and verify that the
stable subspace projects nonsingularly onto the predetermined variables. As a
local diagnostic, I solved the nonlinear BVP at \(\sigma_{XL}=1.10\) with
\(\overline B/\overline B_c=0.5\) and \(2\). Both calculations converged; the
largest normalized collocation residual was \(8.5\times10^{-9}\), and the
largest boundary residual was \(1.1\times10^{-15}\).

These calculations implement Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-capped-local-equilibrium}; they verify local equilibrium
selection near each terminal regime. They do not establish a transition from
the paper's original stocks. That requires continuing the stable manifold
backward while checking that \(C_0\) and \(q_0\) stabilize with the horizon,
that dated residuals converge under mesh refinement, that both transversality
objects have the terminal decay rate in Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-capped-frontier-research}, and that
\eqref{eq:axm-capped-concavity-gate} holds on the complete reachable capability
interval. Only a path passing all those tests is admitted as a finite-cap
equilibrium and eligible for frontier continuation.

This order implements Proposition \ref{prop:axm-infinite-frontier-limit}:
first solve and audit equilibrium for each finite frontier, then take
\(\overline B\to\infty\) on a fixed window. I do not report the local diagnostic
paths as economic transition results.

The adoption experiment in Section

Capability is not a state of the no-AI problem, so the no-AI equilibrium does

\subsection{Numerical representation and the intratemporal equilibrium map}

The outer solver uses the four log variables
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf y(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}\log K(t)&\log B(t)&\log C(t)&\log q(t)\end{pmatrix}^{\prime},
 \qquad A(t)=A_0e^{\gamma_At},\qquad N(t)=N_0e^{nt}.
 \label{eq:axm-numerical-state}
\end{equation}
Labor productivity and population are therefore evaluated analytically rather
than added as numerical states. Logs preserve positivity of \(K,B,C,\) and \(q\). Within the
dated static calculation, the human-research share \(H/N\) is represented by a
logit, which keeps \(0<H/N<1\) and implies \(L=N-H>0\).

The implementation evaluates \(A(t)\) analytically. It uses \(B\) as the
capability state in every dated equation. Saved paths retain the descriptive
field name \texttt{log\_capability}; this field means
\(\log B\), not \(\log A\). The earlier complementary-input,
unit-elasticity, and gross-substitutes transition files set \(\gamma_A=0\) and
\(A_0=1\), so the migration does not change those numerical experiments. I
verify this claim with \path{scripts/check_axm_ab_regression.py}: at
\(\gamma_A=0\), the
migrated dated map and paths reproduce sampled observations from the
unit-elasticity, complementary-input, and gross-substitutes files archived at
commit \texttt{d019192}, with zero numerical difference.
The same check verifies the exact balanced-growth equations at
\(\gamma_A=0.01\) for the automated-research benchmark and the
\(\sigma_{HM}=1\) extension. The adoption experiment in Section
\ref{sec:axm-ai-adoption-experiment} uses \(\gamma_A=0.01\); it is solved and
audited separately as described in Appendix
\ref{app:axm-ai-adoption-algorithm}.

For a given date and a trial value of \((K,B,A,N,q)\), the code constructs the
intratemporal equilibrium as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.},leftmargin=*]
 \item Given a trial labor allocation, it solves the monopoly first-order
 condition for \(\log[X/(AL)]\). At \(\sigma_{XL}=1\), the corresponding
 Cobb--Douglas block has a closed-form solution. Otherwise the code uses a
 bracketed scalar root for
 \(\log p_X+\log(1-e_X)+\log B=0\).
 \item It uses the final-good firm's demand conditions to recover \(Y,Z,s_X,r,w,\)
 and \(p_X\). It then computes the unit expenditure \(P_E\), solves the research
 scale condition \(q\chi\eta E^{\eta-1}=P_E\), and applies the two conditional
 CES research demands.
 \item It solves a second bracketed scalar equation equating the trial \(H\) to
 the conditional demand for human research. It then recovers \(BM\),
 \(M=BM/B\), \(U=X/B\), \(s_M\), and every remaining dated allocation and price.
\end{enumerate}
When the monopoly root is required, it is solved to a numerical tolerance of
\(10^{-13}\); the labor--research root is solved to \(10^{-11}\). The direct research first-order conditions
are implied by the dual research allocation and the scale condition; the audit
nevertheless reconstructs them separately. The nested calculation selects an
interior root. It does not by itself prove that the root is unique or globally
optimal. The fixed-horizon files record both numerical fallback flags; neither is
active at a saved observation on the reported paths. The saved observations also
have positive allocations and a strictly positive margin in the monopoly
second-order condition \eqref{eq:axm-monopoly-soc}.

The resulting static allocation is inserted into the four canonical dynamic equations for
\(K,B,C,\) and \(q\) in \eqref{eq:axm-canonical-system}. Thus the outer right-hand
side can be written as
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mathbf y}=\mathcal F\bigl(t,\mathbf y\bigr),
 \label{eq:axm-numerical-rhs}
\end{equation}
where the arguments suppressed in \(\mathcal F\) are the unchanged parameters in
Table~\ref{tab:axm-parameters}. Equation \eqref{eq:axm-numerical-rhs} is a
reduced numerical representation obtained only after the dated algebraic
equilibrium has been solved. It introduces no additional economic equation.

\subsection{Fixed-horizon collocation for \texorpdfstring{\(\sigma_{XL}\leq1\)}{sigma XL <= 1}}

For the complementary-input and unit-elasticity regimes, the code calls
SciPy's \texttt{solve\_bvp}, a fourth-order, residual-controlled collocation
method with a continuously differentiable piecewise-cubic solution and an
adaptive mesh, for a fixed horizon \([0,T]\). The implementation supplies no
analytical Jacobian, so the library approximates it numerically.
It works with detrended logs
\(\widetilde{\mathbf y}(t)=\mathbf y(t)-\bar{\mathbf g}t\), where, in the state
order of \eqref{eq:axm-numerical-state},
\(\bar{\mathbf g}=(\bar g_K,\bar g_B,\bar g_C,\bar g_q)'\) contains the candidate
limiting rates derived in the text. This transformation improves numerical
conditioning. The raw logs are reconstructed before every evaluation of the
intratemporal map, so detrending does not impose the candidate growth rates on
the solution.

There are four boundary conditions for the four log paths. The two initial
conditions are
\begin{equation}
 K(0)=K_0,\qquad B(0)=B_0.
 \label{eq:axm-fixed-initial-boundary}
\end{equation}
At the terminal date, the algorithm imposes the analytical candidate value
\(c^\star\) of \(C/Y\) and one shadow-value ratio:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{C(T)}{Y(T)}=c^\star,\qquad
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
 \frac{X(T)}{q(T)B(T)^2}
 =\rho-n+\bigl(2-\eta\bar s\bigr)\bar g_B,
 &\sigma_{XL}<1,\\[9pt]
 \displaystyle
 \frac{q(T)B(T)}{Y(T)}
 =\frac{m^\star}{\eta\bar s g_B^\star},
 &\sigma_{XL}=1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:axm-fixed-terminal-boundary}
\end{equation}
For \(\sigma_{XL}<1\),
\(c^\star=1-\alpha(n+\gamma_A+\delta)/(\rho+\gamma_A+\delta)\), with
\(\bar s=\omega_M\) when \(\sigma_{HM}=1\) and \(\bar s=1\) when
\(\sigma_{HM}>1\). The corresponding \(\bar g_B\) is given by
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-cd-research-growth} or
\eqref{eq:axm-complement-ces-research-growth}. For \(\sigma_{XL}=1\),
\(g_B^\star\), \(m^\star\), and \(c^\star\) are the candidate quantities in
\eqref{eq:axm-cd-growth-rates} and \eqref{eq:axm-cd-shares}, with \(\bar s\)
defined in \eqref{eq:axm-sbar}. The two terminal restrictions select the jump
variables \(C(0)\) and \(q(0)\). They approximate the infinite-horizon selection
problem; they are not the transversality conditions themselves.

The limiting rates and shares also generate an initial path guess on a uniform
401-node mesh. I choose candidate levels by a small least-squares calculation
that matches the relevant limiting capital-output and shadow ratios. This guess
initializes the nonlinear solver but is not retained as an equation. At every
collocation evaluation, the algorithm solves the intratemporal map and evaluates
\eqref{eq:axm-numerical-rhs}. The mesh then adapts until the residual tolerance is
met or the 8,000-node cap is reached. I do not use a shooting solution for any
reported path.

With \(A_0=B_0=N_0=1\), the same initialization computes the common
\(K_0=2.9435284733\) from the unit-elasticity, \(\sigma_{HM}=2\) candidate
capital--output ratio under the maintained parameter vector. The complementary
paths use that same \(K_0\). It is therefore a parameter-dependent benchmark
initialization, not an independently calibrated stock and not a quantity held
fixed relative to the earlier \(\eta=0.45\) exercises.

The reported unit-elasticity calculations use a solver tolerance of
\(2\times10^{-5}\). The primary horizons are \(T=3{,}100\) for
\(\sigma_{HM}=1\) and \(T=5{,}800\) for \(\sigma_{HM}=2\). They are re-solved at
\(T\in\{2{,}600,3{,}100,3{,}600\}\) and
\(T\in\{5{,}400,5{,}800,6{,}200\}\), respectively. The complementary-input
calculations use \(\sigma_{XL}=0.75\), a solver tolerance of \(10^{-6}\), and
independent cold starts at \(T\in\{3{,}600,4{,}050,4{,}500\}\) for each value of
\(\sigma_{HM}\). Here a cold start means that no solution from a neighboring
horizon is used; I still supply the analytical initial guess. I display the
\(T=4{,}500\) paths. Saved output is evaluated from the collocation
spline at two-year intervals.

The fixed-horizon algorithm can therefore be summarized as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=4.5em,labelsep=0.6em]
 \item Fix the parameters, \((K_0,A_0,B_0,N_0)\), the horizon, and the two analytical
 terminal ratios.
 \item Construct the detrended analytical guess on the initial mesh.
 \item At each collocation evaluation, solve the labor-allocation root and, when
 \(\sigma_{XL}\neq1\), the nested monopoly root; then recover all dated
 quantities and prices.
 \item Solve jointly for the four log paths, including the initial values of the
 two jump variables, subject to
 \eqref{eq:axm-fixed-initial-boundary}--\eqref{eq:axm-fixed-terminal-boundary}.
 \item Adapt the mesh, reconstruct the dated equilibrium conditions, and reject
 any path that fails the numerical acceptance gates.
 \item Repeat at the stated horizons, compare the initial jumps, and verify the
 equation residuals along every saved horizon path.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Free-boundary continuation for \texorpdfstring{\(\sigma_{XL}>1\)}{sigma XL > 1}}

For the archived unbounded-capability, AI-dominated gross-substitutes
diagnostic, a
finite balanced-growth terminal condition is unavailable. The implementation
uses the reported normalization \(N_0=1\), stops at an artificial boundary
\(z_T=(Y/K)(T)\), treats the terminal date \(T\) as an unknown, and increases
\(z_T\) by continuation. Calendar time is normalized as \(\tau=t/T\in[0,1]\).
For the same four log variables, the outer differential system becomes
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd\mathbf y}{\dd\tau}
 =T\,\mathcal F\bigl(T\tau,\mathbf y\bigr).
 \label{eq:axm-free-boundary-rhs}
\end{equation}
The positive duration is represented by a smooth transformation of an
unrestricted scalar. Four paths plus this scalar require five boundary
conditions:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 K(0)&=K_0,\qquad B(0)=B_0,\\
 \frac{C(T)}{Y(T)}
 &=1-(1-\alpha)^2-\alpha+\theta h
 -\frac{\eta(1-\alpha)^2}{1+\theta-\eta},\\
 \frac{q(T)B(T)}{K(T)}&=\frac{(1-\alpha)^2}{\eta\alpha},\qquad
 \frac{Y(T)}{K(T)}=z_T,
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:axm-free-boundary-conditions}
\end{equation}
The two terminal ratios are the conditional limits in
\eqref{eq:axm-singular-ratios}. Thus \(C(0)\), \(q(0)\), and \(T\) are
determined endogenously by the finite-boundary problem.

The reference path used to initialize this problem incorporates the singular
scaling \(g_B/z\to h\), with \(h\) defined in the conditional result. It is then
represented by cubic splines, and the collocation solver works on corrections to
that reference. The condition \(g_B/z=h\) helps construct the terminal guess; it
is not one of the five restrictions in
\eqref{eq:axm-free-boundary-conditions}. Its convergence is checked ex post.

The archived boundary solutions are obtained through the following continuation
sequence.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.},leftmargin=4.5em,labelsep=0.6em]
 \item Solve a \(\sigma_{XL}=1,\sigma_{HM}=2\) seed at \(T=1{,}600\).
 \item At \(T=100\), continue \(\sigma_{XL}\) through
 \(1.02,1.05,1.10,1.20,1.30,1.40,1.50\).
 \item At \(\sigma_{XL}=1.50\), continue the fixed horizon through
 \(T=150,200,300,400,600\), followed by \(700,750,800,825,840\).
 \item Release \(T\) and continue the free boundary through
 \(z_T=0.5,0.6,0.8,1,1.5,2,3,4\), followed by
 \(6,8,12,16,24,32,48,64\).
 \item Starting from that continuation, re-solve sequentially at
 \(z_T=16,32,64,128\). Only these refined solutions enter the archived
convergence audit.
\end{enumerate}
The preliminary stages use a tolerance of \(3\times10^{-5}\). The four archived
free-boundary solutions use \(10^{-6}\), an initial mesh of 301 nodes, and a
12,000-node cap. The full route consists of 38 boundary-value solves. Increasing
\(z_T\) tests whether the initial jumps, paths on common pre-terminal windows,
non-imposed ratios, and the continuation-corrected singularity-time estimate stabilize.

Equation \eqref{eq:axm-z-law} provides the leading-order remaining-time estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat T(z_T)\equiv T(z_T)+\frac{1}{\theta h z_T}.
 \label{eq:axm-singularity-tail-estimate}
\end{equation}
The numerical files and figures denote this estimate by \(T^*(z_T)\). It
extrapolates beyond the solved boundary and is not the true endpoint established
by the numerical calculation, a terminal condition, or a calendar forecast. The
alternative shooting routine retained in the source code is experimental and
does not generate any result I report.

\subsection{Permanent near-unit perturbations from balanced-growth stocks}
\label{app:axm-near-unit-equilibrium-paths}

Figure \ref{fig:axm-near-unit-equilibrium} applies the continuation algorithm in
Section \ref{app:axm-near-unit-algorithm} only where the result passes the
equilibrium-admission rule. I
fix \(\omega_X=0.20\), compute the analytical \(\sigma_{XL}=1\) balanced-growth
path, and give both reported cases its initial predetermined stocks
\(K_0=2.027734\) and \(B_0=0.443671\). Consumption and the shadow value remain
jump variables; no initial flow is normalized to match across elasticities.

At \(\sigma_{XL}=1\), the path is the exact balanced-growth equilibrium in
Proposition \ref{prop:axm-benchmark-equilibrium-existence}. At
\(\sigma_{XL}=0.99\), I replace the earlier unit-elastic terminal projection by
the complementary-input terminal restrictions derived in Propositions
\ref{prop:axm-complements} and \ref{prop:axm-complements-research}. I continue
the terminal horizon from 2,500 to 12,000 years in 500-year increments. At each
step, the preceding solution supplies the initial collocation guess, extended
with the limiting regime's growth rates.

All 20 boundary-value problems converge. A final refinement at the 12,000-year
terminal has a maximum collocation root-mean-square residual below \(10^{-8}\).
Increasing
the terminal from 11,500 to 12,000 changes the four log variables over years
0--2,500 by at most \(9.1\times10^{-7}\), and the final refinement changes that
window by at most \(1.4\times10^{-6}\). The corrected path differs from the
previously plotted finite-window segment by at most \(1.8\times10^{-8}\), which
explains why the visible lower-elasticity curve barely changes even though its
economic classification does. The log levels of the household and developer
transversality objects at the terminal are \(-442.6\) and \(-446.1\); their
analytical continuation growth rates are \(n-\rho=-0.037\) and \(-0.0396\), respectively.
The unit-elasticity sufficiency proposition does not apply at \(0.99\). I
instead use Proposition \ref{prop:axm-path-specific-sufficiency}. For each
saved date, the audit holds \((K,A,L)\) fixed and computes the largest
\(s_X\) attainable over the full reachable domain \(B\geq B_0\). Across the
path and its complementary long-run continuation, that upper share is \(0.208704\). The exact
maximum of the service-capability elasticity in
\eqref{eq:axm-service-capability-elasticity} over the resulting share interval
is \(1.140555\), leaving a concavity margin of \(0.859445\) below the threshold
of two. The benchmark research technology is jointly concave because
\(2\eta=0.40<1\). I therefore admit the \(\sigma_{XL}=0.99\) result as a
numerical equilibrium trajectory.

The earlier \(\sigma_{XL}=1.01\) calculation used the unit-elastic stable
projection and established only a finite-window solution of the dated
conditions. The corrected audit retains the agreed continuation sequence and
extends the fixed-horizon calculation to \(T=5{,}000\). Its initial jump
variables stabilize, but the terminal AI share is only \(0.230289\), so the
calculation has not reached the AI-dominated asymptotic regime. The implied
near-unit separation timescale is about 23,300 model years.

More importantly, Proposition \ref{prop:axm-no-bounded-capability-tail}
rejects bounded capability with a bounded interest rate, while the regular
AI-dominated continuation reaches a finite-time singularity. Neither is an admissible
infinite-horizon equilibrium under the benchmark definition. Proposition
\ref{prop:axm-no-zero-research-corner} rules out setting \(M=0\) at a finite
date. Proposition \ref{prop:axm-gross-substitutes-concavity-gate} shows that the
path-specific concavity condition fails over the full counterfactual domain
\(B\geq B_0\), because its limiting service-capability elasticity is
\(1/\alpha=3.0303>2\). At \(\sigma_{XL}=1.01\), the exact first-failure share
is \(s_X=0.738620\). The terminal share of the saved finite-window segment is
only \(0.230289\), so this is a failure of the global comparison required by
the sufficiency theorem, not a failure of pointwise concavity on the saved
segment. The audit therefore
records \texttt{accepted: false}, writes no plot-ready trajectory, and excludes
\(1.01\) from every reported trajectory figure. The source and audit files
remain in the repository to document the rejected calculation.

\subsection{Acceptance tests, independent audits, and limits of the evidence}

Every reported simulation must pass an equilibrium-admission rule. The
calculation must satisfy the dated technologies, prices, market clearing,
first- and second-order conditions, and four dynamic equations; remain strictly
admissible; be stable across numerical refinements and longer horizons; connect
to a terminal regime derived for the same value of \(\sigma_{XL}\); satisfy the
sufficient optimality restrictions; and imply that both transversality objects
converge to zero along that continuation. Passing only the dated equations is not
enough.

For the two displayed \(\sigma_{XL}=0.75\) paths, the separate nonunit
optimality audit evaluates \eqref{eq:axm-service-capability-elasticity} over
every capability value \(B\geq B_0\) at each saved date. The largest elasticity
is \(0.670940\) when \(\sigma_{HM}=1\) and \(0.670862\) when
\(\sigma_{HM}=2\), both well below two. The corresponding research concavity
margins are \(1-\eta(1+\omega_M)=0.73\) and \(1-2\eta=0.60\). These checks
complete the path-specific sufficient-optimality condition that the earlier
dated-equation audit did not contain.

Three groups pass. The first contains the no-AI and positive-AI adoption
equilibria at \(\sigma_{XL}=1\). The second contains the complementary-input
equilibria at \(\sigma_{XL}=0.75\). The third contains the near-unit equilibria
at \(\sigma_{XL}=0.99\) and \(1\). The human-research transitions and all
gross-substitutes free-boundary paths fail the long-run-continuation requirement and are not
reported. The global audit also fails if the manuscript references any rejected
trajectory figure.

Finite-date values of the two transversality expressions remain truncation
diagnostics. Admission therefore also requires analytically negative growth
rates for the objects in the imposed continuation. Agreement with an imposed terminal
ratio alone is not independent validation; the overidentifying evidence comes
from dated residuals, non-imposed quantities, and stability across horizons.
