\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I extend the Ramsey--Cass--Koopmans model to ask whether AI can generate
unprecedented economic prosperity while labor's income share vanishes. I treat
the labor share as the model counterpart of a concern that workers who depend on
labor income could lose economic power relative to the owners of capital and AI.
The final good combines physical capital, labor, and AI production services. A
monopolistic AI developer supplies AI services and invests in capability using
human and AI research services. Because capability augments compute in both
production and research, better AI raises current output and helps create still
better AI.

The results depend on how easily AI production services can replace labor. When
they are complements, labor remains a bottleneck: output per person and real
wages grow only with exogenous labor productivity, and labor retains a positive
income share even when AI research services receive nearly all research
expenditure. At unit elasticity, positive endogenous balanced growth is possible
only if the feedback remains below an explicit threshold. When AI production
services are gross substitutes for labor and
eventually dominate production, finite-rate balanced growth is impossible. Along
a conditional branch of the necessary conditions, output and the interest rate
become unbounded in finite time, while labor's income share converges to zero even
though the real wage rises.

Near unit elasticity, I show that departures from the unit-elastic path
accumulate on a time scale of order
\(1/|\sigma_{XL}-1|\). The numerical paths can therefore remain moderate for
thousands of model years before growth, returns, and labor shares diverge
sharply. Much of the displaced labor income is absorbed by inference and
research compute rather than becoming developer profit. The gross-substitutes
calculations describe a conditional finite-boundary branch, not an established
infinite-horizon equilibrium or globally optimal path.
\end{abstract}
