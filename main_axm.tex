\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper develops an endogenous-growth model in which current AI capability
turns compute into both production services and automated research services. An
integrated monopolist supplies AI services and invests in better AI using human
researchers and capability-augmented research compute. Final output combines
capital with a CES aggregate of labor and AI services. When labor and AI services
are gross complements, every regular finite-rate balanced-growth path
characterized here has constant output per person and real wages, while labor
retains a positive income share. Audited finite-horizon paths show that capability
can keep growing and automated services can receive nearly all research
expenditure while these limits emerge. At unit elasticity in final production,
positive per-capita growth under diminishing returns to current research inputs
requires the feedback from better AI to output and research to be bounded; that
restriction alone does not guarantee existence. If labor and AI services are
gross substitutes and AI services come to dominate their composite, unbounded AI
improvement rules out finite constant growth rates. On a conditional branch of
the necessary conditions where output becomes unbounded in finite time, labor's
income share vanishes, but the real wage eventually rises when the smaller of the
two substitution elasticities remains below an explicit threshold. Free-boundary
approximations display a strongly back-loaded transition: output-per-capita
growth remains modest for most of the computed interval, while labor's income
share declines gradually at first; near the terminal region, growth rises sharply
and the share collapses. The approximations support this conditional branch
without establishing an infinite-horizon equilibrium, and their model-time
duration is not a calendar forecast. An exact consolidated decomposition separates
gross capital payments, production and research wages, inference and research
compute, and distributed developer profit. In the conditional AI-dominated
benchmark limit, the gross capital payment remains fixed at \(\alpha Y\), while compute
uses absorb most of the noncapital share that no longer accrues to labor. This is
a decomposition of payments and resource uses, not a statement about
interpersonal inequality. A separate finite-horizon result shows why the
baseline imposes \(\eta<\alpha\). On every fixed regular horizon and for all
positive CES elasticities, this condition is sufficient for the developer's
truncated objective to be coercive in total research expenditure over all
nonnegative research policies, and the truncated value is finite. When both CES
nests permit gross substitution, a fixed-duration research-compute expansion
makes that value unbounded when \(\eta>\alpha\); extending the
policy with zero controls also makes the developer's infinite-horizon objective
unbounded under the stated extension conditions. At equality, the fixed-duration
leading term depends on coefficients and the boundary is not fully characterized.
Coercivity does not prove compactness,
attainment of a global optimum, infinite-horizon well-posedness, or equilibrium
existence. The benchmark value \(\eta=0.20\) is illustrative, not estimated.
\end{abstract}
