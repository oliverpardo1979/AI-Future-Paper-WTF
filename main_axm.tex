\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I develop a decentralized endogenous-growth model in which current AI capability
turns compute into services used in production and AI research, so better AI
raises output and helps create better AI. I use the model to ask whether this
feedback leaves human labor as a binding bottleneck or drives its share of output
toward zero. A monopolistic developer supplies AI services and invests in
capability.

I characterize three regimes organized by the labor--AI substitution elasticity in
final production. With gross complementarity, the regular finite-rate balanced-growth
paths characterized here have constant output per person and real wages, and
labor retains a positive income share. Audited paths show that this outcome can
coexist with automated services receiving nearly all research expenditure. At
unit elasticity, positive per-capita growth on a balanced-growth path requires
capability feedback to remain below an explicit threshold, a necessary but not
sufficient condition for existence. With gross substitution and AI dominance,
unbounded capability rules out finite constant growth rates. On a conditional
branch of the necessary conditions, output instead becomes unbounded in finite
model time and labor's income share converges to zero, although the real wage can
continue to rise.

The benchmark finite-boundary approximation is strongly back-loaded:
output-per-capita growth remains below 2 percent for more than nine-tenths of the
estimated pre-singular interval, even as labor's share falls, before growth rises
sharply and the share collapses near takeoff. The calculation supports the
conditional branch but does not establish an infinite-horizon equilibrium, and
model time is not a calendar forecast.

A decline in labor's income share does not mechanically become developer profit.
Gross capital payments, including depreciation, remain \(\alpha Y\); in the
conditional AI-dominated benchmark limit, inference and research compute absorb
most of the noncapital share and the remainder accrues as developer profit.
Compute expenditures are resource uses. I do not model their ultimate recipients,
so the baseline does not determine interpersonal inequality.
\end{abstract}
