\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I extend the Ramsey--Cass--Koopmans model to study how AI development affects
economic growth, wages, interest rates, and factor shares. The final good
is produced using physical capital and a composite of labor and AI production
services. A monopolistic AI developer supplies these services and invests in AI
capability using human researchers and AI research services. Because greater
capability makes compute more productive in both production and research, better
AI raises current output and also helps produce still better AI, creating a
feedback loop.

The results depend on how easily AI production services can replace labor. When
they are complements, labor remains a bottleneck: AI capability generates no
per-capita growth beyond exogenous labor productivity, and labor retains a positive
income share even when AI research services receive nearly all research expenditure.
In the reported case without exogenous productivity growth, output per person and
real wages approach constants. At unit elasticity, positive endogenous
balanced growth is possible only if the feedback remains below an explicit
threshold. When AI production services are gross substitutes for labor and
eventually dominate production, finite-rate balanced growth is impossible. Along
a conditional branch of the necessary conditions, output and the interest rate
become unbounded in finite time, while labor's income share converges to zero even
though the real wage rises.

Near unit elasticity, I show that departures from the unit-elastic path
accumulate on a time scale of order
\(1/|\sigma_{XL}-1|\). The numerical paths can therefore remain moderate for
thousands of model years before growth, returns, and labor shares diverge
sharply. Much of the displaced labor income is absorbed by inference and
research compute rather than becoming developer profit. The gross-substitutes
calculations solve the dated equilibrium conditions along a conditional
finite-boundary branch; they do not establish an infinite-horizon equilibrium
or global optimality.
\end{abstract}
