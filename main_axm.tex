\title{The Future of Growth and Human Labor under Automated AI Research:\\
AI Capability as Research-Augmenting Technology}
\author{Oliver Pardo}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
I develop a decentralized endogenous-growth model in which AI capability makes
compute more productive in production and AI research. A monopolistic developer
supplies AI production services and invests in capability, so better AI raises current output
and helps create still better AI. I ask whether this feedback leaves human labor
as a binding bottleneck.

The answer depends on how easily AI replaces labor in final production. With
complementarity, output per person and real wages approach constants, and labor
retains a positive income share even if AI research services receive nearly all
research expenditure. At unit elasticity, keeping the feedback below an explicit
threshold is necessary, but not sufficient, for positive balanced growth. With
gross substitution and AI dominance, finite-rate balanced growth is impossible.
Along a conditional branch
of the necessary conditions, output becomes unbounded in finite model time and
labor's income share converges to zero, although the real wage can rise. The
benchmark path is sharply back-loaded: per-capita growth remains below 2 percent
for over 90 percent of the estimated pre-singular interval. It illustrates a
mechanism, not an established infinite-horizon equilibrium or calendar forecast.

The distributional shift is not simply from wages to profits. The gross capital
share, including depreciation, remains fixed, while much of the remaining
output pays for inference and research compute. I do not model the upstream
compute sector, so the model does not determine who receives these payments or
how interpersonal inequality changes.
\end{abstract}
